
### Overview
Sleep is a reversible, naturally recurring state of reduced responsiveness to external [[stimulus|stimuli]], characterized by altered [[consciousness]], decreased [[motor function|motor activity]], and changes in [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]] activity and physiology. 
It is distinct from states like coma or anesthesia due to its reversibility and homeostatic regulation. 

### Core Features
- Reduced sensory awareness
- Decreased voluntary muscle activity
- Distinct brain activity patterns (observable via [[EEG]])
- Homeostatic regulation: The longer one is awake, the stronger the "pressure" to sleep
- [[circadian rhythm|Circadian control]]: Sleep timing is also governed by the body's internal clock. 

### Types of Sleep
Sleep has [[stages of sleep|two primary stages]]

###### NREM (Non-Rapid Eye Movement) Sleep
- There are three stages of [[NREM Sleep]]
- Associated with physical Restoration, Memory Consolidation, and Reduced Brain Activity

###### REM (Rapid Eye Movement) Sleep
- Characterized  by vivid [[dream|dreams]], muscle atonia, and brain activity similar to wakefulness
- Plays a key role in [[emotional regulation]] and memory processing

### Neurobiological Functions
- **Synaptic Homeostasis**: Sleep helps downscale synaptic strength built up during wakefulness ([[Tononi & Cirelli's Hypothesis]])
- **Glymphatic Clearance**: Sleep facilitates the removal of waste products from the brain
- **Memory consolidation**: [[hippocampus|Hippocampal]]-[[Cerebral Cortex|Cortical]] dialogue during sleep helps integrate memories
- **Neurotransmitter cycling**: Sleep involves distinct patterns of [[neurotransmitter|neuromodulators]] like [[dopamine]] and [[serotonin]]
