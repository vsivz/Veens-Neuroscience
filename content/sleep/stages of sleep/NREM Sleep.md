
**NREM (Non-Rapid Eye Movement) Sleep** is one of the [[stages of sleep|two major types]] of [[sleep]], the other being [[REM sleep]].

### General Characteristics
- Reduced [[Cerebral Cortex|cortical]] activity compared to wakefulness
- Absence of rapid eye movements
- Slow, synchronous [[EEG]] activity
- Minimal or fragmented [[dream|dreams]] (when present)
- Decreased metabolic rate, temperature and [[Autonomic Nervous System|autonomic]] activity

In general NREM is associated with:
- restorative functions
- memory consolidation - especially declarative ([[episodic memory|episodic]]/factual) memory
- [[glymphatic clearance]] of metabolic waste

The first half of the night is dominated by NREM, especially [[N3 sleep|N3]]
The second half sees more REM, and less N3. 

### Stages
NREM is divided into *three stages*

###### [[N1 Sleep|Stage N1]]: Light Sleep
- Transition from wakefulness to sleep
- EEG: Low-voltage, mixed frequency ([[theta waves|theta]] ~4-7 Hz)
- Slow rolling eye movements
- Reduced muscle tone, but still easily arousable
- Duration: ~5% of total sleep time

###### [[N2 Sleep|Stage N2]]: Intermediate Sleep
- EEG: [[sleep spindles]] and [[k-complexes]]
- No eye movements
- Further drop in body temperature and heart rate
- Represents ~45-55% of total sleep time; Most abundant stage

###### [[N3 sleep|Stage N3]]: Deep Sleep/Slow Wave Sleep
- EEG: Dominated by [[delta waves]]
- Lowest Arousability - Very hard to wake up
- Associated with: 
	- [[synapse|synaptic]] downscaling
	- [[growth hormone]] secretion
	- [[immune system|immune]] function
	- glymphatic clearance
- Typically occurs most in the first third of the night
- Decreases with age
- ~15-25% of total sleep in healthy young adults
