Some priorities, like hunger and thirst, take priority over others, like school

<mark class="hv">Abraham Maslow</mark> described these prioitites as a <mark class="kv">hierarchy of needs</mark>
1. Physiological needs must be met before all else
2. Then we must have personal safety
3. Then we seek to love and be loved
4. Then we seek self-esteem
5. Then we seek <mark class="kv">self-actualization</mark>, to reach ones full potential

Maslow eventually proposed another level of <mark class="kv">self-transcendence</mark>, where we strive for meaning, purpose and communion in a way that is <mark class="vi">transpersonal</mark> -beyond the self

His contemporary, <mark class="hv">Victor Frankl</mark>, a survivor of a Nazi camp, concurred that the search for meaning was an important human motive

The order of Maslow's hierarchy is not universally fixed
ex. people go on hunger strikes

Culture also influences priorities:
In <mark class="lc">[[individualist]]</mark> western cultures, self-esteem is more important than in <mark class="lc">[[collectivist]]</mark> eastern ones

But the very simple idea that some motives are more compelling is useful
