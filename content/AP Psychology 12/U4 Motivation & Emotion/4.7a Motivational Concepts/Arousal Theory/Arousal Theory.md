Some motivated behaviors *increase*, rather than decrease, arousal. 
- well-fed animals will leave shelder to explore and gain info
- curiosity drives monkeys to figure out how to open a lock that opens nothing
- curiosity drives newly mobile infants to investigate every accessible corner of the house

![[sensation seeking theory|clean no-t]]

human motivation seeks an optimal level of arousal

moderate arousal and even <mark class="gc">[[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]</mark> can be motivating - sometimes leading to higher levels of achievement 

<mark class="ii">too much</mark> stimulation or stress motivates us to decrease arousal

Two <mark class="hv">early twentieth-century psychologists</mark> studied the relationship of arousal to performance: <mark class="kv">the Yerkes-Dodson law</mark>

![[Yerkes-Dodson Law|clean no-t]]

For the average person, when taking an exam, it is useful to be moderately aroused: alert but not too nervous
If you are already nervous, it can be better to avoid becoming further aroused with <mark class="gc">[[caffeine]]</mark>

>[!pers] Personal Connection 
> This phenomenon of caffeine decreasing performance by increasing anxiety is different for me because of my ADHD. Caffeine antagonizes adenosine receptors, increasing dopamine and norepinephrine in the brain. In ADHD, low baseline levels of NE mean that the added NE merely bring NE to a more stable, homeostatic, level. It can still go over with too much, but it would take much more than an average person.

But this point can vary
More difficult tasks, for the average person, require lower arousal for the best performance
