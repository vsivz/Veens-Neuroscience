**How does groupthink happen?**
In 1961, US president John F. Kennedy and his advisors decided to invade [[Cuba]], which had recently freed itself from a US-puppet-dictator. 
They did this with 1400 CIA-trained Cuban exiles. 
When the invaders were easily captured and linked to the US government, Kennedy wondered "How could I have been so stupid?"

Social Psychologist <mark class="hv">Irving Janis</mark> studied the decision-making process that lead to the failure. 
The soaring morale of the recently-elected president created <mark class="gc">[[overconfidence]]</mark> 
To preserve the morale, group members supressed their own dissent
Since nobody raised concerns everyone assumed support was unanimous. 
Janis described this thinking as <mark class="kv">groupthink</mark>
 
>[!psych] groupthink
>the mode of thinking that occurs when the desire for harmony in a decision-making group overrides a realistic apraisal of alternatives

Despite dangers of groupthink, two heads are often better than one.
