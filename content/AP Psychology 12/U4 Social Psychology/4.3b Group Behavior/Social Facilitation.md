
<mark class="hv">[[Norman Triplett's Experiment|Triplett's]]</mark> claim of strengthened performance in other's prescence is called <mark class="kv">social facilitation</mark>. 
But studies show a more complex reality:
The prescence of others strengthens the <mark class="ii">most likely</mark> response:
The correct one on an <mark class="ii">easy</mark> task and an incorrect one on a <mark class="ii">difficult</mark> task

When others observe us, we become aroused, and this arousal amplifies our reactions.
This effect contributes to the home advantage, where sports people tend to perform better in their home location

In the <mark class="ii">social context</mark> of *individuals being observed*, other's presence has the <mark class="ii">psychological effect</mark> of *increased arousal*, leading to <mark class="ii">a behavioral effect</mark> of *amplified dominant behavior*
