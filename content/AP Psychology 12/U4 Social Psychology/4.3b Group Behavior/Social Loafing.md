>[!psych] Social Loafing
>the dendency for people in a group to exert less effort when pooling their efforts toward attaining a common goal than when held individually accountable

Studies show that Social Loafing was especially common among men in <mark class="lc">[[individualist]]</mark> cultures

Whe people act as part of a group they may:
- <mark class="ls">feel less accountable</mark> and therefore worry less about what others think
- <mark class="ls">view individual contributions as dispensible</mark>
- <mark class="ls">overestimate their own contribution</mark>, and downplaying the efforts of others
- <mark class="ls">free ride on others effort</mark>: many will consciously choose to slack off, especially when they share equally in the benefits regardless of their own contribution

In the <mark class="ii">social context</mark> of *group projects*, others' prescence may have the <mark class="ii">psychological effect</mark> of *Diminished feelings of responsibility* if not held individually accountable, leading to <mark class="ii">behavioral effects</mark> of *decreased effort*
