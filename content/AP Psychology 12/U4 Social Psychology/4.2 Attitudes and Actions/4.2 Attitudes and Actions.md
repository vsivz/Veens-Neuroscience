**How do our attitudes affect behavior?**
>[!psych] attitudes
>feelings, often influenced by our beliefs, that predispose us to respond in a particular way to objects, people, and events

If we <mark class="ii">believe</mark> that something is threatening us, we may <mark class="ii">feel</mark> fear and anger toward the person and <mark class="ii">act</mark> defensively

there is <mark class="vi">two-way</mark> traffic between our attitudes and actions

***

#### Attitudes Affect Actions
<mark class="ii">Situational factors</mark>, ex. intense social pressures, can override the attitude-behavior connection

Attitudes are especially likely to affect behavior when:
- <mark class="ls">extenal influences</mark> are minimal
- attitude is <mark class="ls">stable</mark>
- attitude is <mark class="ls">specific to the behavior</mark>
- <mark class="ls">easy to recall</mark> attitude

###### Role Playing and Attitudes

When you adopt a new <mark class="kv">role</mark>, you strive to follow social norms

![[role|clean no-t]]

At first behaviors may feel fake, because you are *acting* a role. 
Eventually, you become the character you were playing

Various military groups have gotten people to do things that they would not have normally done as a good human being, for example the 1970 Greek government eased many men into the role of a torturing interrogator, using [[The Foot-in-the-Door Phenomenon]]. 

A study of german military men showed that military training hurt their personalities, making them less agreeable even 5 years later.
