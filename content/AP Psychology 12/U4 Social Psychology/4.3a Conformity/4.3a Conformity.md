>[!psych] conformity
>adjusting our behavior or thinking to coincide with a group standard

>[!experiment] 
><mark class="hv">Solomon Asch</mark> devised a test to study conformity.
>College students were told they were participants in a study of visual perception.
>They arrive in time to take a seat with 5 other people. 
>They are shown a standard line and 3 comparison lines. 1 comparison line is identical to the standard line. 
>They are then asked which line is identical to the standard line. 
>When students answered alone, they were correct <mark class="na">>99%</mark> of the time. 
>But if several others, who were <mark class="gc">[[confederates]]</mark>, answered wrong, then <mark class="na">>$1 \over 3$</mark> of the time, students went along with the group

Later investigations have not found as much conformity as Asch
But they revealed that we are more likely to conform when we:
- are made to feel incompetent or insecure
- are in a group with atleast three other people
- are in a group in which everyone else agrees - a single other person disagreeing drastically improves our odds at disagreeing
- admire the group's status and attractiveness
- have not made a prior commitment to any response
- know that others in the group will observe our behavior
- are from a culture that strongly encourages respect for social standards
