Social psychologist <mark class="hv">Stanley Milgram</mark>, a student of Solomon Asch, created famous and controversial experiments to test obedience

The study claims to study the effect of punishment on learning

Milgram's assistant asks you and another person to draw slips from a hat to see who will be the "teacher", and "learner". However, both slips say "teacher". The other person is a <mark class="gc">[[confederates|confederate]]</mark>

the learner is led to an adjoining room and strapped to the chair. 

Your task is: teach and then test the learner on a list of word pairs. if the learner gets wrong answer, you are to flip a switch to deliver a brief electric shock. 

For the first wrong answer, you give a mild shock (15 V), and you are to give stronger and stronger ones each time. 

If you keep continuing:
- learner will grunt for 3rd, 4th and 5th shocks
- after 8th switch (120 V) learner cries out that the shocks are painful
- after the tenth switch (150 V), learner begins shouting to leave the experiment. The stern experimenter pressures you, making statements such as "You have no other choice, you must go on"
- After 330-volts, learner falls silent
- the experimenter will keep pushing until the final 450-V switch

Previously, Milgram asked non-participants what they would do. 
Most were sure they would stop as soon as the learner indicated pain

But in the experiment, more than <mark class="na">60%</mark> complied fully, right up to the last switch
