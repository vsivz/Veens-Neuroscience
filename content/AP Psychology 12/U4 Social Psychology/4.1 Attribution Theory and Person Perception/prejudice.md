
>[!psych] prejudice
> an unjustifiable and usually negative attitude toward a group and its members.

Prejudice generally involves negative emotions, stereotyped beliefs, and a predisposition to discriminatory action

This group is often people of a particular racial or ethnic group, [[gender]], or [[3.3c Sexual Orientation|sexual orientation]] 

There are three components to prejudice:
- Negative emotions, such as hostility or fear
- [[stereotypes]] - generalized beliefs
- a predisposition to [[AP Psychology 12/U4 Social Psychology/4.1 Attribution Theory and Person Perception/discrimination|discriminate]]

###### Explicit and Implicit Prejudice
Sometimes our processing is explicit, happening within our conscious awareness
Other times, that processing is implicit, happening outside our awareness

Prejudice is no different. Unconscious prejudices can cause discrimination even when people do not intend to

Psychologists study implicit prejudice by:

- <mark class="ls">testing for unconscious group associations</mark>
	tests where people quickly pair a person's image with a trait show many exhibit negative associations
- <mark class="ls">considering unconscious patronizaton</mark>
	When white university women assessed flawed student essays they believed had been written by either a white or black student, they were more likely to be harsher in their criticisms and lower in evaluations when grading the essays of white students
- <mark class="ls">monitoring reflexive bodily responses</mark>
	neuroscientists can detect signals of implicit prejudice in viewer's facial-muscle responses and the activation of the emotion-processing [[amygdala]]
