>[!psych] Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
>a popular integrative [[psychotherapy|therapy]] that combines [[5.5b Cognitive Therapies|cognitive therapy]] (changing self-defeating thinking) with [[5.5b Behavior Therapies|Behavior Therapy]] (changing behavior)

Like other cognitive therapies, CBT seeks to make people aware of their own irrational negative thinking and to replace it with new ways of thinking. And like other behavior therapies, it trains people to *practice* the more positive approach.

[[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]], [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depressive]] and [[5.4b Bipolar Disorders|Bipolar Disorders]] share a common problem of *unhealthy emotional regulation*
An effective CBT program for these emotional disorders trains people both to 
- replace their catastrophic *thinking* with realistic *appraisals*
- as homework, practice *behaviors* that are incompatible with their problem

CBT has been used to treat those with [[5.4a Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders|Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders]]
In one study, people learned to prevent their compulsive behaviors by relabeling their obsessive thoughts
Feeling the urge to wash their hands again, they would tell themselves that they are having a compulsive urge, explaining that the hand washing was the result of abnormal brain activity, which they had previously seen on PET scans.
Instead of giving in, they would spend 15 minutes doing an enjoyable activity, helping to unstick the brain
Three months later, for most, symptoms had diminished and PET scans revealed normalized activity.

Other studies confirm its effectiveness for other disorders, including [[posttraumatic stress disorder|PTSD]] and [[alcohol use disorder|alcohol]] or other [[substance use disorder]]s
