In the 1930s, Portuguese physician Egas Moniz developed the lobotomy

>[!psych] lobotomy
>a [[psychosurgery|psychosurgical]] procedure once used to calm uncontrollably emotional or violent patients. The procedure cut the [[nerves]] connecting the [[frontal lobes]] to the [[Limbic System|emotion-controlling centers of the inner brain]]

He found that cutting these nerves calmed uncontrollably emotional or violent patients

This would later become a crude, but quick and easy, procedure.
Neurosurgeons would:
- shock the patient into a coma
- hammer an icepick-like instrument through the top of each eye socket into the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]]
- wiggle it to sever connections running up to the frontal lobes

Between 1936-1954, 35,000 with [[Psychiatric Disorders]] were lobotomized in just the US

A lobotomy often decreased misery or tension, but the person was permanently lethargic, immature and uncreative

The practice quickly died out following the introduction of calming [[5.5d drug therapies|drug therapies]] in the 1950s
