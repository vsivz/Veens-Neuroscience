>[!psych] electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)
> a [[biomedical therapy]] for severe [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] in which a brief electric current is sent through the brain of an anesthetized person

This is one of the most famous [[neural stimulation]] techniques
The original version, from 1938, involved an awake person strapped to a table and jolted with electricity to the brain. 
Modern ECT is far kinder and gentler, and not "convulsive"
The patient receives a general anesthetic, and a muscle relaxant (to prevent the bodily convulsions)
A psychiatrist then delivers a brief electric pulse, sometimes only to the brain's right side.
This triggers a 30-to 60-second brain seizure
Within 30 minutes, the person awakens, with no memory of the treatment, nor the previous hours.

ECT has been shown to effectively treat severe [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] in "treatment-resistant" patients, who have not responded to [[5.5d drug therapies|drug therapy]]
After three sessions/week, for 2-4 weeks, 70%+ patients of modern ECT improve markedly, without discernible brain damage or increased dementia risk
ECT also reduces [[suicide|suicidal]] thoughts, and has been credited with saving many from suicide

We do not know for sure how ECT works. 
Maybe the brief electric current calms neural centers where overactivity produces depression
Some research indicates that ECT stimulates [[neurogenesis]] and new [[synapse|synaptic]] connections

This isn't perfect however. 
Many ECT-treated patients relapse. Relapses are somewhat fewer for those who also receive [[antidepressants]] or do [[aerobic exercise]]
Most agree that at worst, ECT may be considered a lesser evil
