>[!psych] aversive conditioning
>[[associative learning|associates]] an unpleasant state (such as nausea) with an unwanted behavior (such as drinking [[alcohol]])

It creates a negative (*aversive*) response, to a *harmful* [[stimulus]]

In the short run, it may help change reactions to alcohol
In one study, 685 patients with [[alcohol use disorder]] completed an aversive therapy program
Over the next year, they returned for several booster treatments, pairing alcohol with sickness.
At the end of that year, 63% were not drinking. But after 3 years only 33% were not drinking. 

This is partially because of cognition. People know that outside the therapists office, they can drink without nausea. This ability to [[AP Psychology 12/U3 Development and Learning/3.7 classical conditioning/discrimination|discriminate]] can limit the effects of aversive conditioning.
Thus, therapists often combine aversive conditioning with other treatments.
