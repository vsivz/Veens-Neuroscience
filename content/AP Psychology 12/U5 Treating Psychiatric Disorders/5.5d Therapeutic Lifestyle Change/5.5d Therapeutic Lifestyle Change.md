*Therapeutic Lifestyle Change* is based on the principle that everything psychological is concurrently biological, making them impossible to completely separate

Thus, our *lifestyle* affects our mental health
Our lifestyle includes:
- exercise
- nutrition
- relationships
- recreation
- relaxation

Our [[evolutionary psychology|shared history]] has prepared us to be physically active and socially engaged
Our ancestors hunted, gathered, and built in groups

Modern research shows that outdoor activity in a natural environment reduces [[stress]] and promotes health

###### Application to therapy
Training seminars promote therapeutic lifestyle change
Small groups of people with [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] undergo a 12-week training program with the following goals:
- [[aerobic exercise]], 30 min/day, at least three times weekly (increases fitness and vitality; stimulates [[endorphins]])
- light exposure, 15-30 min each morning with a light box (amplifies arousal, influences [[hormones]])
- Reducing [[rumination]] by identifying and redirecting negative thoughts (enhances positive thinking)
- Adequate [[sleep]], with a goal of 7-8 hours nightly (Good sleep boosts [[immune system]], increases energy, alertness, mood)t
- Social connection, with less alone time, and at least 2 meaningful social engagements weekly (helps satisfy the human need to belong)
- Nutritional supplements, including a daily supplement with omega-3 fatty acids (reduces [[aggression|aggressive behavior]])
An initial small study (n=74) showed 77% who completed experienced relief from depressive symptoms, compared to 19% in a treatment-as-usual control group

###### Biological Basis
These examples all illustrate [[biomedical therapy]], targeting biological systems, but doing so through lifestyle changes
Every thought and feeling depends on the functioning [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]]
Every creative idea, moment of joy or anger, experience of depression emerges from the brain's electrochemical activity, [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmitters]]
Most [[Psychiatric Disorders]] are all biological events
Some even consider [[psychotherapy]] to be a biomedical therapy, because changing our thoughts and behaviors can change the brain
When psychotherapy relieves behaviors associated with [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]] or [[5.4c Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders|schizophrenia]], PET scans reveal a calmer brain

This is just another example of how humans are an integrated [[Biopsychosocial Approach|Biopsychosocial]] system
