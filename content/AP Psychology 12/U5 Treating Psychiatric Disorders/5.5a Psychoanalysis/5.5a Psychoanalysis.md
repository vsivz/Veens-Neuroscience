The first major [[psychotherapy]] was [[Sigmund Freud]]'s psychoanalysis

>[!psych] psychoanalysis
>Freud's therapeutic technique. Freud believed the patient's [[free association]]s, [[resistance|resistances]], dreams and transferences - and the analyst's interpretations of them - released previously repressed feelings, allowing the patient to gain self-insight. 

Few today practice therapy as Freud did, but his work:
- helped form the foundation for treating [[Psychiatric Disorders]]
- continues to influence modern therapists working from the *psychodynamic perspective* 

###### Goals
Freud believed that in therapy people could achieve better living by releasing the energy they had previously devoted to [[Freudian Personality Structure|id-ego-superego conflicts]]. 
He thought there are things we [[repression|repress]], that we do not want to know
Through psychoanalysis, Freud wanted to bring these things into conscious awareness

By helping them reclaim their [[unconscious]] thoughts and feelings, the therapist (analyst) could:
- give insight into the origins of their disorders
- reduce growth-impeding inner conflicts

##### Techniques
Psychoanalytic theory emphasizes the power of *childhood experiences* to mold the adult
It must reveal the past in order to free the present. To do this, Freud turned to [[free association]]

![[free association|clean no-t]]

Sometimes, one pauses for a second before saying something shameful, the mind goes blank, or one clutches up, unable to remember important details. 
Freud thought that these mental blocks indicate [[resistance]], indicating that [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]] lurks and that there is sensitive material
The analyst will note this resistance and then provide an [[interpretation]] into its meaning.

![[resistance|clean no-t]]
![[interpretation|clean no-t]]

One may develop strong positive or negative feelings for the analyst. They may suggest [[transference]] of feelings that were experienced in earlier relationships with family members or other important people. 

![[transference|clean no-t]]

Now, few offer traditional psychoanalysis. 
Most of the underlying theory is not supported scientifically.
Analysts interpretations cannot be supported or refuted.
These problems have been addressed in the modern [[psychodynamic theories]]
