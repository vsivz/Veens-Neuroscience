###### Flaws of traditional psychiatric viewpoints
Despite many strengths, there is a significant flaw in [[psychotherapy]] and [[biomedical therapy]]:
These locate the cause of [[psychological disorder]]s *within the person*
This viewpoint attaches labels to people, distinguishing "normal" from "abnormal"
From here, one can easily continue that we must "fix" those who are "abnormal", whether through therapeutic methods, or [[5.5d drug therapies|psychotropic drug]]s

However, there is another important viewpoint. 
We could interpret many psychological disorders as *understandable responses to a disturbing world*
Following this concept would show that it is not a problem within the person, but rather *within society/the world*

The soaring mental health problems during the Covid pandemic illustrated the importance of the person's social context
Rates of [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]], [[suicide]], and [[addiction]] have worsened, despite the help of psychotherapy and biomedical therapy
While treatments for pathogenic disease, like antibiotics and vaccines, have clearly added years to the lifespan, mental health services have not seen any comparable added joy to the average life. 

###### Preventive Mental Health
Unhealthy situations cause unhappy people
$\therefore$ it is better to *prevent* problems by reforming an unhealthy situation and developing coping competencies, than to wait for and then treat problems

*Preventive mental health* is upstream work, seeking to prevent psychological casualties by identifying and alleviating the conditions that *cause* them

There is very clear evidence showing that:
- poverty
- lack of meaningful work
- constant criticism
- unemployment
- [[AP Psychology 12/U4 Social Psychology/4.1 Attribution Theory and Person Perception/discrimination|discrimination]]
All can undermine people's:
- sense of competence
- [[personal control]]
- [[self-esteem]]

Such [[stress|stresses]] increase their risk of depression, [[alcohol use disorder]], and suicide

Preventing psychological problems means:
- empowering those who have developed an attitude of [[learned helplessness]]
- changing environments that breed loneliness, suicidal thinking, and excessive [[alcohol]] and [[1.3b Drugs!|drug]] use

For example, teaching children to:
- manage emotions
- get along with others
- keep up with academic demands

It can use [[positive psychology]] interventions to enhance human flourishing

>[!experiment]
>One intervention taught [[Adolescence|adolescents]] that [[personality]] isn't fixed - that people can change - and reduced their chances of depression by 40%

Prevention can provide a double payoff - people who have a better sense of life's meaning are more social, so by strengthening people's sense of meaning in life, their loneliness can also be lessened

*Community psychologists* are among the upstream preventative workers, as they focus on creating environments that support psychological health

###### Resilience
Preventive mental health includes efforts to build [[AP Psychology 12/U5 Positive Psychology/5.2b Enhancing Well-Being/resilience]]
Faced with extreme suffering or [[5.4a Trauma-and Stressor-Related Disorders|trauma]], people may:
- experience lasting harm, possibly including [[posttraumatic stress disorder]]
- experience resilience
- experience growth

For example, in the aftermath of 9/11, many exhibited resilience, especially those who had supportive close relationships, and who had not recently experienced other stressful events

Struggling with crises can lead to [[posttraumatic growth]]

![[posttraumatic growth|clean no-t]]

Many survivors of cancer reported:
- greater appreciation of life
- more meaningful relationships
- increased personal strength
- changed priorities
- (beneficiality is questionable) richer spiritual life
