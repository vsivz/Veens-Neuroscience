
>[!psych] person-centered therapy
>a [[5.5a Humanistic Therapies|Humanistic Therapy]], developed by Carl Rogers. in which the therapist uses techniques such as [[active listening]] within an accepting, genuine, empathetic environment to facilitate clients' growth

Also called *client-centered therapy*

This is a *nondirective [[psychotherapy|therapy]]*, where the client leads the discussion

The therapist listens, without judging or interpreting, and refrains from directing the client toward certain insights

Rogers encouraged therapists to foster growth by exhibiting:
- **acceptance**, helping clients feel freer and open to change
- **genuineness**, hope to encourage clients to express their true feelings
- **empathy**, try to sense, validate and reflect their clients' feelings, helping them experience deeper self-understanding and self-acceptance

![[active listening|clean no-t]]

Noone can be *totally* non directive. 
The therapist's most important contribution is to accept and understand the client. 
Given a nonjudgmental environment that provides [[unconditional positive regard]], people may accept even their worst traits, and feel valued and whole

![[unconditional positive regard|clean no-t]]

To improve communication in our own relationships by listening more actively, we can:
- **Paraphrase**, check understanding by summarizing the person's words out loud
- **Invite Clarification**, "what might be an example of that?"
- **Reflect Feelings**, "it sounds frustrating"
