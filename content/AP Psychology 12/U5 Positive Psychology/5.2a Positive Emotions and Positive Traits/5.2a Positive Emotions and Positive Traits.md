There are <mark class="na">3 pillars</mark> of positive psychology:
- <mark class="ls">positive well-being</mark>: 
	- satisfaction with the past
	- happiness with the present
	- optimism about the future
- <mark class="ls">positive traits</mark> focuses on exploring and enhancing:
	- creativity
	- courage
	- compassion
	- integrity
	- self-control
	- leadership
	- wisdom
	- spirituality
- <mark class="ls">positive groups, communities, and cultures</mark> - fostering positive social ecology
	- healthy families
	- friendly neighborhoods
	- effective schools
	- socially responsible media
	- civil dialogue

The reverse is also true - doing good promotes good feeling

Stressful events, ex. arguments, team losses, poor grades, can trigger a bad mood. But these moods typically gone by the next day. 
Worse events, like the end of a friendship, can hurt mood for longer periods, but most bad moods end eventually. 
Grief over the loss of a loved one or anxiety after severe trauma can linger. But even tragedy is not permanent in its effects

The Values in Action classification is a system that organizes 24 character strengths into six broad virtue categories

- <mark class="ls">wisdom</mark>: creativity, curiosity, judgment, love of learning, perspective
- <mark class="ls">courage</mark>: bravery, honesty, perseverance, and zest
- <mark class="ls">humanity</mark>:kindness, love, and social intelligence
- <mark class="ls">justice</mark>: fairness, leadership, and teamwork
- <mark class="ls">temperance</mark>:forgiveness, humility, prudence, and self-regulation
- <mark class="ls">transcendence</mark>:appreciation of beauty and excellence, gratitude, hope, humor, and spirituality
