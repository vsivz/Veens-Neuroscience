**1) Anorexia vs. Bulimia**
When differentiating between Anorexia Nervosa and Bulimia Nervosa, one must look at the individuals weight. While Bulimia has weight fluctuations at or above the minimal range, Anorexia has weight that is significantly below the expected weight (DSM-5, Textbook). While Bulimia requires binge-eating/purging, Anorexia can also have this, (textbook) specifically in the Binge-Eating/Purging subtype (DSM-5).
The slides and study guide imply that the primary differentiating factor between the two is the Binge-Eating/Purging behavior, which they only mention for Bulimia.

**2) Bipolar Disorders**
Bipolar I disorder only requires a single manic episode. (Although hypomanic and major depressive episodes can, and often do, occur) (DSM-5, vaguely said in textbook). Also the DSM directly states "Despite the substantial differences in duration and severity between a manic and hypomanic episode, bipolar II disorder is not a “milder form” of bipolar I disorder", and goes on to explain how those with Bipolar II typically have greater chronicity, and much more severe and prolonged depressive episodes. 
The study guide includes major depressive episodes in the criteria for Bipolar I. The slides also state that Bipolar I is a more severe illness than Bipolar II due to Mania.  

**3) Schizophrenia**
Schizophrenia requires a month where symptoms are present most of the time, and a 6-month period where signs of the disturbance persist, which can include less-impairing positive symptoms, and negative symptoms (DSM). 
The slides state that it requires a 6-month period of symptoms. In addition, the study guide defines Catatonia with the definition of Catatonic Stupor, ignoring the positive symptom of Catatonic Excitement. 

**4) Personality Disorders**
With the exception of ASPD, all other personality disorders may be diagnosed in individuals under 18, although more caution may be taken when diagnosing younger individuals. 
The slides state that Personality Disorders aren't diagnosed until age 18.

**5) DBT**
Dialectical Behavior Therapy's defining characteristic is how it uses the idea of a Dialectic, a balance between opposites. It emphasizes a balance between both self-acceptance and change. (Textbook)
The slides focus on the specific techniques used within DBT and the study guide just mentions that it is used to treat BPD. 

**6) antianxiety drugs**
The slides define antianxiety drugs as drugs that affect neurotransmitters promote relaxation and reducing excessive nervousness/worry, and says that BZDs and Azapirones are the different types. However nearly every single class of antidepressant drug (SSRIs, SNRIs, SMSs, etc.) are also antianxiety drugs, as both depression and anxiety are strongly associated with similar neurotransmitter deficiencies. 
The slides do say say how BZDs are almost never prescribed except for very acute cases, but Azapirones like Buspirone are rarely a first-line treatment either, even in GAD. SSRIs and SNRIs are instead the first antianxiety medication used, as they typically have more efficacy than Azapirones, which may be used later as an adjunct therapy, or after failure of other antianxiety medications.
