People with *specific learning disorders* experience chronic difficulties perceiving and processing information, making it difficult for them to transition from beginner to adult. 

People with SLDs are typically of average or above-average intelligence.

These disorders typically begin in early childhood, leading children to underperform academically.

Children with SLDs may experience difficulties:
- reading
- spelling
- writing
- understanding numbers and mathematical reasoning

Their learning difficulties are *persistent*, lasting at least six months, and not due to other mental or neurological disorders.

Children with SLDs may do well academically until they face challenging situations, ex. timed tests, completing complex written assignments on a deadline, or juggling a demanding academic workload

Dyslexia, Dyscalculia, and Dysgraphia are common names for certain kinds of SLDs
