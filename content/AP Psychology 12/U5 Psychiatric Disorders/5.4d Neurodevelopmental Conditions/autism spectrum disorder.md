*Autism Spectrum Disorder* is a cognitive and social-emotional [[psychological disorder|condition]]

Characteristics:
- *social deficiencies*
- *repetitive behaviors*

It was once believed to affect 1 in 2500 children. 
ASD is now diagnosed in 1 in 38, 1 in 54, 1 in 62 & 1 in 166 children in South Korea, the US, Canada and Germany respectively

The increases in ASD diagnoses have been offset by a decrease in the number of children with a "cognitive disability" or "learning disability", which suggests a relabeling of neurodevelopmental differences in children


The underlying source of ASD's symptoms seems to be poor communication between [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain regions]] that normally coordinate to allow taking another's viewpoint.
Several studies have revealed underconnectivity - fewer-than-normal fiber tracts connecting the front of the brain to the back.
With underconnectivity, there is less of the whole-brain synchrony that integrates visual and emotional information.
In children as young as three, EEG-recorded activity can fortell ASD. 

People without ASD, for example, yawn after seeing others yawn. As they view and imitate another person's emotions they feel something of what the other person is feeling. Scientists are exploring whether treatment with [[oxytocin]], the hormone that promotes social bonding, may improve social skills in those with ASD.


From age two-months on, children typically spend more and more time looking into others' eyes, but those who develop ASD do so less and less. 

Researchers debate whether those with ASD have impaired [[Theory of Mind]]
Individuals with ASD have difficulty inferring how others think differently than they do.


Partly because of this, 46% of adolescents with ASD had been bullied, compared to 11% for other children.
This helps explain why people with ASD have a quadrupled risk of experiencing [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] in their lifetime


ASD has differing levels of severity. 
Some people, those who would have previously been diagnosed with Asperger's Syndrome which has since been included as part of ASD, generally function at a high level. 
They have normal intelligence, often accompanied by exceptional skill or talent in a specific area. 
They may lack the motivation and ability to interact and communicate socially, and they tend to become distracted by irrelevant stimuli. 

Those at the spectrum's more severe end struggle to use language. 


ASD gets diagnosed four times more often in [[Male-Female Differences|boys than girls]] 
Some believe that this is because boys, more often than girls are "systemizers" who understand things according to rules or laws, as in mathematical and mechanical systems. 
This hypothesis asserts that girls are more often "empathizers", who tend to excel at reading facial expressions, predicting what others will feel, and knowing what to do socially.

People with ASD are "systemizers" who have more difficulty reading facial expressions, intuitively knowing what others feel, and understanding how to have smooth social interactions.
People working in STEM careers are more likely than others to exhibit some ASD-like traits. 

[[Prenatal Development|Prenatal]] environment can be a factor in developing ASD, especially when it is altered by maternal infection, psychiatric [[1.3b Drugs!|drug]] use, or stress [[hormones]].
[[genes]] also matter. The heritability of ASD is near 80%. 
If one identical twin has ASD there is a 90% chance the other will too, though the twins often differ in symptom severity.
Random genetic mutations in sperm cells may also play a role. As men age, these mutations become more frequent, so older men have higher chances of fathering a child with ASD.
