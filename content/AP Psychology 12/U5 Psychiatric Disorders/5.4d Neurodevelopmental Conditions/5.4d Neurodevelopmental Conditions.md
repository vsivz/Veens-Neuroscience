>[!dsm] Info from the DSM-5-TR
>Differentiating Stereotypies from tics
>Typically, stereotypies onset before age 3, tics  4-6 years
>Stereotypies are consistent and fixed in their patterns, tics are variable and changing
>Stereotypies may involve arms, hands or the entire body, tics commonly involve eyes, face, head and shoulders.
>Stereotypes are more fixed, rhythmic and prolonged in duration than tics, which are generally brief, rapid, random and fluctuating
>Stereotypies may be enjoyed by children, not tics

>[!dsm] Info from the DSM-5-TR
>tourettes requires multiple motor and one or more vocal tics
>Persistent motor or vocal tic disorder requires one or multiple motor or vocal tics, but not both
>Provisional tic disorder is for the first year of tics, after they have persisted longer, other disorders can be made

###### Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

>[!psych] attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder ([[ADHD]])
>a psychological condition marked by extreme inattention and/or hyperactivity and impulsivity

Stimulant drugs calm hyperactivity and increase the ability to increase and focus. Behavior therapy and aerobic exercise may also help.

This is a highly genetic condition. 

Some may think that ADHD is overdiagnosed. They claim that children are not meant to sit for hours in chairs. 
They are wrong.
ADHD is a condition with a very strong neurobiological basis. It's deficiencies have treatments that have been shown to have very high efficacy. 
ADHD assessments are very carefully designed to measure behaviors that have a very strong positive correlation with said neurobiological basis. Even if a teacher were to make a recommendation just because a child is hyper, the people who do the assessments are highly trained in differentiating high-energy children with ADHD.
Especially in minority groups, and for women, it is likely underdiagnosed.
