>[!psych] anxiety disorders
>a group of [[psychological disorder|disorders]] characterized by excessive fear and anxiety and related maladaptive behaviors


For example, people with [[social anxiety disorder]] may become extremely anxious in social settings where others might judge them, such as:
- parties
- class presentations
- eating in public

One may experience severe anxious feelings from these situations, and stay home to avoid the anxious feelings. But that was a maladaptive behavior, preventing them from learning to cope, and leaving them feeling lonely.

#### Conditioning
Through [[3.7 classical conditioning|classical conditioning]], fear responses can become linked with formerly neutral objects and events. 
<mark class="na">58%</mark> of people with social anxiety disorder said that their disorder began after a [[5.4a Trauma-and Stressor-Related Disorders|traumatic event]]

Anxiety or an anxiety-related disorder is more likely to develop when bad events happen unpredictably and uncontrollably. 

A single painful and frightening event may trigger a full phobia, due to [[stimulus generalization]] and [[reinforcement]]

- Stimulus generalization: a person experiences a fear-provoking event and later develops fear of similar events.
- Reinforcement: Anything that enables avoiding or escaping a feared situation can reinforce [[maladaptive behavior]]. Fearing a panic attack, one may decide not to leave the house. Reinforced by feeling calmer, we are likely to repeat that behavior. 

**Cognition:**
We may also learn fears by observing others.

#### Biology
If one identical twin has an anxiety disorder, the other is also at risk
When raised separately, identical twins may develop similar specific phobias.

Some [[genes]] influence anxiety disorders by regulating brain levels of [[neurotransmitter]]s:
- [[serotonin]], which influences sleep, mood, and attending to threats
- [[glutamate]], which heightens activity in the brain's alarm centers

Anxiety disorders, and Trauma-related disorders, reflect the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]]'s danger-detection system gone hyperactive. 
The [[amygdala]], in particular, is a region where hyperactivity may indicate an anxiety disorder.
