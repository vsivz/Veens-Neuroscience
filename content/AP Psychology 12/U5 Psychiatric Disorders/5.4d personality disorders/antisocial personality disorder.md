>[!psych] antisocial personality disorder
>a [[Cluster B Personality Disorders|Cluster B]] personality [[psychological disorder|disorder]] in which a person (usually [[Male-Female Differences|male]]), exhibits a lack of conscience for wrongdoing, even toward friends and family members; may be aggressive and ruthless or a clever con artist


People with ASPD, usually can display symptoms by age 8
Their lack of conscience becomes plain before age 15 as they lie, steal, fight, or display unrestrained sexual behavior, although this is little evidence.

Not all children with these traits become antisocial adults, and for many males, antisocial behavior often subsides after [[Adolescence]]

Criminality is not a essential component of antisocial behavior; many criminals do not exhibit ASPD

People with ASPD are more *socially deficient*, exhibiting less emotional intelligence - the ability to understand, manage and perceive emotions

Those with ASPD are *impulsive*, and then feel and fear little

This can have horrible consequences, including murder

***
Preschool boys who later became aggressive or antisocial adolescents tend to be impulsive, uninhibited, unconcerned with social rewards, and low in [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]].

Those who were slow to develop conditioned fears at age 3 were also more likely to commit a crime later in life

Twin and adoption studies reveal that biological relatives of people with antisocial and unemotional tendencies are at increased risk for antisocial behavior.
No single [[genes|gene]] codes for complex behaviors such as crime, but genes predisposing lower mental ability and self-control predict a higher crime risk.

The genes that put people at risk for antisocial behavior also increase the risk of [[substance use disorder]]

Genetic influences, often in combination with negative environmental factors like child abuse, family instability, or poverty, can wire the brain to develop like this

In people with antisocial criminal tendencies, the [[amygdala]] is smaller
The genetic vulnerability of people with antisocial tendencies appears as low arousal in response to threats. Awaiting events that most would find scary, ex. electrical shocks, they show little [[Autonomic Nervous System]] arousal

Long-term studies show that their stress [[hormones|hormone]] levels were lower than average as teens, before they had ever committed a crime. 

Shown emotionally evocative photographs, ex. a man holding a knife to a woman's throat, criminals with ASPD display less heart rate, perspiration, and activity in [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]] regions responding to emotional stimuli. 

They also have a larger and hyperreactive [[dopamine]] system, which predisposes their impulsive drive to do something rewarding despite consequences

Murderers, especially those who murdered impulsively, had less [[frontal lobes|frontal lobe]] activity, and violent repeat offenders hat less frontal lobe tissue.
