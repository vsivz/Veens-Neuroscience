### Differential Diagnoses: Anxiety Disorders

**Specific Phobia** - **Agoraphobia**: If an individual fears only one of the feared situations, then specific phobia, situational may be diagnosed. If two or more agoraphobic situations are feared, its typically Agoraphobia. Furthermore, if the fear of the situation is because of reasons other than panic-like symptoms or incapacitation, (ex. fearing a plane because of a fear that it will crash) then diagnosis will be specific phobia.

**Specific Phobia** - **Panic Disorder**: Those with a Specific Phobia may also experience panic attacks from their feared situations. If the panic attacks occur just in response to the specific object or situation, then a diagnosis of Specific Phobia would be given. If the individual also experiences panic attacks that were unexpected, without the specific object or situation, then a diagnosis of Panic Disorder may be given. 

**Specific Phobia** - **Obsessive-compulsive disorder**: If the primary fear/anxiety is as a result of obsessions (ex. fear of blood due to obsessive thoughts about contamination from blood-borne pathogens, fear of driving due to obsessive images of harming others), and other criteria for OCD are met, then OCD should be diagnosed. 

**Social Anxiety Disorder** - **Agoraphobia**: Individuals with agoraphobia may fear or avoid social situations because escape may be difficult, or help may not be available in the event of incapacitation/panic attack, whereas those with SAD are fearful of these because of scrutiny of others. Those with SAD will typically be calm when left entirely alone in these situations, unlike in Agoraphobia.

**Social Anxiety Disorder** - **Panic Disorder**: Those with SAD may have panic attacks, but these panic attacks are always caused by social situations. Additionally, those with SAD are more likely to be distressed by fear of negative evaluation from the panic attack, than the panic attack itself. 

**Social Anxiety Disorder** - **Specific Phobia**: Individuals with specific phobias may fear embarrassment or humiliation (e.g., embarrassment about fainting when they have their blood drawn), but they do not generally fear negative evaluation in other social situations.
##### Social Anxiety Disorder - Avoidant Personality Disorder:
*Scope*
With SAD, anxiety is typically situational and tied to specific social or performance scenarios (ex. public speaking, meeting new people). The individual may actively want to engage, but feels paralyzed by fear.
With AvPD, there is a pervasive pattern of avoidance, and affects most aspects of life. It stems from a deep-seated belief in their own unworthiness. Then avoidance is motivated by a sense of inadequacy, and a chronic fear of rejection.
*Sense of Self*
With SAD, low self-esteem can be present, but is often tied to specific social scenarios (ex. "I'm bad at speaking in meetings")
With AvPD, there is a core, pervasive sense of inadequacy and inferiority across all domains of life
*Severity and Chronicity*
With SAD, symptoms may change over time, and is less severe, and easier to improve
With AvPD, symptoms are more ingrained, chronic, and enduring, often beginning in early adulthood or adolescence
*Interpersonal Relationships*
With SAD, they may still have relationships, but avoid specific interactions they find anxiety-provoking (ex. meeting new people)
With AvPD, there may be avoidance of close relationships altogether unless they are certain they will be accepted without judgement
However, SAD is typically more comorbid with AvPD than any other Personality Disorder, and AvPD is more comorbid with SAD than any other anxiety disorder.

**Generalized Anxiety Disorder** - **Panic Disorder**: Panic attacks triggered by worry in GAD do not qualify for panic disorder. However, panic disorder may be considered if they experience unexpected panic attacks as well. and show persistent concern or worry because of the attacks.

### Differential Diagnoses: OCD+

**Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder** - **Generalized Anxiety Disorder**: Recurrent thoughts in GAD are usually about real-life concerns; obsessions of OCD usually do not involve real-life concerns and can include content that is odd, irrational, or of a seemingly magical nature

**Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder** - **Body dysmorphic disorder**: In BDD, the obsessions and compulsions are limited to concerns about physical appearance

**Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder** - **Hoarding Disorder**: Hoarding disorder symptoms focus exclusively on the difficulty discarding or parting with possessions & excessive accumulation. However, if an individual has OCD-like obsessions (ex. concerns about incompleteness or harm), and these obsessions lead to compulsive accumulation (ex. acquiring all objects in a set for a sense of completeness, not discarding old newspapers because they may contain information that could prevent harm), then they would have OCD, not hoarding disorder. 

**Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder** - **Psychotic Disorders**: Some with OCD have poor insight or even delusional beliefs. However, they have obsessions and compulsions (distinguishing their condition from delusional disorder) and do not have other features of Schizophrenia or Schizoaffective Disorder, (ex. hallucinations, disorganized speech)

##### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder - Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder
Despite similar names, these are very different.
OCPD is not characterized by intrusive thoughts, images, or urges, or by repetitive behaviors that are performed in response to these intrusive symptoms;
OCPD is instead an enduring and pervasive maladaptive pattern of excessive perfectionism and rigid control
If an individual has symptoms of both OCD and OCPD, both may be diagnosed.

### Differential Diagnoses: Mood Disorders

**Major Depressive Disorder** - **Persistent Depressive Disorder**: If criteria are met for both MDD and PDD, both can be diagnosed. The comorbid presence of major depressive episodes should be noted using the appropriate specifier on Persistent Depressive Disorder.

(Not on Test) - **Major Depressive Disorder, with psychotic features** - **Schizoaffective Disorder**: In Schizoaffective disorder, delusions or hallucinations are present for at least 2 weeks in the absence of a major depressive episode.

**Major Depressive Disorder** - **Major Depressive Episodes superimposed on a Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorder (except Schizoaffective Disorder)**: Depressive symptoms may be present during Schizophrenia, Delusional Disorder, Schizophreniform Disorder or other specified or unspecified Schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorder. These depressive symptoms can be considered associated features, and do not merit a separate diagnosis. However, when the specified depressive symptoms meet the full criteria for a major depressive episode, a diagnosis of Other Specified Depressive Disorder may be given in addition to the psychotic disorder. 

**Persistent Depressive Disorder** - **Psychotic Disorder**: Depressive symptoms may occur in psychotic disorders. A separate diagnosis of PDD is not made if the symptoms only occur during the course of the psychotic disorder (including residual phase)

##### Mania - Hypomania
An episode is classified as Mania if it meets at least one of the following criteria, in addition to the symptoms of Mania/Hypomania:
- Duration of 7 days
- Hospitalization
- Psychosis
- Severe enough to cause marked impairment in social or occupational functioning

If it has a duration of 4-6 days, and it does not cause marked impairment, necessitate hospitalization or contain psychosis, it is a Hypomanic Episode. 

**Bipolar I Disorder** - **Borderline Personality Disorder**: Personality disorders like BPD may have substantial symptomatic overlap with Bipolar I, since mood swings and impulsivity are common. To differentiate, Bipolar I requires these symptoms to represent a distinct episode of illness, or there must be a noticeable increase in these symptoms over the individual's baseline. 

**Bipolar I disorder, with psychotic features** - **Schizoaffective Disorder, Bipolar Type**: For Schizoaffective disorder, delusions or hallucinations must occur for at-least 2 weeks in the absence of a manic or major depressive episode. If psychotic symptoms occurred exclusively during manic and major depressive episodes, the diagnosis is Bipolar I disorder, with psychotic features

### Differential Diagnoses: Neurodevelopmental, Eating Disorders
##### Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder - Autism Spectrum Disorder
Symptoms of both of these may resemble each other. However, these conditions are also frequently comorbid. 
ADHD alone can be diagnosed by the absence of restricted, repetitive behaviors and unusual, intense, interests in ADHD.
ADHD alone may see social dysfunction and peer rejection, compared to social disengagement, isolation, and indifference to facial and tonal communication cues seen in ASD alone. 
Children with ASD may display tantrums because of an inability to tolerate change, while children with ADHD may display tantrums during a transition because of impulsivity and poor self-control.

**Anorexia Nervosa** - **Major Depressive Disorder**: In MDD, there may be significant weight loss and low appetite, but those with MDD do not have either a desire for excessive weight loss, or an intense fear of gaining weight, and this weight loss typically results from little pleasure from food, and low motivation to eat.
##### Anorexia Nervosa, Binge-eating/purging type - Bulimia Nervosa
Anorexia may either be a Binge-eating/purging type, or a restricting type. 
To differentiate the Binge-eating/purging from Bulimia, which requires binge-eating and purging behavior, Anorexia is marked by weight that is significantly below the minimally normal range, while Bulimia has weight fluctuations at or above a minimally normal range.
For individuals initially diagnosed with Anorexia who binge and Purge, a diagnosis of Bulimia should only be given when all criteria for Bulimia have been met for three months.

**Bulimia Nervosa** - **Binge-Eating Disorder**: Binge-eating disorder includes the periods of binge-eating, but does not have the regular inappropriate compensatory behaviors, like throwing up

**Bulimia Nervosa** - **Major Depressive Disorder, with atypical features**: Atypical MDD frequently sees overeating, but individuals with this disorder do not engage in inappropriate compensatory behaviors. If criteria for both are met, both diagnoses should be given.

### Differential Diagnoses: Dissociative Disorders

##### Dissociative Identity Disorder - Dissociative Amnesia
Both DID and Dissociative Amnesia include gaps in the recall of everyday events, important personal info, and traumatic events that are inconsistent with ordinary forgetting. DID is distinguished from Dissociative Amnesia by the additional presence of two or more distinct personality states. 

##### Dissociative Identity Disorder - Schizophrenia & Other Psychotic Disorders: 
DID can include psychotic symptoms
In DID, auditory hallucinations, thought insertions and thought withdrawal are often related to the intrusions of different personality states. In Schizophrenia, these hallucinations are typically independent voices, and disorganized thoughts are accompanied by delusions.
In DID, there are a wide range of hallucinations, like visual, somatic, etc. and these are often related to trauma
In Schizophrenia, hallucinations are primarily auditory, with visual hallucinations being much rarer

**Dissociative Amnesia** - **Posttraumatic Stress Disorder**: Some with PTSD cannot recall part or all of a specific traumatic event. When that amnesia extends to events beyond the immediate time of the trauma, a comorbid diagnosis of dissociative amnesia may be made.

### Differential Diagnoses: Cluster A Personality Disorders

**Paranoid Personality Disorder** - **Psychotic Disorder with delusions of persecution**: If other psychotic features are present (ex. hallucinations, other delusions) then the relevant psychotic disorder is diagnosed. For an additional comorbid diagnosis of PPD, the personality disorder must have been present before the onset of psychotic symptoms, and must persist when the psychotic symptoms are in remission

**Schizoid Personality Disorder** - **Psychotic Disorder**: In the event of psychotic symptoms, the diagnosis of Schizoid PD can be made if the symptoms do not exclusively occur during the course of a psychotic episode

**Schizoid Personality Disorder** - **Autism Spectrum Disorder**: Especially in milder forms of either condition, they may be harder to differentiate. A key factor is that ASD can often be differentiated by stereotyped behaviors and interests. Furthermore, the social withdrawal is not an essential feature of ASD, which can just see other social difficulties

**Schizoid Personality Disorder** - **Schizotypal Personality Disorder**: StPD has the cognitive and perceptual distortions that Schizoid PD lacks

**Schizoid Personality Disorder** - **Paranoid Personality Disorder**: PPD is marked by suspiciousness and paranoid ideation, which can fuel isolation, while in Schizoid PD, isolation is fueled by indifference. 

**Schizotypal Personality Disorder** - **Psychotic Disorder**: In the event of severe Psychotic symptoms, an additional diagnosis of StPD may be made if the Personality Disorder was present before the onset of psychotic symptoms, and persist when the psychotic symptoms are in remission.

**Schizotypal Personality Disorder** - **Avoidant Personality Disorder**: While both StPD and AvPD see limited close relationships, in AvPD there is an active desire for relationships constrained by a fear of rejection, whereas in StPD, there is a lack of desire for relationships and persistent detachment

### Differential Diagnoses: Cluster B Personality Disorders

**Antisocial Personality Disorder** - **Narcissistic Personality Disorder**: Both share a tendency to be tough-minded, superficial, exploitative and lack empathy. However, NPD does not include the impulsivity, aggression and deceit of ASPD, and those with ASPD are less needy of the admiration and envy of others, compared to those with NPD. Those with ASPD also have a history of adolescent conduct disorder, and adult criminal behavior, unlike in NPD.

**Antisocial Personality Disorder** - **Histrionic Personality Disorder**: ASPD and HPD share a tendency to be impulsive, superficial, excitement-seeking, reckless, seductive, and manipulative, but those with HPD tend to be more exaggerated in their emotions, and do not characteristically engage in antisocial behavior.  While ASPD may see manipulation for profit, power or other material gratification, those with HPD may be manipulative for nurturance in their relationships.

**Antisocial Personality Disorder** - **Borderline Personality Disorder**: 
Like HPD, BPD may see manipulation for nurturance, while those with ASPD may be manipulative for profit or gain. Those with ASPD tend to be less aggressive and emotionally unstable than those with BPD.

**Borderline Personality Disorder** - **Separation Anxiety Disorder (in adults)**: while both Separation Anxiety Disorder and BPD are characterized by fear of abandonment by loved ones, BPD also involves problems in identity, self-direction, interpersonal functioning, and impulsivity. 

**Borderline Personality Disorder** - **Histrionic Personality Disorder**: both involve attention seeking, manipulative behavior and rapidly shifting emotions, BPD is distinguished by self-destructiveness, angry disruptions in close relationships, and chronic feelings of deep emptiness and loneliness. 

**Borderline Personality Disorder** - **Dependent Personality Disorder**: both involve fear of abandonment. But one with BPD would react to abandonment with feelings of emotional emptiness, rage and demands, while one with DPD would react with increasing appeasement and submissiveness, and urgently seeks a replacement relationship to provide caregiving and support. BPD can further be distinguished from DPD by the typical pattern of unstable relationships. 

**Histrionic Personality Disorder** - **Narcissistic Personality Disorder**:  both crave attention from others, but NPD typically want praise for their "superiority", while those with HPD are willing to be viewed as fragile or dependent to get attention. 

**Narcissistic Personality Disorder** - **Mania/Hypomania**: Grandiosity may emerge as part of manic or hypomanic episodes, but the association with mood change and functional impairment can be used to distinguish from NPD
