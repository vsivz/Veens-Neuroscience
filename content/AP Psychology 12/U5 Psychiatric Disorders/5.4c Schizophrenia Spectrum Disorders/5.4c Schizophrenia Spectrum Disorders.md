People with a Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorder live in a private inner world, preoccupied with strange ideas and images

The word schizophrenia means "split" (schizo) "mind" (phrenia)

>[!psych] schizophrenia spectrum disorders
>a group of [[psychological disorder|disorders]] characterized by delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thinking or speech, disorganized or unusual motor behavior, and negative symptoms (such as diminished emotional expression)


Schizophrenia is a [[psychotic disorder]]

![[psychotic disorder|clean no-t]]

Schizophrenia comes in varied forms:

People with Schizophrenia display symptoms that are 
- **positive**: *inappropriate* behaviors are *present*
- **negative**: *appropriate* behaviors are *absent*

Those with *positive* symptoms may:
- experience disturbed perceptions
- talk in disorganized or deluded ways
- exhibit inappropriate laughter, tears or rage

Those with *negative* symptoms may exhibit:
- absence of emotion in their voices
- expressionless faces
- mute and rigid bodies

People with schizophrenia sometimes *hallucinate*, see, hear, feel, taste, or smell things that exist only in their minds;
Most often, the hallucinations are voices, which sometimes make insulting remarks or give orders

Hallucinations are false *perceptions*. People with Schizophrenia also have disorganized, fragmented thinking, often distorted by false beliefs called [[delusions]].

![[delusions|clean no-t]]

One cause of disorganized thinking may be a breakdown in [[selective attention]].
People with Schizophrenia are easily distracted by tiny unrelated stimuli, such as the grooves on a brick, or the tones in a voice

Their speech may form *word salad*

The expressed emotions of schizophrenia are often inappropriate, for example laughing at someone's death
Others lapse into an emotionless *flat affect* state of no apparent feeling

Most also have an impaired [[Theory of Mind]] - difficulty reading other peoples' facial expressions and states of mind
unable to understand others' mental states, individuals with schizophrenia struggle to feel sympathy and compassion

These emotional deficiencies occur early in the illness and have a genetic basis

People with schizophrenia may experience [[catatonia]]

![[catatonia|clean no-t]]

***
An estimated 20 million people worldwide have schizophrenia
It typically strikes as young people are maturing into adulthood. Men tend to be diagnosed more often than women, and to be struck earlier, and with more severity.

#### Prenatal environment
Risk factors include:
- low birth weight
- maternal diabetes
- older paternal age
- oxygen deprivation during delivery
Famine and extreme [[stress]] also increase the risks

###### midpregnancy viral infection
Studies have shown that people are at increased risk of Schizophrenia if:
- their country experienced a flu epidemic during the middle of their [[Prenatal Development|fetal development]]
- born in a densely populated area, where viral diseases spread more rapidly
- born during the winter and spring months, therefore in utero during the fall-winter flu season
- the previous but reversed in the southern hemisphere, where the seasons are the reversed 
- mother was sick with influenza during pregnancy
- blood drawn from mother show higher-than-normal levels of antibodies suggesting a viral infection

#### Genetic influences
Some people are [[genes|genetically]] vulnerable to Schizophrenia

Normal odds are $\frac{1}{270}$
odds if one has a parent or sibling with the disorder: $\frac{1}{10}$
odds of one has an identical twin with the disorder: $\frac{1}{2}$

But identical twins share more than just genes, they share a prenatal environment
About $\frac{2}{3}$ share a placenta and the blood it supplies; 
If the co-twin of an identical twin shared the placenta the chances of developing the disorder are: $\frac{6}{10}$

If the identical twins had separate placentas, the odds drop down to $\frac{1}{10}$

Twins who share a placenta are more likely to share the same prenatal viruses, which may be a factor

Due to [[epigenetics|epigenetic factors]], certain experiences can "turn on" genes that increase risk for schizophrenia
