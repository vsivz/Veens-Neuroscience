
Abnormal brain activity and [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain structure]] accompany schizophrenia

**Frontal Lobes**
Some people diagnosed with schizophrenia have abnormally low brain activity in the brain's [[frontal lobes]], which help us reason, plan and solve problems
The brain waves that reflect synchronized neural firing in the frontal lobes decline noticeably. 

**Overactive Regions**
One study took PET scans of brain activity while people with Schizophrenia were hallucinating
When participants heard a voice or saw something, their brain became vigorously active in several core regions

One was the [[Thalamus]], which filters incoming sensory signals and transmits them to the [[Cerebral Cortex|cortex]]

Another PET scan study of people with paranoia found increased activity in the [[amygdala]], the fear-processing center

**Brain size**
In schizophrenia, fluid-filled brain cavities called ventricles become enlarged.
Cerebral tissue also shrinks

If one affected identical twin shows brain abnormalities, the odds are atleast 50% that the other twin's brain will have them 

Some studies even found these abnormalities in people who later developed the disorder. The greater the brain shrinkage, the more severe the thought disorder.

Smaller-than-normal areas may include the cortex, the [[hippocampus]] and the [[corpus callosum]]
Often, the thalamus is smaller than normal, which may be why filtering sensory input and focusing attention can be difficult

Schizophrenia also involves a loss of neural connections across the brain network

Schizophrenia involves not just a single isolated brain abnormality, but problems with several brain regions and their interconnections
