
<mark class="na">800,000</mark> people annually die from suicide. 

Researchers have found a number of differences between the suicide rates of different groups:
- <mark class="ls">National differences</mark>- Russia has <mark class="na">twice</mark> the suicide rate of the United States, which has <mark class="na">twice</mark> the rate in Spain
- <mark class="ls">Racial differences</mark>- Within the United States, White aend Indigenous people die by suicide <mark class="na">twice</mark> as often as Black, Hispanic, or Asian People. The rate among Indigenous Canadians is triple that of other Canadians
- <mark class="ls">[[Male-Female Differences|Gender Differences]]</mark> - Women and girls are more likely than men to consider or attempt suicide. But worldwide, men are twice as likely to actually die by suicide. 
- <mark class="ls">Trait differences</mark>- Suicidal thoughts increase when perfectionist people feel driven to reach a goal or standard but find it unattainable
- <mark class="ls">Age differences and trends</mark>- In [[Late Adulthood]], suicide rates increase worldwide, with the highest rates found among those older than <mark class="na">70</mark>
- <mark class="ls">Other group differences</mark>
	- Rates are higher among the rich, nonreligious, and unmarried
	- <mark class="na">One in four</mark> transgender adults has attempted suicide, and this rate doubles if the person is Indigenous
- <mark class="ls">Year-by-year differences</mark>- In most countries, suicide is increasing. Between <mark class="na">2009-2019</mark>, US high school students' rates increased by <mark class="na">62%</mark>
