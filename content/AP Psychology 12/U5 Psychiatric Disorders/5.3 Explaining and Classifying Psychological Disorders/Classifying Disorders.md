In many countries, the most common tool for describing disorders is the [[APA]]'s [[DSM-5-TR]], re-released in 2022

![[DSM-5-TR|clean no-t]]

Trained mental health workers use this to guide diagnoses.
For example, someone who meets <mark class="ii">all</mark> of the criteria for [[insomnia]] may be diagnosed with an insomnia disorder
But for other disorders, like [[major depressive disorder]], only some criteria is needed to be met

Real world tests (field trials) have showed significant variation in the [[reliability]] of the DSM's diagnostic results

For some disorders, like childhood [[autism spectrum disorder]], Clinician Agreement was <mark class="na">70%</mark> (If one psychiatrist made a diagnosis, there was a 70% chance another would)

For some others, like [[Generalized Anxiety Disorder]], agreement was closer to <mark class="na">20%</mark>

***

The DSM has been critisized frequently

One reason is that its criteria may be so broad that regular behaviors get pathologized

Another issue is how we assign labels to individuals, which affect how we see their behavior, and how they see themselves, which may affect their actions in a [[self-fulfilling prophecy]]
