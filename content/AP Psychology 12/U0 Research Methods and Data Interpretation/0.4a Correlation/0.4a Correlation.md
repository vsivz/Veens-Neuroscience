*Correlational Research* is a [[0.3b Non-Experimental Methods|non-experimental]] method that describes the relationship between two or more [[variable|variables]]

[[naturalistic observation]] and [[survey|surveys]] often show us that one [[traits|trait]] or behavior tends to coincide with another. 
In such cases, we say the two *correlate*.

>[!psych] correlation
>a measure of the extent to which two factors vary together, and thus of how well either factor predicts the other

![[variable|clean no-t]]

A statistical measure, the [[correlation coefficient]], helps us figure out how closely two things vary together, and thus how well either one *predicts* the other. 

![[correlation coefficient|clean no-t]]

[[scatterplot|Scatterplots]] can be a very revealing way to show how strongly two variables are related. 

![[scatterplot|clean no-t]]

Correlational research has a *directionality problem*: it cannot tell us which variable is the cause, and which one is the effect. 
For example, if teen social media use correlates with [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]], that could indicate that social media use causes depression, or that depression leads people to use more social media. 
The research also have a *third variable problem*
