>[!psych] preparedness
>a biological predisposition to learn [[associative learning|associations]], such as between taste and nausea, that have survival value

It was initially thought that any natural response could be conditioned to any [[neutral stimulus]]
But this was shown to be false, an animals's capacity for conditioning is limited by biological constraints
Each species' predispositions *prepare* it to learn the associations that enhance its survival

>[!experiment]
>Garcia and Koelling were challengers to the prevailing idea that all associations could be learned equally well
>They exposed rates to a particular taste, sight, or sound ([[conditioned stimulus|CS]]), and later also to radiation or drugs ([[unconditioned stimulus|UCS]]) that led to nausea ([[unconditioned response|UCR]]) 
>There were 2 startling discoveries
>
>(1) Even if sickened as late as several hours after tasting a particular novel flavor, rats thereafter avoided that flavor. 
>This appeared to violate the notion that the UCS must follow the CS. 
>
>(2) The rats developed aversions to tastes, but not to sights or sounds.
>This contradicted the [[behaviorism|behaviorists]]' idea that any perceivable [[stimulus]] could be a CS.
>But it made adaptive sense. For rates the easiest way to identify tainted food is to taste it; if sickened after sampling new food, they later avoid it. 

Humans also seem biologically prepared to learn certain associations. If one becomes very sick hours after eating oysters, one may develop an aversion to the taste of oysters more so than the sight of the restaurant or the music you listened to while eating, and may experience [[one-trial conditioning]]
