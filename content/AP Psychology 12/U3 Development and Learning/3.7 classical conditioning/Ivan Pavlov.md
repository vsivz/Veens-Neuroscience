This Russian Psychologist is famous for his experiments with [[3.7 classical conditioning|classical conditioning]]

He came upon his big discovery while studying dogs' digestive system

He noticed that putting food in a dog's mouth caused salivation. But not just the taste of food, but also in response to the sight of food, the food dish, the person delivering the food, and even the sound of that person's footsteps.

He realized that this pointed to a simple form of learning, demonstrating the principles of classical conditioning in his now famous experiment:

Pavlov and his team of researchers objectively experimented. 

They paired various [[neutral stimulus|neutral stimuli]], events the dog could see/hear and didn't [[associative learning|associate]] with food, with food.
If a sound regularly signaled the arrival of food, the dog, anticipating the food, began salivating upon hearing the tone

![[neutral stimulus|clean no-t]]


However, a dog does not learn to salivate in response to food in its mouth. 
Food in the mouth, *unconditionally*, triggers a dog's salivary reflex. Thusly, Pavlov called this drooling an [[unconditioned response]], and the food an [[unconditioned stimulus]]

![[unconditioned response|clean no-t]]
![[unconditioned stimulus|clean no-t]]

Salivation in response to a tone however, is [[3.7 Learning|learned]]
It is *conditional*, upon the dog's associating a tone with the food
Thus, this response is the [[conditioned response]]
The stimulus that used to be neutral, in this case a previously meaningless tone, is the [[conditioned stimulus]]
