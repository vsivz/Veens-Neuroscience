Sex <mark class="gc">[[hormones]]</mark> influence us at several points in our lifespan
- During the <mark class="lc">[[Prenatal Sexual Development|Prenatal Period]]</mark>, they <mark class="vi">direct</mark> our sexual development
- During <mark class="lc">[[Puberty]]</mark>, a sex hormone surge ushers us into <mark class="lc">[[Adolescence]]</mark>; in adolescent males, higher testosterone levels predict more male-typical traits and disorders
- After puberty and well into the late adult years, sex hormones <mark class="ii">facilitate sexual behavior</mark>

Women have <mark class="ii">less testosterone</mark> than men do
yet women are responsive to their testosterone level
testosterone-replacement therapy sometimes helps restore dimiinished arousal, desire, and sexual activity

In men, normal fluctuations in testosterone levels have little effect on sexual drive
In fact, men's hormone levels sometimes vary <mark class="ii">in response</mark> to sexual stimulation

 <mark class="vi">Large hormonal surges or declines</mark> affect sexual desire at <mark class="na">two</mark> predictable points, and sometimes an unpredictable third point
 
 <mark class="ls">The pubertal surge in sex hormones</mark>
 - triggers the development of sexual characteristics and sexual interest
 - If puberty's hormonal surge is precluded, sex characteristics and sexual desire do not develop normally

<mark class="ls">In later life</mark> sex horomone levels fall
- Women experience menopause as their estrogen levels decrease
- Men experience a more gradual change

For some people, <mark class="ls">Surgery or drugs</mark> may cause hormonal shifts
- Male sex offenders who took Depo-Provera, a drug that reduced testosterone levels to that of a prepubertal boy, lost much of their sexual urge
- If a woman's natural testosterone level drops, ex. because of the removal of the ovaries, her sexual interest may wane
