US teen pregnancy rates have declined, but social factors still contribute to teens' sexual risk taking

<mark class="ls">Communication</mark>
Most teens are uncomfortable discussing sex and contraception with parents, partners and peers.
Teens who talk freely and openly with their parents and with their partner are more likely to use contraceptives

<mark class="ls">Impulsivity</mark>
If passion overwhelms intentions to use contraceptives, unplanned sexual activity may result in unsafe sex and pregnancy

<mark class="ls">Alcohol Use</mark>
Among older teens and young adults, most sexual encounters outside a relationship occur after <mark class="gc">[[alcohol]]</mark> use, often without affirmative consent
By depressing the brain centers that control judgement, inhibition, and self-awareness, alcohol disarms normal restraints
People who use alcohol prior to sexual activity are also less likely to use condoms

<mark class="ls">Mass Media</mark>
Popular Media influence teens by providing <mark class="kv">social scripts</mark> for sexual behavior
Media also affect peer perceptions: the more sexual content adolescents and young adults view or read, the more likely they are to: 
- perceive their peers as sexually active
- develop sexually permissive attitudes
- experience early intercourse
- use condoms inconsistently
