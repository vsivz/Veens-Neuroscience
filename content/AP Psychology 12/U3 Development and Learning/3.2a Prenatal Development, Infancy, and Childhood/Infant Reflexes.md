**What are newborns capable of?**
Newborns come equipped with <mark class="vi">automatic survival responses</mark> ideally suited for our survival

When their cheek is touched, babies turn toward that touch, open their mouth and <mark class="kv">root</mark> for a nipple
Finding one, they close on it and begin <mark class="kv">sucking</mark>
Other reflexes include:
- <mark class="kr">startle</mark> reflex, when arms and legs spring out followed by fist clenching and loud crying
- when something touches the palm of their hand, they display a surprisingly strong <mark class="kv">grasping reflex</mark>

Researchers exploit <mark class="gc">habituation</mark>, demonstrated by infants getting bored with non-novel stimuli, to ask infants what they <mark class="ii">see and remember</mark>

Newborns prefer sights and sounds that <mark class="vi">facilitate social responsiveness</mark>
They turn their head in the direction of human voices.
They gaze longer at a drawing of a face-like image
Even late-stage fetuses look more at face-like patterns in red lights shined into the womb
Young infants prefer to look at objects <mark class="na">8-12 inches</mark> away - the distance between a nursing infant's eyes and its mother's
Within days after birth, our brain's <mark class="gc">[[neural networks]]</mark> were stamped with the smell of our mother's body. We wil later have a preference for these scents.
