## Brain Development
In the womb, your developing brain formed nerve cells at a rate of almost <mark class="na">$\text{a quarter million} \over min$</mark>

From infancy on, brain and mind - <mark class="qu">neural hardware and cognitive software</mark> develop together. 
Upon birth, you had most of the brain cells you would ever have. 
But your nervous system was immature
After birth, your branching <mark class="gc">[[neural networks]]</mark> underwent <mark class="ii">rapid development</mark>, explaining why infant brain size increases so rapidly in the early days

From ages <mark class="na">3 to 6</mark>, most rapid brain growth was in the <mark class="gc">[[frontal lobes]]</mark>, which enabled rational planning
During those years, the brain required vast amouts of energy. This energy intensive process caused rapid progress in your ability to <mark class="vi">control attention and behavior</mark>.

As they did, your mental abilities surged.

>[!experiment]
>Some young rats were raised in solitary confinement, while others were raised in a communal playground that simulated a natural environment
>Later analysis of the rat's brains showed that rats in the enriched environment usually developed a heavier and thicker cortex

In Humans too, lack of stimulation can slow brain and cognitive development
Stimulation by touch or massage benefits 
Preemies who have had skin-to-skin contact with their parents sleep better, experience less stress, and show better cognitive development 10 years later

<mark class="gt">[[Nature vs. Nurture|Nature and Nurture]]</mark> interact to sculpt our synapses. 
Brain maturation provides us with an abundance of neural connections. 
Experinces activate and strengthen some neural pathways while others weaken. 
By puberty, this pruning process results in a massive loss of unemployed connections

During early childhood, while excess connections are still there, ready to be used, young children can most easily master skills such as the grammar and accent of another language. 
We seem to have a <mark class="kv">critical period</mark>, or <mark class="lc">[[sensitive period]]</mark> for some skills
Lacking any exposure to spoken, written, or signed language before adolescense, a person will never master any language
Likewise, without early visual experience, a person whose vision is restored by cataract removal will never achieve normal perceptions- because the brain cells normally assigned to vision will die or be diverted to other uses

>[!psych] Critical Period
>an optimal period early in the life of an organism when exposure to certain stimuli or experinces produces normal development

But brain development does not end with childhood. 
Thanks to the brains <mark class="gc">[[neuroplasticity]]</mark>, our neural tissue is ever changing and reorganizing in response to new experiences.
The developing brain enables 
-  <mark class="kv">fine motor</mark> coordination - involving the <mark class="vi">small muscles of the body</mark>
- <mark class="kv">gross motor</mark> coortination - involving large muscles and whole-body movement

With occasional exceptions, this <mark class="ii">development sequence is universal</mark>
In the United States
- <mark class="na">25%</mark> of babies walk by <mark class="na">11 months</mark>
- <mark class="na">50%</mark> of babies walk by <mark class="na">53 weeks</mark>
- <mark class="na">90%</mark> of babies walk by <mark class="na">15 months</mark>

Identical twins typically begin walking on nearly the same day

Maturation, including rapid development of <mark class="gc">[[Cerebellum]]</mark> creates our readiness to learn walking by the age of 1
The same is true for other physical skills, including bowel and bladder control - before necessary muscular and neural maturation, neither pleading nor punishmenet will produce successful toilet training.
