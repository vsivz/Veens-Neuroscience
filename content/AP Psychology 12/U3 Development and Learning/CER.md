#### Claim
The evidence supports that enviromental factors are significant factors in language development
#### Evidence 1
According to Source A, "children from socio-economically advantaged backgrounds tend to have richer language evironments and more opportunities for language learning compared to tohse from disadvantaged backgrounds."
Furthermore, Source A also states that "Parental education level and engagement in literacy activites were strong predictors of children's language skills."
In addition to this Source B suggests that there is a significant genetic component, which was measured using twin studies. 
#### Reasoning 1
The evidence was collected through a variety of methods. This diversity in research methods reduces bias from a single unfair test, making it likely that the result is *statistically significant*. 
Furthermore, the concept of *observational learning* would apply here. Families from socio-economically priveleged families would be more likely to have one parent be able to stay at home with the child. This increased ability to observe the parent speaking would result in a substansial increase in the child's language ability. 
This would be exemplified by parental education levels, which would be higher in more socio-economically priveleged households, as a higher education level would result in better quality language for the child to observe. This is also demonstrated in the evidence that parental education level were strong predictors in language ability. 
Finally, this concept also explains the findings in Source B. Source B may be unintentionally actually measuring talkativity in general. This is because identical twins would likely spend a lot of time together, and twins genetically predisposed to talk more, would increase the chance that the other twin would be able to *observe* more talking, which would increase their own abilities.
