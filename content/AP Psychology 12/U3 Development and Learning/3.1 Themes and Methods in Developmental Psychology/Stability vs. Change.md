Throughout our lives, we experience both stability and change
Some characteristics, like temperament, are <mark class="ii">very stable</mark> 
- Inhibited 14-month-olds become reserved adults
- Out-of-control children are more likely to engage in teen smoking or adult criminal behavior
- Children observed being repeatedly cruel to animals often become violent adults

Other aspects of our future are <mark class="ii">far less stable</mark>
- Our social attitudes are less stable
- Older children and adolescents learn new ways of coping

In some ways <mark class="ii">we all change with age</mark>
- Many shy toddlers begin opening up by <mark class="na">age 4</mark>
- after <mark class="na">adolescense</mark>, most people gradually become more <mark class="ii">conscientious, stable, agreeable, and self-confident</mark>
- Many risk prone adolescents become more cautious as adults

When asked if they have changed in the past decade, and will change in the next decade, people <mark class="ii">both young and old</mark> exhibit an <mark class="kv">end-of-history illusion</mark>

>[!psych] End Of History Illusion
>Recognizing change from the past, but presuming one will change little in the future

Life requires both stability and change. 
Stability provides our identiy. 
Change gives us hope for a brighter future.
