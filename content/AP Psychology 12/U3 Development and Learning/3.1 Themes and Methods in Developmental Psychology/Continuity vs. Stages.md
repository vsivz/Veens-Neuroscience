Researchers who emphasize <mark class="ii">experience and learning</mark> tend to see development as a <mark class="vi">slow, continuous process</mark>
Researchers who emphasize <mark class="ii">biological maturation</mark> tend to see development as a <mark class="vi">seqauence of genetically predisposed stages or steps</mark>
Although the <mark class="vi">time to progress</mark> through each stage may differ, everyone passes through these stages in the <mark class="vi">same order</mark>

The <mark class="kv">stage theories</mark> (Piaget's theory of cognitive development, Erikson's Theory of Psychosocial development etc. ) propose developmental stages.
Many modern developmental psychologists <mark class="ii">do not identify as stage theorists</mark>
But the stage concept is still useful
The human brain experiences growth spurts during childhood and puberty that correspond roughly to Piaget's stages
Also, stage theories contribute a developmental perspective on the whole lifespan
