**How do caregiver-infant attachment bonds form?**
Most babies are social creatures, developing an <mark class="ii">intense attachment</mark>to their caregivers
Infants come to prefer familiar faces and voices

By <mark class="na">4.5 months</mark>, infants can distinguish between familiar and unfamiliar languages
After about <mark class="na">8 months</mark>, soon <mark class="lc">[[Object Permanence]]</mark> emerges and children become mobile, children develop <mark class="kv">separation anxiety</mark> when away from caregivers and <mark class="kv">stranger anxiety</mark> when in the prescence of strangers

Children this age have <mark class="gc">[[schemas]]</mark> for familiar faces

>[!psych] stranger anxiety
>the fear of strangers that infants commonly display, beginning by about 8 months of age

# Physical Contact


One-year-olds typically cling tightly to a caregiver when they are frightened or expect separation
This caregiver-infant <mark class="kv">attachment</mark> bond is a powerful survival impulse

>[!psych] attachment
> an emotional tie with others; show in young children by their seeking closeness to caregivers and showing distress on separation

![[Harlow's Experiment|Embed]]

Most parent-infant emotional communication occurs via touch, which can be either <mark class="ii">soothing</mark>(snuggles) or <mark class="ii">arousing</mark>(tickles)
Such parental affection boosts brain development and later cognitive ability

Another aspect of human attachment is one person providing another with:
- a <mark class="ls">secure base</mark> from which to explore
- a <mark class="ls">safe haven</mark> when distressed

As we mature, our secure base <mark class="ii">shifts</mark>, from parents to peers to partners
