**What are the different kinds of attachment?**
<mark class="hv">Mary Ainsworth</mark> designed the <mark class="kv">strange situation</mark> to figure out what accounts for differences in children's attachment

>[!psych] strange situation
>a procudure for studying child-caregiver attachment; a child is placed in an unfamiliar environment while their caregiver leaves and then returns, and the child's reactions are observed

<mark class="hv">Erik Erikson</mark>, working with his wife <mark class="hv">Joan Erikson</mark>, believed that securely attached children approach life with a sense of <mark class="kv">basic trust</mark>
He attributed this to <mark class="vi">early parenting</mark> 
He theorized that infants with sensitive, loving caregivers form a lifelong attitude of trust rather than fear.

Many researchers now believe that our early attachments form the foundation for our adult relationships
People who report secure relationships with their parents tend to enjoy secure friendships

attachment can either be [[Secure Attachment|secure]] or [[Insecure Attachment|insecure]]

Studies reveal that heredity affects <mark class="kv">temperament</mark>, and that <mark class="vi">temperament affects attachment style</mark>

>[!psych] temperament
>a person's characteristic emotional reactivity and intensity

Some babies are noticeably difficult - irritable, intense and unpredictable
Others are easy, cheerful, relaxed, and on predictable feeding and sleeping schedules
<mark class="gc">[[identical twins]]</mark> are more likely than fraternal twins to have similar twins

Differences in temperament appear physiologically
Anxious, inhibited infants have high and variable heart rates and a reactive nervous system
Whe facing new or strange situations, they become more physiologically aroused

<mark class="vi">Temperament differences typically persist</mark>

Emotionally intense preschoolers tend to beocme relatively intense young adults
>[!study] 
> Dutch researcher Dympha van dem Boom randomly assigned 100 tempermentally difficult 6-9-month-olds to either an 
>- experimental group, where mothers received personal training in sensitivce responding
>- control group, where they did not
>At 12 months of age, 68% of the infants in the experimental group were securely attached, compared to 28% in the control group

Other studies confirm that intervention programs can increase parental sensitivity and, to a lesser extent, infant attachment security
Such interventions seem to be especially beneficial for children with difficult temperaments
Studies have shown that a father's love and acceptance are comparable with a mother's love in predicting an offspring's health and well-being. 

Children's anxiety over separatino from parents peaks at around <mark class="na">13 months</mark>, then <mark class="ii">gradually declines</mark>
This happens regardless of:
- whether they have 1 or 2 parents
- Cared for at home or in day care
- Which country they live in
But our capacity for love continues to grow
