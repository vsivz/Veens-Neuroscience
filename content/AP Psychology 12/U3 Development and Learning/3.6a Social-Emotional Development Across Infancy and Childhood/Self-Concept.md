**How and when do children know who they are?**
By the end of childhood, at <mark class="na">about age 12</mark>, most children have developed a <mark class="kv">self-concept</mark>.
Their <mark class="kv">[[self-esteem]]</mark> is how they <mark class="ii">feel</mark> about who they are

>[!psych] self-concept
>all our thoughts and feelings about ourselves, in answer to the question, "Who am I?"

>[!experiment] 
><mark class="hv">Charles Darwin</mark> offered an idea: self-awareness begins when we recognize ourself in a mirror
> To see whether a child recognizes that the child in the mirror is them, researchers sneakilny dabbed color on their nose
> At about <mark class="na">6 months</mark>, children reach out and touch the mirror image as if it were another child
> By <mark class="na">15-18</mark> months, they begin to touch their own noses when they see the colored spot appear in the mirror
> Apparantly <mark class="na">18-month-olds</mark> have a <mark class="gc">[[schemas|schema]]</mark> of how their face should look


By the time they reach <mark class="na">school age</mark>, children's self-concept now includes:
- gender identity
- group memberships
- psychological traits
- similarities and differences compared to other children
- They come to see themselves as good and skillful in some ways but not others
- They form a concept of traits which, ideally, they would like to have

By age <mark class="na">8-10</mark> their self-image is quite stable
