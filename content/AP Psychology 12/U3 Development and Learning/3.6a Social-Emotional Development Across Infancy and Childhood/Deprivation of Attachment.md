**How does adversity affect children?**
<mark class="kv">Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)</mark>, related to deprivation of attachment, are potentially traumatizing.
Babies locked away at home under conditions of <mark class="vi">abuse or extreme neglect</mark>, are often <mark class="ii">withdrawn, frightened, and even speechless</mark>

The same is true of those raised in institutions without the stimulation and attention of a regular caregivers. 
In <mark class="hv">1970s and 1980s in Romania</mark>, many families left chidlren at government-run orphantages, with untrained and overworked staff.
Child-to-caregiver ratios were often 15:1, so the children were deprived of healthy attachments with atleast one adult. 

Whe the deprived chidlren were tested years later, and compared with children assigned to quality foster care, they had lower intelligence scores, abnormal stress responses, and quadruple the rate of <mark class="gc">[[ADHD]]</mark>

## Trauma, Abuse and Poverty
Many children who grow up under adversity are <mark class="ii">resilient</mark>
They withstand trauma and become well-adjusted adults
So do most victims of childhood sexual abuse

But many others who experience enduring abuse don't recover so easy
The <mark class="lc">[[Harlow's Experiment|Harlow's Monkeys]]</mark>, raised in total isolation bore life-long scars
As adults, when placed with other monkeys their age, they either cowered in fright or lashed out in aggression
When they reached sexual maturity, most incapable of mating

Abused may <mark class="vi">become the abusers</mark>
Most abusive parents, and many murderers, have reported being neglected or battered as children
<mark class="na">30% of people who have been abused</mark> later abuse their children, <mark class="na">four times the US national rate</mark>
Abusive parents tend to abuse their own children in the ways they were abused as children, such as through neglect, emotional abuse, physical abuse, or sexual abuse

Although most abused children do not later become violent criminals or abusive parents, early extreme trauma may still leave footsteps on the brain
Abused children's brains respond to angry faces with <mark class="vi">heightened activity</mark> in threat detecting areas.
In conflict-plagued homes, even sleeping infants' brains show heightened reactivity to hearing angry speech
As adults, abused children are more uncomfortable when touched, and tend to struggle in adulthood to regulate negative emotions.
They exhibit stronger startle responses, and are <mark class="na">twice as likely</mark> to attempt <mark class="gc">[[suicide]]</mark>

Abused children show changes in their levels of <mark class="gc">[[serotonin]]</mark>, which calms agressivce impulses
Children raised in poverty show <mark class="ii">unique hardships</mark>
- less schooling
- as adolescents and adults, commit more crimes
- more <mark class="gc">[[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety disorders]]</mark>
- more <mark class="gc">[[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]]</mark>
- more <mark class="gc">[[substance use disorder|substance abuse]]</mark>
- more trouble getting a job

By sensitizing the stress response system, such early stress can permanently heighten reactions to later stress
Child abuse can also leave <mark class="gc">[[epigenetics|epigenetic marks]]</mark>

In a study of US adults, <mark class="na">8%</mark> reported experienceing physical abuse at least fairly often before the age of 18.
<mark class="na">84% of these individuals</mark> had experienced atleast one <mark class="gc">[[psychiatric disorder]]</mark>

Abuse victims are at especially heighened risk for depression if they carry a gene variation that spurs stress-hormone production, showing the interaction of <mark class="gt">[[Nature vs. Nurture|Nature and Nurture]]</mark>
