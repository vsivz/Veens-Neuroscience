**How does cuture influence gender roles?**
<mark class="vi">Cultures shape our behaviors</mark> by defining how we ought to behave in a particular <mark class="kv">role</mark>
we can see this in <mark class="kv">gender roles</mark>

>[!psych] role
>a set of expectations (norms) about a social position, defining how those in the position ought to behave

>[!psych] gender role
>a set of expected behaviors, attitudes, and traits for men and for women

<mark class="ii">Very recently, </mark> gender roles have <mark class="vi">changed drastically</mark>
At the beginnning of the 20th century, only one country (New Zealand) granted women the right to vote. By 2015, all countries had. 
But progress must still be made.

Globally, women are underrepresented in STEM
Men tend to hold the most faculty positions, receive greater financial research support and have more articles accepted in the most prestigious journals

Gender roles also <mark class="vi">vary from one place to another</mark>
Nomadic societies of food-gathering people have had little division of labor by sex. 

Expectations about gender roles also factor into <mark class="ii">cultural attitudes</mark> about <mark class="kv">Sexual Aggression</mark>

>[!psych] sexual aggression
>any physical or verbal behavior of a sexual nature that is unwanted or intended to harm someone physicall or emotionally. Can be expressed as either sexual harassment or sexual assault.
