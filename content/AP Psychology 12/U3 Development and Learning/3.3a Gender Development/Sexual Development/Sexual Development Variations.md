People who are intersex may be born with <mark class="vi">unusual combinations</mark> of  male and female chromosomes, hormones, and anatomy

Genetic males with <mark class="kv">Klinefelter Syndrome</mark> are born with two or more X chromosomes as well as a Y chromosome, which often results in sterility and small testes. 
Genetic females with <mark class="kv">Turner Syndrome</mark>, born with only one normal X chromosomes, may not have menstrual periods, develop breasts, or be able to have children without reproductive assistance

In the past, medical profssionals often recommended what used to be called <mark class="ii">sex-reassignment surgery</mark>, to create an unambiguous sex identity for these children.
Today, experts recommend postponing <mark class="vc">gender-affirming surgery</mark> until a child's naturally developing physical appearance and gender identity becomes clear.
