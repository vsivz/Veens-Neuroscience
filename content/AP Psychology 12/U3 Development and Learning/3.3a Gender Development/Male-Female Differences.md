**How do males and females differ, and how are they alike?**
Regardless of whether one is male, female or <mark class="kv">intersex</mark>, most people recieve <mark class="na">23 chromosomes</mark> from their mother and <mark class="na">23 chromosomes</mark> from their father. 
Of these 46 total, <mark class="na">45</mark> are unisex - same for everyone. 
Our similar biology helped our <mark class="gt">[[evolutionary psychology|evolutionary]]</mark> ancestors face similar adaptive challenges. 
So one's gender gives no clue to vocabulary, happiness or ability to see, learn, or remember.

![[intersex|clean no-t]]


But in some areas, female and male traits do differ
The average female enters <mark class="lc">[[Puberty]]</mark> about <mark class="na">2 years earlier</mark> than the average male, and her life expectancy is <mark class="na">4 years longer</mark>
She <mark class="vi">expresses emotions more freely</mark>, smiling and crying more. 
She is better at spelling and reading.
She can detect fainter odors
She has <mark class="na">twice the risk</mark> of developing <mark class="gc">[[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]]</mark> and <mark class="gc">[[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety disorders]]</mark> 
She has <mark class="na">10 times the risk</mark> of developing an <mark class="gc">[[eating disorder]]</mark>

The average male is <mark class="na">4 times as likely</mark> to die by <mark class="gc">[[suicide]]</mark>, to abuse tobacco, and to develop <mark class="gc">[[alcohol use disorder]]</mark>
He has greater size and strength
He is more likely to be diagnosed with <mark class="gc">[[autism spectrum disorder]]</mark>, <mark class="gc">[[Color-Deficient Vision]]</mark> and <mark class="gc">[[ADHD]]</mark>
As an adult, he is more at risk for <mark class="gc">[[antisocial personality disorder]]</mark> 

Females and Males each have their <mark class="vi">own heightened strengths and risks</mark>

## Aggression
Men generally admit to more <mark class="kv">aggression</mark>, especially extreme physical violence
 <mark class="na">Nearly $1 \over 2$</mark> of 14-19 year old US boys feel pressure to be "willing to punch someone if provoked"
In romantic relationships, minor acts of physical violence, ex. slaps, are roughly equal, but the mots violent acts are committed by men, including <mark class="na">90%</mark> of murders
Men take the lead in hunting, fighting, warring and supporting war

## Social Power

<mark class="na">almost half</mark> of humanity believe men to be <mark class="ii">superior political leaders</mark>
and more than <mark class="na">40%</mark> believe men to be better business executives

People tend to association negotiation with males, and men often have an advantage in negotiation outcomes.

When asked a difficult question, men are more liely to hazard answers they dont know, a phenomenon called <mark class="kv">male answer syndrome</mark>

## Social Connectedness
We all have a need to belong.
<mark class="hv">In the 1980s</mark>, many developmental psychologists believed that all children struggle to create a separate, independent identity
Modern research suggests that this describes <mark class="ii">Western, individualistic males</mark> more than <mark class="ii">relationship-oriented</mark> females
Males tend to be <mark class="vi">independent</mark>
As children, males typically form large play groups filled with activity and competition, with <mark class="ii">intimate discussion</mark>

Brain scans show no striking sex differences
These scans do suggest that a female's brain, more than a male's, is wired in a way that enables social relationships

Females tend to be more <mark class="vi">interdependent</mark>
as children, women are more likely to play in small groups, often with one friend. They compete less and imitate social relationships more
Teen girls spend more time with friends and less time alone
In late adolescence, they spend more time on social media, and average more daily text messages than boys
