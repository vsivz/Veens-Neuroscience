**How do children develop?**
Developmental Psychologist <mark class="hv">Jean Piaget</mark> studied children's develping cognition.
His interest began when he was in paris developing questions for children's intelligence tests
Piaget became intrigued by wrong answers, which were strikingly similar to other children of the same age group.
He saw developing intelligence in this.

Piaget realized that a child's brain is not a miniature model of an adult's.  
His obervations showed us that <mark class="vi">children reason differently than adults</mark>
Piaget believed that children's minds develop through a series of <mark class="lc">[[Continuity vs. Stages|stages]]</mark>, in an upward march from the newborn's simple reflexes to the adult's abstract reasoning power

Piaget proposed that children build <mark class="gc">[[schemas]]</mark>

Piaget viewed children's cognitive development as a process guided by biological maturation and environmental interaction: children construct their understanding of the world while experiencing it. 
In Piaget's view, cognitive development consists of <mark class="na">4 major stages</mark> each with distinctive characteristics that pertain to <mark class="ii">specific kinds of thinking</mark>

Piaget has been singled out by Time magazine as one of the 20th century's 20 most influential scientists and thinkers
He identified significant cognitive milestones, and stimulated worldwide interest in how the mind develops. 
His emphasis was less on ages, than on their sequence

But modern psychology views development as more <mark class="lc">[[Continuity vs. Stages|continuous]]</mark> than Piaget, who mostly focused on the <mark class="lc">discontinuous stages</mark>. 
Children's minds experience spurts of change, followed by greater stablilty as they progress from one cognitive plateau to the next. 
By detecting the beginnings of each type of thinking at earlier ages, modern researchers have revealed conceptual abilies Piaget missed. Moreover, they see formal logic as a smalller part of cognition than he did.
