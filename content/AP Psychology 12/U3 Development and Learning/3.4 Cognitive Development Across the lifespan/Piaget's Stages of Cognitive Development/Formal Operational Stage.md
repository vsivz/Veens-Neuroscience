By about <mark class="na">age 12</mark>, our reasoning expands from the purely concrete (<mark class="ii">involving actual experience</mark>) to encompass <mark class="vi">abstract thinking</mark> (involving <mark class="ii">symbols</mark> or <mark class="ii">imagined realities</mark> such as beauty)

As <mark class="lc">[[Adolescence|adolescents]]</mark>,  children can now ponder hypothetical propositions and deduce consequences: <mark class="vi">if *this* then *that*</mark> 

Systematic reasoning, what Piaget called <mark class="ii">Formal Operational</mark> thinking, is within their grasp

Although full-blown logic and reasoning await adolescence, the rudiments of formal operational thinking begin earlier than Piaget realized. 
Ex
`If John is in school, then Mary is in school. John is in school. What can you say about Mary?`
Formal operational thinkers have no trouble answering, but neither do most 7-year-olds
