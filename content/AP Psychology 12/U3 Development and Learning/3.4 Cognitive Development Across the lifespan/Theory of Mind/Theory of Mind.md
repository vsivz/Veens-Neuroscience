**When do we start to understand others?**
Preschoolers, although still <mark class="lc">[[Egocentrism|egocentric]]</mark>, develop this ability to infer others' mental states when they begin to form a <mark class="kv">theory of mind</mark>

>[!psych] theory of mind
>people's ideas about their own and other's mental states - about their feelings, perceptions, and thoughts, and the behaviors these might predict

As this ability gradually develops, preschoolers learn to use their understanding of how their actions will make others feel

Between about <mark class="na">ages 3 and 4.5</mark>, children begin to realize that others may <mark class="vi">hold false beliefs</mark>
>[!experiment] 
>Jennifer Jenkins and Janet Astington showed Canadian children a Band-Aid box and asked them what was inside. 
>The box actually contained pencils, surprising the children.
>Asked what other children would think was in the box, a <mark class="na">3-year-old</mark> would answer "pencils"
>By age <mark class="na">4 to 5</mark>, children developed their Theory of Mind enough to understand that other children would answer "Band-Aids"

Children with <mark class="gc">[[autism spectrum disorder]]</mark> have demonstrated difficulty with this
Such children also have difficulty reflecting on their own mental states. 
They are less likely to use the personal pronouns `I` and `me`
