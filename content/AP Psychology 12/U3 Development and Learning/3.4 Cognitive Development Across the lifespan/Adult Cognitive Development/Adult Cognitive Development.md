**What happens to a developed brain after development**
As we age, our thinking, reaction speed, and sensory abilities begin to <mark class="ii">diminish</mark>, even though impulse control and wisdom may grow
Older adults are more prone to <mark class="gc">[[demetia]]</mark>

Looking back later in life, adults asked to recall the one or two most important events over the last half-century, tend to name events from when they were in their <mark class="ii">teens or twenties</mark>

This <mark class="kv">reminiscence bump</mark> is also displayed when asked to name their all-time favorite music, movies and athletes

***

>[!experiment]
>people watched video clips as 14 strangers said their names, using a common format
>Even after a second or third replay of the information with more personal information, younger adults consistently remembered moer names thatn older adults

However, when asked to <mark class="gc">[[recognition|recognize]]</mark> 24 words they earlier tried to memorize, older adults showed no signs of memory decline like they did when they had to <mark class="gc">[[recall]]</mark>

Teens and young adults surpass both young children and 70-year-olds at <mark class="gc">[[prospective memory]]</mark>
But older people's prospective memory remains strong when <mark class="gc">[[retrieval cues|events help trigger]]</mark> a memory

By comparison time-based tasks, and especially habitual tasks can be challenging.

Young adults vary in abilities to learn and remember, but 70-year-olds vary much more.

When information is <mark class="ls">meaningless</mark>, older people have more trouble, but when the information is <mark class="ls">meaningful</mark>, older people's rich <mark class="gc">[[crystallized intelligence]]</mark> helps support that information.

But they may take longer than younger adults to produce the information, and are more prone to the <mark class="gc">[[tip-of-the-tongue phenomeon]]</mark>

***

More education earlier in life predicts better cognitive ability late in life.
Our brain remains <mark class="gc">[[neuroplasticity|plastic]]</mark> throughout life

One analysis of cognitive training programs showed that they consistently improved scores on <mark class="vi">tests related to their training</mark>
But more studies show that many programs improve performance on closely related tasks, but not on unrelated tasks.

Age is less a predictor of memory and intelligence than proximity to a natural death.
<mark class="kv">terminal decline</mark> is the typical acceleration of cognitive decline in the last 3-4 years, and especially as death gets close
