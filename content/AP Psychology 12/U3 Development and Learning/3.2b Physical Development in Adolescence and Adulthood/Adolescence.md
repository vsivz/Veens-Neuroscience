**How do physical changes affect developing teens?**
>[!psych] Adolescence
>the transition period from childhood to adulthood, extending from puberty to independence

Thus, in cultures where postpubertal teens are self-supporting, such as among Aboriginal Australians, adolescence hardly exists.
In Western cultures, where sexual maturation occurs earlier and independence later, adolescence is <mark class="ii">lengthening</mark>

<mark class="hv">G. Stanley Hall</mark> believed that the tension between <mark class="ii">biological maturity</mark> and <mark class="ii"> social dependence</mark> creates a period of "storm and stress"

Its a time characterized by
- <mark class="vi">diminishing parental control</mark>
- teens craving <mark class="ii">social acceptence</mark>, but often feeling socially <mark class="vi">isolated</mark>

<mark class="na">$3 \over 4$ US friendships</mark> that started in the seventh grade dissolve by the end of the eighth grade

When people from the west look back, they see good and bad, viewing it as a time where:

Adolescence begins with <mark class="gc">[[Puberty]]</mark>, the time when we mature sexually

>[!psych] puberty
>the period of sexual maturation, during which a person usually becomes capable of reproducing

Just like in earlier life stages, the <mark class="vi">sequence</mark> of physical changes is far more predictabe than their <mark class="vi">timing</mark>

Early maturing adolescents are at increased risk for <mark class="gc">[[Mental Health|mental health problems]]</mark>
This vulnerability is greatest for teens with emotionally reactive <mark class="lc">[[Temperament and Parenting|temperaments]]</mark>

***

The adolescent brain is a <mark class="vi">work in progress</mark>
Until puberty brain cells <mark class="gc">[[neuroplasticity|increase their connections]]</mark>
Then, during adolescence comes a selective <mark class="ii">pruning</mark> of unused neurons and connections

As teens mature, their <mark class="gc">[[Prefrontal cortex]]</mark> continues to develop
The continuing growth of <mark class="gc">[[neurons|myelin]]</mark> enables better communication with other brain regions

these developments bring improved judgements, impulse control and long-term planning

Maturation of the prefrontal cortex nevertheless lags behind that of the emotional <mark class="gc">[[Limbic System]]</mark>
Puberty's hormonal surge and limbic system development help explain teens' impulsivenes, riskly behavior and emotinal storms

Teens don't underestimate the risks of behaviors like vaping or fast driving, their bains are just biased towards <mark class="vi">immediate rewards</mark>, which explain why teens worldwide struggle with self-control

The prefrontal cortex will continue to mature until about <mark class="na">age 25</mark>
It will alsto become better connected with the limbic system, enabling <mark class="ii">better emotional regulation</mark>

Brain scans of young teens reveal that prefrontal cortex immaturity is most evident among juvenile offenders and drug users
