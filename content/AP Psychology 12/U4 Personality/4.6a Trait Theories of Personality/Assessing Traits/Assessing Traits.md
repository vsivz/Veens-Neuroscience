Several trait-assessment techniques exist - some more valid than others.
Some provide quick assessments of a single trait, ex. extraversion, anxiety, self-esteem

<mark class="kv">Personality Inventories</mark> are longer questionnaires, covering a wide range of feelings and behaviors, assessing multiple traits at once.

![[personality inventory|clean no-t]]

One classic personality inventory is the <mark class="kv">Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI)</mark> 

![[Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory|clean no-t]]

the MMPI items were <mark class="kv">empirically derived</mark>
From a large pool of items, Hathaway (one of the creators) and his collegues selected those on which particular diagnostic groups differed. 

For example "My hands and feet are usually warm enough", does not seem useful, but people with anxiety were more likely to answer false

The researchers grouped the questions into 10 clinical scales, including:
- depressive tendencies
- masculinity-femininity
- introversion-extraversion

the modern MMPI-2 has additional scales that assess work attitudes, family problems, and anger
![[Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory|clean no-t]]
Where as most [[projective test]] are scored subjectively, personality inventories are scored objectively. 

But that does not guarantee validity, 
Ex. individuals taking MMPI for work may lie to create a good impression
