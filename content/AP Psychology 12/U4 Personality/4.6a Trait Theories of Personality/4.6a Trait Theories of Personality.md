By placing people on several trait dimensions simultaneously, psychologists can describe countless individual personality variations

But psychologists have the challenging task of condensing the very large list of possible descriptors to a manageable number of basic traits

One technique is [[factor analysis]], a statistical procedure that identifies clusters (factors) of test items that tap basic components of a trait. 

For example, one who describes themselves as outgoing may also say that they like excitement and practical jokes and dislike quiet reading

This is a statistically correlated cluster of behaviors that reflect a basic factor, or trait, which in this case would be extraversion
