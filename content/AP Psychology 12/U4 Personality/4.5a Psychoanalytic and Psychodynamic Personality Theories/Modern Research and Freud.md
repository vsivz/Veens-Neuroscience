
[[Sigmund Freud|Freud]] was usually wrong about a lot of things
There are a few salvageable Freudian ideas:
- drawing attention to the unconscious and irrational
- importance of human sexuality
- conflict between biological impulses and social restraints
- some defense mechanisms

[[repression]] is partially correct
We sometimes preserve our [[self-esteem]] by neglecting threatening information
Repression itself is rare, even in response to terrible trauma. 
Some researchers do believe that extreme, prolonged [[stress]], might disrupt memory by damaging the [[hippocampus]]
But typically, high stress and associated stress [[hormones]] enhance memory.

Freud was right about the significance of what he called the [[unconscious]]
However, many psychologists now see the unconscious not as a mass of seething passions and repressive censoring, but as a form of information processing that occurs outside our awareness. 
The subconscious also involves:
- [[schemas]] we build
- [[priming]] to stimuli
- right-hemisphere activity that allows [[split-brain]] patient's left hand to carry out an instruction the patient cannot verbalize
- the [[implicit memory]]
- the [[emotion]] activated instantly
- the [[stereotypes]] and implicit [[prejudice]]

Research also supports two of Freud's [[defense mechanisms]]
Reaction formation:
People who have an unconscious same-sex orientation, but who consciously identify as straight, report more negative attitudes towards gay men and lesbians

Projection:
People see their traits, attitudes and goals in others

Research has also shown that we unconsciously defend ourselves from [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]
Experiments testing [[terror-management theory]] show that thinking about one's mortality provokes various terror-management defenses
