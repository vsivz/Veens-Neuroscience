The Oedipus Complex is [[Sigmund Freud|Freud]]'s idea that boys, during the [[psychosexual stages|phallic stage]] develop both unconscious sexual desires for their mother and jealousy and hatred for their father, whom they think of as a rival

He thought that these feelings would cause boys to feel guilty and fear punishment, possibly by castration

He continued that children cope with these feelings by [[repression|repressing]] them, and trying to be like the rival parent. 

Through this identification process, [[superego]]s gain strength as they incorporate many of their parent's values

Freud believed that identifying with a same-sex parent gave us our sense of [[Gender Identity]]

Freud believed that unresolved conflicts during the [[psychosexual stages]] could resurface in adulthood as [[maladaptive behavior]]
At any point in these stages, strong conflict could fixate- lock- one's pleasure-seeking energies in that stage
