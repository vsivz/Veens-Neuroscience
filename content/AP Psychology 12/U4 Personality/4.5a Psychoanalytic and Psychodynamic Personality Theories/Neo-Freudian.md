
[[Sigmund Freud|Freud]]'s ideas were very controversial, but he attracted followers
Several young, ambitious, physicians formed an inner circle around their strong-minded leader
These early [[5.5a Psychoanalysis|psychoanalysts]], often called Neo-Freudians, adopted Freud's techniques and accepted his basic ideas:
- the personality structure of the [[id]], [[ego]], and [[superego]]
- the importance of the [[unconscious]]
- the childhood roots of personality
- the dynamics of [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]] and [[defense mechanisms]]

But there were two clear distinctions
First, they emphasized the conscious mind's role in interpreting experience
Secondly, they doubted that sex and [[aggression]] were all-consuming motives

Alfred Adler and Karen Horney agreed with Freud that childhood is important
But they believed that childhood social, not sexual, tensions are crucial for personality formation
