The humanistic view of personality focused on the <mark class="vi">potential for healthy personal growth</mark>
and people striving for <mark class="ii">self-determination</mark> and <mark class="ii">self-transcendence</mark>

![[Drawing 2024-12-10 08.05.41.excalidraw]]



Carl Rogers' had a <mark class="kv">person-centered perspective</mark>
Carl Rogers believed that the ingredients of a growth-promoting environment are:
- acceptance
- genuineness
- empathy

when people are accepting, they offer unconditional positive regard
>[!psych] unconditional positive regard
>a caring, accepting, nonjudgmental attitude, which Carl Rogers believed would help people develop self-awareness and self-acceptance




[[Self-Concept]] was a key point for both Maslow and Rogers

Our ideal self is who we want to be, while our actual self is what we actually are



Some humanistic psychologists rejected standardized assessments and relied on interviews and conversations
Others like Rogers used questionnaires where people described their ideal and actual selves, which were later used to judge progress during [[therapy]]

Some now use the life story approach, which creates a rich narrative detailing each person's unique life history

The big problem with humanistic psychology is that its vague and subjective.
