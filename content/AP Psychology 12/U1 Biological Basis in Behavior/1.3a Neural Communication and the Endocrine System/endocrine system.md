Interconnected with the [[1.2 Overview of the Nervous System|Nervous System]] is a second 'slow' communication system, the Endocrine System
The endocrine system secretes another form of [[Chemistry|chemical]] messengers, [[hormones]], which travel through the bloodstream, and affect other tissues, including the brain. 

![[hormones|clean no-t]]

When hormones act on the brain, they influence interest on sex, food, and [[aggression]]
Some hormones are chemically identical to [[neurotransmitter]]s

Endocrine messages tend to outlast the effects of neural messages

In a moment of danger, the [[Autonomic Nervous System|ANS]] orders the adrenal glands to release [[epinephrine]] and [[norepinephrine]], (also known as adrenaline and noradrenaline)

These hormones increase heart-rate, blood pressure, and blood sugar, providing a surge of energy to power our fight-or-flight response

The most influential endocrine gland is the [[pituitary gland]]

Among the hormones released by the pituitary gland are the:
- [[growth hormone]] - stimulates physical development
- [[oxytocin]] - enables orgasm, in women labor contractions and milk flow while nursing; aids in social support

Pituitary secretions also direct other glands to release their hormones
