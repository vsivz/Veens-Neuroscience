Neural Transmission is the process by which [[neurons]] communicate with each other through [[Science/Physics11/Electric Circuits/Electric Circuits|electrical]] and [[Chemistry|chemical]] signals

![[action potential|clean no-t]]

***
![[Screenshot_20241225_152930.png]]

Like batteries, [[neurons]] generate [[Science/Physics11/Electric Circuits/Electric Circuits|electricity]] from [[Chemistry|chemical events]]. 

**Main Steps**:
1. <mark class="ls">Resting State</mark>: Neurons start in a resting state where the inside of the neuron is negatively charged compared to the outside
   This difference is due to ions, particularly sodium and potassium being unevenly distributed 
   The *resting potential* - this positive-outside/negative-inside state - is typically around -70mV

2. <mark class="ls">Stimulation</mark> : Neural signals can be excitatory, or inhibitory. If the excitatory signals exceed the inhibitory signals by a minimum [[threshold]], the combined signals trigger an action potential.
   If the stimulation is strong enough to reach a threshold (usually around -55mV), the neuron 'fires'
![[threshold|clean no-t]] 
![[all-or-none response|clean no-t]]

3. <mark class="ls">Depolarization</mark> : Sodium channels open, and sodium ions rush into the neuron. The influx of positive ions makes the inside of the neuron more positively charged compared to the outside. 
   This local depolarization causes the charge difference (voltage) to change in the neighboring region
   This triggers sodium channels in the next parts of the axon to open. 
   This process repeats along the length of an axon, in a domino effect

4. <mark class="ls">refractory period</mark> : neurons need a short break
