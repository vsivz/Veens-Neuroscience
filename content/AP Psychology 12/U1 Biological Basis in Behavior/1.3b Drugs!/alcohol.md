Alcohol slows brain activity that controls judgment and inhibitions, causing 3 million yearly deaths

**Slowed Neural Processing**
Alcohol slows [[sympathetic nervous system]] activity
Larger doses cause:
- reactions to slow
- speech to slur
- skilled performance to deteriorate

Alcohol is a potent [[sedative]], especially when paired with sleep deprivation

When drunk, people aren't aware of how drunk they are. Unaware of their intoxication, people may think they can drive safely even when they can't.

**Memory Disruption**
Alcohol can disrupt memory formation, and heavy drinking can have long-term effects on the brain and cognition

In rats, at a period corresponding to human [[Adolescence]], binge drinking contributes to [[neurons|neuron]] death and reduced [[neurogenesis]]

In humans, heavy drinking may lead to blackouts, in which drinkers continue to interact, but are unable to later recall what happened. 
These blackouts result partly from the way alcohol suppresses [[REM sleep]], which helps store the day's experiences into permanent memories

**Reduced Self-Awareness**


>[!experiment]
>those who consumed alcohol (rather than a placebo) were doubly likely to be caught mind-wandering during a reading task, yet were less likely to notice that they zoned out

Alcohol also focuses attention on an immediate arousing situation, and distracts it from normal inhibitions and future consequences

**Expectancy Effects**
Believing we're consuming alcohol ca cause us to act out alcohol's presumed influence

>[!experiment] 
>researchers gave uni men either an alcoholic or a nonalcoholic drink, which tasted identical. After watching an erotic movie clip, the men who *thought* they had consumed alcohol were more likenly to report having strong sexual fantasies and feeling guilt free. 
>Being able to attribute their sexual responses to alcohol released their inhibitions, whether or not they had actually consumed any alcohol.
