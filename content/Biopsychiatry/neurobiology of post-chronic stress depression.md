
Following the end of a period of prolonged [[stress]], a person can fall into a [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]]

Chronic stress and [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]] can lead to prolonged activation of the [[HPA axis]], resulting in the excessive release of [[cortisol]]

Over time, elevated cortisol levels can:
- Damage the [[hippocampus]], a brain region crucial for learning, memory and emotion regulation
- Disrupt the [[Prefrontal cortex]], leading to reduced decision-making and emotional regulation capacity
- Overactivate the [[amygdala]], making the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]] more reactive to negative emotions

When the stress-ends, the dysregulation of the HPA-axis may persist, leaving the brain in a state of imbalance predisposing depression.

###### Dopamine Dysregulation
Cortisol can crucially disrupt [[dopamine]] pathways in several ways:
- Chronic cortisol exposure inhibits the activity of the enzyme [[tyrosine hydroxylase]], which is responsible for the synthesis of dopamine from [[Tyrosine]]; Cortisol also reduces the availability of tyrosine by interfering with its transport across the BBB
- Cortisol increases the transcription of the [[SLC6A3]] gene, which codes for [[DAT]], resulting in more DAT proteins. This leads to greater [[reuptake]] of dopamine, lowering dopamine levels

Stress-induced [[inflammation]] plays a major role in depleting dopamine:
- Chronic stress triggers the [[immune system]] to release pro-inflammatory [[cytokines]], which interfere with the conversion of tyrosine to dopamine
- Stress activates microglial cells (the brain's immune cells), leading to the release of inflammatory molecules. These in particular damage dopamine-producing [[neurons]] in the [[ventral tegmental area]] and [[substantia nigra]]

Reduced efficacy of the reward system:
- Stress increases dopamine as part of the fight-or-flight response
- Over time, constant activation can lead to [[Receptor Biology|receptor]] desensitization
- Chronic stress reduces levels of [[brain-derived neurotrophic factor]], which is essential for maintaining the health and plasticity of dopaminergic neurons

Dysregulation of the Prefrontal Cortex and Nucleus Accumbens Connection:
- The prefrontal cortex, in particular the [[Medial Prefrontal Cortex (outer)]] and [[Orbitofrontal Cortex]] is responsible for Reward evaluation and impulse control
- The [[Nucleus Accumbens]] regulates Motivation and drive, goal-directed behavior & reward processing
- Impairment of the PFC and the disruption of synaptic connections between these regions reduces top-down control of the NAc by the PFC. This leads to the NAc becoming less responsive to pleasurable stimuli, and goal-directed behavior. 

[[epinephrine]] and dopamine competition:
- both epinephrine and dopamine are synthesized from tyrosine
- under chronic stress the body prioritizes the production of epinephrine, diverting resources away from dopamine synthesis

###### Dopamine deficiencies causing depression
The reduced availability of dopamine contributes to several hallmark symptoms of depression:
- [[anhedonia]] - dopamine is critical for the brain's reward system. When dopamine is low, the brain struggles to process pleasurable stimuli, making activities that were once enjoyable feel dull or meaningless
- amotivation - dopamine drives motivation and goal-directed behavior. Reduced dopamine signaling leaves the brain unable to prioritize or pursue goals
- Physical Fatigue - dopamine affects energy regulation; low levels can lead to a persistent state of lethargy and physical exhaustion
