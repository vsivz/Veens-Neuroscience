
Executive Dysfunction refers to impairments or difficulties with [[Executive Functions]], which are higher-order cognitive processes controlled primarily by the [[Prefrontal cortex]]. 

##### Executive Functions
These functions enable a person to:
- Plan and organize tasks
- Initiate or start activities
- Sustain attention and remain focused 
- Manage time and prioritize effectively
- shift attention flexibly
- control impulses and emotions
- use [[working memory]] effectively
- monitor and self-regulate behaviors

##### Symptoms
People experiencing executive dysfunction might exhibit:
- difficulty with [[task initiation]] - procrastination, lack of motivation
- poor organizational skills
- trouble maintaining concentration
- difficulty making decisions
- impulsivity or emotional dysregulation
- challenges with goal-directed behavior or strategic thinking

##### Causes
- [[psychological disorder|psychiatric disorders]]:
	- [[ADHD]]
	- [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]]
	- [[autism spectrum disorder]]
	- [[5.4c Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders|schizophrenia]]
- Neurological Conditions - [[traumatic brain injury]], dementia, stroke, [[Parkinson's Disease]]
- [[substance use disorder|substance abuse]]
- [[chronic stress]] or [[5.4a Trauma-and Stressor-Related Disorders|trauma]]
