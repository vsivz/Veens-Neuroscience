The PFC is also often smaller in volume in ADHD, with some regions like the right inferior frontal gyrus in the [[Ventrolateral Prefrontal Cortex|VLPFC]], responsible for inhibitory control, especially

The PFC uses recurrent excitatory connections to maintain information

Normally alpha-2A receptors help regulate these connections by suppressing "noisy" or irrelevant inputs and enhancing signal quality. These receptors are modulated by  norepinephrine, which stabilize neural firing and improve attention

In ADHD, low levels of norepinephrine reduce receptor activation, leading to weaker, less stable neural connections. 

Dopamine plays a role in maintaining the signal-to-noise ration in the PFC by enhancing relevant signals and suppressing irrelevant distractions. In ADHD reduced dopamine levels lead to increased "noise", making it harder for the brain to focus.
