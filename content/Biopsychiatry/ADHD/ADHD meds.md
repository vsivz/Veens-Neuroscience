### Overview
***
During fight or flight response, the [[Central Nervous System]] responds to stimulation by releasing [[epinephrine]], aka adrenaline.
Leads to things like:
- Faster heart rate
- slowed digestion
- increased alertness
- faster breathing
- higher blood pressure 
- Dilated pupils
- energy

[[Stimulants]] stimulate the central nervous system
nonstimulants do not stimulate the central nervous system

Stimulant medications give body synthetic stimulants that increase the production of norepinephrine and dopamine
Because of this, stimulant medications often have the same side effects as the effects of a fight or flight response

MRI studies suggest that long-term treatment of ADHD with stimulants (specifically Amphetamines & Methylphenidates) decreases abnormalities in [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain structure]] and function found in ADHD. 

The idea that ADHD stimulant meds act as a gateway drug has been discredited by multiple sources, which showed that abuse is statistically very low and stimulant therapy in childhood does not increase the chance of [[substance use disorder|substance]] and [[alcohol use disorder|alcohol use disorders]]
A study found that people treated with stimulants during childhood were *less likely* to have substance use disorders.
