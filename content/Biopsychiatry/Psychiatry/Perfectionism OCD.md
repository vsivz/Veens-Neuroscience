Perfectionism OCD is a subtype of [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]] where an individual is excessively focused on:
- flawlessness
- order
- certainty

Unlike general perfectionism, which can be a personality trait or coping mechanism, perfectionistic OCD is characterized by *intrusive thoughts, compulsions & significant distress when perceived perfection is not met*

###### Intrusive Thoughts (Obsessions)
People with perfectionistic OCD experience *persistent, unwanted thoughts* that create [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]
These thoughts often revolve around:
- Making mistakes
	- Worry that even minor errors will have serious consequences
	- Over-analyzing past actions to determine if a mistake was made
	- Fear of looking incompetent, irresponsible, or unintelligent

- Worrying that something is "off" or "not quite right"
	- pervasive sense that something is off or incomplete, even without a logical reason
	- a compulsion to repeat tasks until they 'feel right' (ex. rewriting notes, arranging objects)
	- frustration with minor imperfections (ex. fonts being slightly misaligned, handwriting looking messy)

- Fear of imperfect outcomes
	- anxiety about submitting "subpar" work, even if it meets objective standards
	- doubting whether something has been done "correctly" or "completely" (ex. checking an email 10 times before sending)
	- hesitancy to start tasks due to the fear that they won't be done perfectly

- Fear of negative judgment
	- Worrying excessively about how others perceive one's work, decisions, or behaviors
	- Overanalyzing social interactions to ensure nothing imperfect was said
	- Seeking reassurance from others to confirm that they haven't made a mistake

- Fear of uncertainty and incompleteness
	- Struggles to make decisions due to doubt about the "right" choice
	- Overthinking even simple choices (ex. which color to use in a document, which word to use in an email)
	- avoiding finishing tasks because the outcomes might not be 100% correct

###### Rituals (Compulsions)
To cope with the anxiety, they engage in repetitive behaviors or mental rituals, such as:

- Excessive Checking & Reviewing
	- Re-reading emails, essays, messages, or reports multiple times to ensure they are perfect
	- constantly checking work for minor errors
	- overanalyzing past conversations for mistakes

- Repeating or Redoing tasks
	- Rewriting, retyping, or redoing work multiple times until it feels "just right"
	- erasing and rewriting notes or documents due to perceived imperfection
	- repeating a phrase, action, or movement until it feels "correct"

- Seeking Reassurance
	- Asking others repeatedly whether something is done correctly 
	- Needing validation from professors, bosses, or friends that their work is good enough
	- Looking for excessive feedback before making a decision

- Avoidance Behaviors
	- Procrastination due to the fear of imperfection
	- Avoiding difficult decisions for fear of making the 'wrong' choice
	- Avoiding new tasks or challenges unless they are certain they will excel

- Arranging & Organizing Rituals
	- Fixating on symmetry, alignment, or neatness
	- Repeatedly adjusting objects or arranging things in a specific order
	- Spending excessive time formatting documents to make them look 'perfect'

###### Intolerance of uncertainty
A core feature of Perfectionistic OCD is intolerance of uncertainty - the persistent discomfort with ambiguity, doubt or the possibility of imperfection
People with this type of OCD typically feel an overwhelming need for certainty, leading to compulsive behaviors. 

People with OCD feel intense distress and wear when faced with 'what if?' scenarios, leading to obsessive overanalysis and compulsions.

Examples:
- Academic or Work Settings: 
	- Obsession: Fear of doing some work incorrectly
	- Compulsion: Endless revising and checking, procrastination due to fear of mistakes
- Social Interactions
	- Obsession: Fear of making a social mistake
	- Compulsion: Replaying conversations in the mind, seeking reassurance from others
- Decision-making:
	- Obsession: Fear of picking the wrong option
	- Compulsion: Over-researching, delaying decisions, constantly changing plans.
- Creative or Personal Projects:
	- Obsession: Fear of project not being good enough
	- Compulsion: Redoing work, but never being satisfied
