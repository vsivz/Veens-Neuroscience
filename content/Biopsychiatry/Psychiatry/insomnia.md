
###### Overview
Insomnia is a common [[sleep]] [[psychological disorder|disorder]]. 
It is defined as the persistent difficulty in falling or staying asleep, or experiencing non-restorative sleep, despite having adequate opportunity and circumstances for sleep. 

###### Types of insomnia:
- **Acute Insomnia** - Short-term insomnia that typically lasts from a few days to a few weeks. It is often triggered by stressors such as a [[Significant Life Changes and Stress|significant life event]] or a period of increased [[stress]]
- **Chronic Insomnia** - Long-lasting insomnia that occurs at least three nights per week for three months or longer. It may be:
	- Primary - Not associated with other health conditions
	- Secondary - Linked to other issues like [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]], [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]], [[chronic pain]], or [[5.5d drug therapies|medication]]

###### Causes
**Primary Insomnia**
- Often idiopathic, meaning no clear cause is identified
- May be linked to hyperarousal 

**Secondary Insomnia**
- Psychological Factors - Stress, anxiety & depression
- Medical Conditions - Chronic pain, respiratory issues, gastrointestinal problems, or neurological conditions
- Medications and Substances - [[stimulants]], certain [[antidepressants]] and [[caffeine]] use, especially close to bed time. 
- [[circadian rhythm]] disorders - conditions like jet lag or shift work disorder can disrupt the natural sleep-waken cycle

###### Consequences
**Daytime impairments**
- Fatigue and low energy
- impaired concentration, memory and decision-making
- Mood disturbances such as irritability, anxiety or depression
- Increased risk of accidents

**Long-term Consequences**
- Lead to or exacerbate chronic health issues, including cardiovascular problems and metabolic disturbances
- Poor sleep can worsen conditions like anxiety, which can then worsen insomnia, creating a vicious cycle
