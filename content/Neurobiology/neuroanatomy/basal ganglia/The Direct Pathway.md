This is one of the major [[Corticostriatal-Thalamocortical Circuit|Corticostriatal-Thalamocortical (CSTC) Circuits]] of the [[basal ganglia]]
It works with [[The Indirect Pathway]] and [[The Hyperdirect Pathway]] to regulate movement, with the Direct Pathway facilitating movement initiation.

###### Anatomical Components
- **[[Cerebral Cortex|cortex]] (Motor & Associative Areas)**
	Excitatory ([[glutamate|glutamatergic]]) projections to the [[striatum]]
	Provides initial command signals for voluntary movement
- **striatum**
	Receives excitatory input from the cortex
	Contains [[GABA|GABAergic]] medium spiny [[neurons]] (MSNs) that project directly to the internal segment of the [[Dorsal Pallidum|globus pallidus]] (GPi) and the [[substantia nigra]] pars reticulata (SNr)
	The [[D1 receptor|D1 dopamine receptor]]-expressing MSNs are involved in the direct pathway
- **Globus Pallidus Internus & Substantia Nigra Pars Reticulata**
	The main output [[nucleus|nuclei]] of the basal ganglia
	under resting conditions, GPi/SNr neurons are tonically active, exerting constant (GABAergic) inhibition of the [[Thalamus]]
- **Thalamus**
	Normally inhibited by the GPi/SNr
	When the inhibition is reduced, the thalamus excites the [[motor cortex]], facilitating movement
- **Motor Cortex**
	Receives excitatory input from the thalamus
	Sends commands to the [[spinal cord]] leading to movement execution

###### Mechanism

**1.** Excitation of the Striatum
- The Motor Cortex sends glutamatergic inputs to the striatum
**2.** Inhibition of the GPi/SNr
- The striatum contains D1-expressing MSNs that send GABAergic signals to the GPi/SNr
- Since GPi/SNr are normally constantly inhibiting the thalamus, this inhibition reduces their activity
**3.** Disinhibition of the Thalamus
- Because the GPi/SNr is inhibited, it releases its suppression on the thalamus
- The Thalamus is now free to send excitatory signals back to the motor cortex
**4.** Excitation of the motor cortex
- The motor cortex receives increased excitatory input from the thalamus, leading to enhanced activation of motor output pathways
- This promotes voluntary movement execution

###### Role of Dopamine
- The SNc releases [[dopamine]] onto the striatum
- Dopamine binds to D1 receptors, which are excitatory, and enhance the activity of the direct pathway
- This leads to stronger inhibition of the GPi/SNr, further disinhibiting the thalamus and enhancing movement.
