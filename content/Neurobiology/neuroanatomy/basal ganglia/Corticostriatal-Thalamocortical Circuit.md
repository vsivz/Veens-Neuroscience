The Corticostriatal-Thalamocortical (CSTC) Circuit is a key neural pathway involves in motor control, cognition & emotional regulation, and is the main way that the [[basal ganglia]] functions.

It also is referred to as the Cortico-Basal Ganglia-Thalamo-cortical (CBGTC) loops. 
The term CSTC is used more often in psychiatric and cognitive neuroscience, which CBGTC is used more often in motor-related research. 

It consists of reciprocal loops connecting the [[Cerebral Cortex|cortex]], [[striatum]], [[Thalamus]], and back to the cortex

Dysfunctions in this circuit have been implicated in several [[psychological disorder|psychiatric disorders]], like [[obsessive-compulsive disorder|OCD]]

It contains three key pathways

###### Direct Pathway (Facilitatory)
Promotes movement and cognitive flexibility

- The cortex excites the striatum
- The striatum inhibits the internal segment of the globus pallidus (GPi) and substantia nigra pars reticulata (SNr)
- This reduces inhibitory output to the thalamus, increasing thalamocortical activity and facilitating movement or cognition

###### Indirect Pathway (Inhibitory)
Suppresses unwanted movements and thoughts

- The cortex excites the striatum
- The striatum inhibits the external segment of the globus pallidus (GPe)
- The GPe normally inhibits the *subthalamic nucleus (STN)* but its inhibition is now reduced
- The STN is now more active, exciting the GPi/SNr
- This increases inhibition of the thalamus, reducing cortical activity and suppressing movement or cognition

###### Hyperdirect Pathway
Provides rapid inhibitory control over motor or cognitive output

- The cortex directly excites the STN
- The STN excites the GPi/SNr
- This increases inhibition of the thalamus, rapidly stopping ongoing motor or cognitive activity.
