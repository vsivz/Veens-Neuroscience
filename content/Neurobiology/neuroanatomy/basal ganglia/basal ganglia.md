---
title: basal ganglia
---

The basal ganglia are a group of deep, [[Cerebral Cortex|subcortical]] [[nucleus|nuclei]] that play a central role not only in the fine-tuning and initiation of voluntary movement, but also in several non-motor functions, such as habit formation, decision-making, and reward processing. 

##### Overview
At a high level, the basal ganglia serve as a modulatory hub  in the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]].
They help 'select' which motor (and non-motor) programs are executed while inhibiting competing actions. 
This selection process is essential for smooth, coordinated movement as well as for aspects of learning and behavior. 
Dysfunction in these circuits is implicated in disorders ranging from [[Parkinson's Disease]] and [[Huntington's Disease]] to [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]] and [[addiction]]

##### Function
**Movement**
The basal ganglia are a key part of the network of brain cells and [[nerves]] that control the body's voluntary movements. 
They can approve or reject movements signals sent by other brain regions, allowing you to control certain muscles without also using other muscles that are nearby.  
If the basal ganglia approve a signal, it continues to the motor pathways. If they don't, they redirect it into an area where other brain cells dampen those signals until they stop. 

**Decision-making**
The basal ganglia processes how you evaluate goals and risks.
It processes signals that effect your emotions and motivation.
Because of this, it plays a key role in leaning and forming habits, planning and carrying out tasks, and more. 

**Reward and addiction**
Because the Basal Ganglia involve processes like emotions, motivation and habits, they also affect how you learn and how you feel in response to things happening around you. 
That includes feeling rewarded about something you do, or feeling the need to avoid something. 
Because of the involvement of these processes, the basal ganglia play a role in addiction. 

##### Anatomy
The Basal ganglia aren't a single structure in the brain. 
They are a group of several structures, found in the center of the brain, atop the [[midbrain]]

- **[[striatum]]**: 
	the main input structure of the basal ganglia 
	receives excitatory ([[glutamate|glutamatergic]]) inputs from the cortex and [[dopamine|dopaminergic]] inputs from the SNc
	- **[[dorsal striatum]]**
		Composed primarily of the [[Caudate Nucleus]] & [[Putamen]]
		primarily involved in motor control and habit learning
		plays a role in decision-making and action selection
		receives inputs from the motor and association cortices
		sends outputs via the *direct, indirect & hyperdirect pathways* to regulate movement
	- **[[ventral striatum]]**
		Composed of the [[Nucleus Accumbens]] & [[Olfactory tubercle]]
		Involved in reward processing, motivation, and goal-directed behavior
		Plays a crucial role in addiction and reinforcement learning
		receives inputs from the [[Limbic System]] and the [[Prefrontal cortex]]
		rends outputs to the *ventral pallidum* and the *ventral tegmental area*
- **[[Pallidum]]**
	the major output structure of the basal ganglia
	exerts inhibitory ([[GABA|GABAergic]]) control over downstream targets to regulate movement and behavior
	- **[[Dorsal Pallidum]]**
		Composed of the *Globus Pallidus Externus (GPe)* (part of the indirect pathway) & the *Globus Pallidus Internus (GPi)* (part of the direct pathway)
		The GPi acts as a major output nucleus, controlling voluntary movement through its projections to the [[Thalamus]]
		The GPe modulates activity in the indirect pathway to refine motor movement
	- **[[Ventral Pallidum]]**
		A critical output structure for the limbic loop of the basal ganglia
		Plays a role in reward processing, motivation and emotion-related motor behavior
		Receives input from the *Ventral Striatum*
		Projects to the *mediodorsal thalamus*, lateral [[hypothalamus]] and *habenula*, influencing decision-making and reward related actions
- **[[Subthalamic Nucleus]] (STN)** 
	Modulates the indirect pathway to suppress unwanted movements
	part of the hyperdirect pathway, helping to inhibit motor output rapidly
- **[[substantia nigra]]** 
	Composed of the *Pars Compacta (SNc)* and *Pars Reticulata (SNr)*
	The SNc releases dopamine to regulate striatal activity
	The SNr functions similarly to the *GPi* as an output structure
- **[[ventral tegmental area]] (VTA)** 
	Provides dopaminergic input to the ventral striatum, modulating reward and motivation
