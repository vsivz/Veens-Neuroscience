This is a [[dopamine receptors|dopamine receptor]], so it gets activated by [[dopamine]]

It belongs to the family of [[D2-like receptors]] and superfamily of [[G-protein-coupled receptors]]

Its highly expressed in the
- [[Basal Ganglia]], where it facilitates movement
- [[Prefrontal cortex]], where it modulates cognitive functions like [[working memory]]
- [[ventral tegmental area]] and [[Nucleus Accumbens]], where it plays a crucial role in reward and reinforcement mechanisms

###### Isoforms
- D$\mathsf{_{2}}$S (Short isoform): predominantly located on dopamine neuron terminals and somatodendritic regions, these function as autoreceptors
- D$\mathsf{_{2}}$L (Long isoform): more commonly found on postsynaptic neurons, and is involved in modulating the excitability of downstream neurons in motor and reward circuits

###### Dopamine Regulation
- By functioning as autoreceptors, when activated they decrease neuronal firing and limit further dopamine release. 
- This helps to maintain appropriate extracellular dopamine levels

- D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptor activation influences synaptic strength
- By modulating neuronal excitability, often via inhibitory actions that lower the likelihood of [[action potential]] generation, D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors play a role in synaptic plasticity that underpins learning and adaptation

- The tight control of dopamine release via autoreceptors ensures that target regions receive signals within a physiologically optimal range

###### Motor Function
- In the [[striatum]], postsynaptic D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors are mainly expressed on medium spiny [[neurons]] of [[The Indirect Pathway]]
- Their activation increases the inhibitory output of these MSNs, contributing to the suppression of competing or unwanted motor programs
- The balance between D$\mathsf{_{1}}$-mediated facilitation and D$\mathsf{_{2}}$-mediated inhibition is crucial for smooth, coordinated movement

- By influencing the excitability of neurons within [[basal ganglia]] circuits, D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors help integrate signals from various regions to help determine when and how motor responses are executed

###### Reward
- D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors contribute to reward processing by modulating the response of key reward circuits, including the [[Nucleus Accumbens]]
- They help assign motivational value to [[stimulus|stimuli]] and are involved in reinforcement learning by assigning incentive salience to these stimuli
- dopamine acting through D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors does not simply convey pleasure, but rather influences the drive to seek out rewarding stimuli. While hedonic impact ("liking") of rewards is partly mediated by other systems (such as [[opioids|opioid]] circuits), dopamine, and particularly signaling through D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors, is crucial for the wanting aspect. 
- When activated D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ receptors can influence how strongly a cue is imbued with an incentive value - i.e. dopamine binding to them adjusts the desirability of a stimulus, thus affecting the drive to pursue it

- Their balanced activity facilitates the ability to switch between behavioral strategies, which is particularly important in tasks requiring cognitive flexibility
- Their activity encodes negative feedback, and the idea of reward prediction error
- Dysregulation here is implicated in conditions like [[addiction]] and [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]]

- D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ Receptors induce [[long-term depression]] at [[glutamate|glutamatergic]] [[synapse|synapses]], which is crucial for unlearning connections that do not consistently predict reward
