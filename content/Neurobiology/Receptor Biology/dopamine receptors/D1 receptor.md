
This is a [[dopamine receptors|dopamine receptor]], so it gets activated by [[dopamine]]

 They are [[G-protein-coupled receptors|GPCRs]] of the family of [[D1-like receptors]]

It is the most abundant kind of dopamine receptor in the [[Central Nervous System]]

###### Motor Control
*In the Direct Pathway*
- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ Receptors are primarily expressed on medium spiny [[neurons]] (MSNs) in the [[striatum]] that constitute the [[The Direct Pathway|Direct Pathway]] of the [[basal ganglia]]
- Dopamine from the *[[substantia nigra]] pars compacta* (SNc) activates D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors on these MSNs
- [[Cerebral Cortex|cortical]] motor commands go to the striatum, where D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ activation enhances the excitability of these MSNs. 
- These MSNs release [[GABA]] onto neurons in the *[[Dorsal Pallidum|Globus Pallidus]] internal segment* (GPi) and *Substantia Nigra Pars Reticulata* (SNr), inhibiting them
- Normally neurons in the GPi & SNr constantly fire inhibitory signals to suppress the [[Thalamus]], and when they are inhibited, the Thalamus is disinhibited
- The thalamus sends excitatory signals back to the [[motor cortex]], promoting movement initiation

*Neural Dynamics*
- Electrophysiological studies have shown that D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptor activation not only increases the firing rates of MSNs, but also modulates the pattern and synchronization of neuronal activity
- This coordinated activity ensures that only the appropriate motor commands are amplified, while competing signals are suppressed
- This balance is essential for smooth, goal-directed movements

*Modulation of D$\mathsf{_{2}}$ Receptors* 
- MSNs of the direct (D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors) and [[The Indirect Pathway|Indirect Pathway]] ([[D2 receptor|D2 receptors]]) inhibit each other
- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ activation makes direct pathway MSNs more excitable, while the MSNs of the indirect pathways remain suppressed
- This promotes a "winner-takes-all" mechanism: If the direct pathway MSNs fire strongly they inhibit indirect pathway MSNs, ensuring that movement-facilitating signals remain dominant
- This prevents the indirect pathway from counteracting direct pathway activation
- This enhances the contrast between 'selected' and 'rejected' motor commands

###### Cognition
*Working Memory*
- In the [[Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex]], a region critical for [[working memory]] and [[Executive Functions]], D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors modulate the activity of pyramidal neurons and [[interneurons]]
- These neurons remain persistently active during working memory tasks, thought to be responsible for 'holding information online'
- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptor activation in prefrontal pyramidal neurons enhances the stability of these neurons by:
	- increasing neuronal excitability at moderate dopamine levels
	- suppressing excessive background noise 
	- strengthening [[synapse|synaptic]] connections between neurons that are co-active during working memory tasks

*Optimal Dopamine Levels*
- Low Dopamine Levels:
	- prefrontal neurons do not receive enough modulation, leading to weak and unreliable working memory signals
	- task-relevant neurons may be drowned out by distracting signals
- Optimal Dopamine Levels
	- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors fine-tune excitability, enhancing the stability of persistent firing without excessive inhibition
- High Dopamine Levels
	- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors over-activate inhibitory processes
	- This leads to suppression of both relevant and irrelevant signals, causing working memory deficits
	- This is seen in [[stress]]-related cognitive impairments, where high dopamine surges suppress prefrontal activity

###### Reward
- Within reward circuits, most notably in the [[Nucleus Accumbens]], D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptors are integral to processing motivational signals
- Neurons in these regions respond to rewarding stimuli by altering their firing patterns in a manner that promotes the behavior and reinforcement learning
- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ activation increases the excitability of these neurons

- D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ receptor-mediated signaling in [[Limbic System|Limbic circuits]] allows the integration of contextual and sensory information with motivational states
- This integration is essential to adaptive behavior, enhancing responsiveness to cues that predict rewards
- This also reinforces the memory of rewarding experiences

- enhanced excitability from D$\mathsf{_{1}}$ activation facilitates [[long-term potentiation]] at [[glutamate|glutamatergic]] [[synapse|synapses]] - which is essential for the consolidation of reward-related learning
