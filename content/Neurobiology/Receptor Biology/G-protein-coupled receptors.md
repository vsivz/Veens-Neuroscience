These are the largest class of [[Receptor Biology|receptors]] in the human [[genome]]
They are major drug targets. Around 30-40% of all approved drugs act on GPCRs to modulate their activity

##### Mechanism
A GPCR sits on the surface of a cell, waiting for a signal like a [[hormones|hormone]] or [[neurotransmitter]]
When the signal binds, the GPCR changes shape, turning it 'on'

The GPCR activates a helper protein inside the cell called a G-protein. This G-protein breaks into two parts, which go off to pass the signal to other parts of the cell. 

These G-protein parts tell other molecules, like enzymes or ion channels to do something, such as making the cell produce energy.
