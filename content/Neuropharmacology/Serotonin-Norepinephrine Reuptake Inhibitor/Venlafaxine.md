Venlafaxine is an [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|SNRI]] primarily used to treat [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] and [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]
###### Uses
The IR form is only approved to treat [[major depressive disorder]]
the XR form is also approved to treat:  
- [[Generalized Anxiety Disorder]]
- [[social anxiety disorder]]
- [[Panic Disorder]]

Studies have also shown it to be useful for [[posttraumatic stress disorder|PTSD]]
Some studies have suggested that it may have some efficacy for [[ADHD]] and [[obsessive-compulsive disorder|OCD]]

It is also used as a treatment to reduce episodes of cataplexy in patients with [[narcolepsy]]

It has not been officially approved to treat pediatric patients, but it is still prescribed if the possible benefits outweigh risks. 

Studies have shown that its efficacy in treating depression is not influenced by the severity of depression at the start of the treatment
###### Administration + related
It is sold under brand name Effexor

It has a half life of 4 hours, so it requires twice-daily dosing, or an extended-release formulation.

The recommended starting dose for Venlafaxine XR is 37.5 mg/day, to be titrated up to a recommended maintenance dose of 150-225 mg/day
Higher doses (ex. 225 or 375 mg/day)  are more effective than lower doses (ex. 75 mg/day) but also cause more side effects

###### Mechanism and Neuropharmacology
Like other SNRIs, it increases [[serotonin]] and [[norepinephrine]] levels by blocking [[SERT]] and [[NET]]

It has its highest affinity for SERT, so at lower doses like 75 mg/day, it functions primarily as an SSRI, with its noradrenergic affects appearing at higher doses, like 150-225 mg/day.

At very high doses, it also weakly inhibits [[DAT]], increasing [[dopamine]] levels.

It is metabolized by [[CYP2D6]] into [[Desvenlafaxine]]. The amount that gets metabolized into Desvenlafaxine is dependent on the extensivity of a person's CYP2D6 enzyme. Both drugs will enter the brain and contribute effects. 
Desvenlafaxine is similarly potent to Venlafaxine, so the differences in metabolism between extensive and poor metabolisers will not change the efficacy, although poor metabolisers tend to have more severe side effects, suggesting that Venlafaxine has more side effects. 

Venlafaxine is a substrate of [[P-glycoprotein]] which pumps it out of the brain.

###### Side Effects
The most Common side effects are: (Percentages listed are for Effexor XR, which is more commonly prescribed. The IR variant usually has higher rates for each.)
- Nausea (30%)
- [[insomnia]] (18%)
- Dizziness (16%)
- Dry mouth (15%)
- Drowsiness (15%)

Other common side effects ($\leq 10\%$) include:
- loss of appetite
- constipation
- sweating
- hypertension
- sexual problems

Severe side-effects include:
increased risk of [[Serotonin Syndrome]]
Venlafaxine can increase eye pressure, so those with glaucoma may require more frequent eye checks. 
Compared to other [[antidepressants]], the increased risk for suicidal thinking in children is greater on Venlafaxine
For patients with a [[5.4b Bipolar Disorders|bipolar disorder]], Venlafaxine appears more than [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]] and [[Bupriopon]] to induce [[mania]]

Venlafaxine has a higher rate of moderate to severe withdrawal symptoms relative to other antidepressants, which may be due to the short half-life of venlafaxine and its active metabolite.
