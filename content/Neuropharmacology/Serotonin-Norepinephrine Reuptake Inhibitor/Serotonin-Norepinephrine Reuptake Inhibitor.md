SNRIs are a class of [[antidepressants|antidepressant]]s that increase levels of [[serotonin]] and [[norepinephrine]] in the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]]. They bind to [[SERT]] and [[NET]], to block the [[reuptake]] of these [[neurotransmitter]]s. 

Compared to [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]], SNRIs are more targeted towards [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] than [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]], as the norepinephrine can boost the low energy and lack of motivation from depression, while it may increase physical symptoms of anxiety, which SSRIs would be less likely to cause. 

They are preferred for conditions involving chronic pain due to norepinephrine's role in pain modulation. 

SNRIs include:
- [[Venlafaxine]]
- [[Desvenlafaxine]]
- [[Duloxetine]]
