Desvenlafaxine is an [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|SNRI]]

It is primarily used to treat [[major depressive disorder]]

###### Administration & Related
It has a half-life of 11 hours.

It's recommended starting and maintenance dose is 50 mg/day
Studies have shown that doses of 50 to 400 mg/day appear effective for MDD, although no additional benefit was demonstrated at doses greater than 50 mg/day, and adverse effects and discontinuation was more frequent at higher doses. 

It is available in 25, 50 & 100mg extended-release oral tablets

It is sold under brand name Pristiq

While it is only approved for MDD, it is used off-label for other conditions, including [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety disorders]]
###### Mechanism and Neurobiology
Like other SNRIs, it increases [[serotonin]] and [[norepinephrine]] by blocking [[SERT]] and [[NET]]

It is a synthetic form of the isolated major active metabolite of [[Venlafaxine]]
When [[CYP2D6]] normal metabolisers take venlafaxine, about 70% of the drug is converted to Desvenlafaxine, so the effects tend to be similar. 

Desvenlafaxine is less selective in blocking SERT relative to NET, compared to Venlafaxine.
Desvenlafaxine is ~10x more selective for SERT than NET, compared to ~30x selectivity of Venlafaxine.

###### Side Effects
Desvenlafaxine tends to have less side effects than Venlafaxine

The most common side effects are:
- Nausea (22%)
- Dizziness (13%)
- Dry Mouth (11%)

Other common side effects include:
- Drowsiness
- [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]
- increased sweating
- decreased appetite
- constipation
- [[insomnia]]
- sexual side effects

Serious side effects include:
- [[Serotonin Syndrome]]
- Increased [[suicide|suicidal]] thoughts, especially in those under 25
- Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
- Glaucoma; Desvenlafaxine may increase eye pressure, causing or worsening narrow-angle glaucoma, which can lead to blindness. If eye pain is noticed, eye pressure should be tested. 
- Seizures
- Low Sodium Level (Hyponatremia)
