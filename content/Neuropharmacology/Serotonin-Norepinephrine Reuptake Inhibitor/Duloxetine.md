Duloxetine is an [[antidepressants|antidepressant]] of the [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|SNRI]] class
###### Uses
It's main uses are in:
- [[major depressive disorder]]
- [[Generalized Anxiety Disorder]]
- neuropathic pain, chronic musculoskeletal pain, fibromyalgia

Studies have shown Duloxetine to have less efficacy than [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]] and newer antidepressants in treating MDD
Studies have also shown Duloxetine to have more side effects and less tolerability than other antidepressants

Duloxetine is effective in treating GAD. 
A review from the Annals of Internal Medicine lists it among first line drug treatments for GAD.

###### Administration + related
It is primarily sold under brand name Cymbalta
It comes in 20, 30, 40 & 60 mg oral delayed-release capsules

###### Mechanism and Neurobiology
Duloxetine causes increased [[serotonin]] and [[norepinephrine]], by blocking [[SERT]] and [[NET]]
Some studies indicate that Duloxetine was 3 times as potent at inhibiting serotonin reuptake than norepinephrine reuptake.

It is metabolized by [[CYP2D6]] and [[CYP1A2]]

###### Side Effects
Common side effects include:
- Nausea
- Dry Mouth
- Dizziness
- Drowsiness 
- Constipation
- Appetite suppression
- Sweating
- Sexual dysfunction

Serious side effects include
- [[suicide|suicidal]] thoughts, especially in those under 25
- serotonin syndrome
- liver damage
- orthostatic hypotension (low blood pressure upon standing)
- hypertension
- abnormal bleeding
- low sodium levels (hyponatremia)

Side effects tended to be mild-to-moderate, and tended to decrease in intensity over time
Sexual dysfunction was more pronounced in men, and rates were similar to [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]]
The exact mechanism behind increased sweating is not fully known. One possible reason could be the effect on the [[sympathetic nervous system]]. Increased norepinephrine levels increase perspiration. In addition, the amount of sweating is influenced by the noradrenergic tone, which is determined by interactions between noradrenergic and serotonergic neurons, so Duloxetine may increase this due to its action on both neurotransmitters.
