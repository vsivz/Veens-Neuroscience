
Diphenhydramine is a first generation [[H1-Antihistamines|H$\mathsf{_1}$-Antihistamine]], commonly used off-label for insomnia due to its sedative properties. 

### Pharmacodynamics

###### Histamine
Diphenhydramine is an [[antagonist]] at [[histamine]] [[H1 receptors|H$\mathsf{_1}$ receptors]] in the [[Central Nervous System]], although in practice it tends to act as an [[inverse agonist]].
Histamine release from the [[tuberomammillary nucleus|TMN]] stimulates wakefulness and arousal through widespread stimulation in [[Cerebral Cortex|cortical]] and subcortical regions.
By blocking [[H1 receptors|H$\mathsf{_1}$ receptors]], DPH prevents histamine from binding, resulting in decreased cortical arousal, sedation and sleep promotion.

###### Acetylcholine
DPH also exhibits [[Anticholinergics|Anticholinergic]] activity, blocking [[mAChRs]], particularly in the [[Cerebral Cortex|cortex]], [[hippocampus]], [[forebrain|basal forebrain]], and [[Reticular Formation|Reticular Activating System]].

[[acetylcholine]], is essential for maintaining cortical arousal and [[REM sleep]]
Blocking muscarinic receptors disrupts cholinergic signaling, leading to:
- Reduced cortical arousal
- Increased sleep latency reduction (falling asleep faster)
- Decreased REM sleep duration

This anticholinergic property significantly contributes to sedation, but may also lead to undesirable cognitive side effects. 
###### Other
Diphenhydramine has also been shown to have a very mild inhibition of [[SERT]], leading to small increases in [[serotonin]].
By reducing histamine levels, DPH also indirectly reduces [[dopamine]] levels. 


### Pharmacokinetics
Peak plasma concentration occurs ~2-3 hours after administration.
