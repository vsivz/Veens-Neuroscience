###### Reasoning for Lisdexamfetamine
Especially after doubling the dose to 10 mg/day, Fluoxetine began to induce a depressive episode in me. 
However, I quickly noticed that this would only appear when my Dextroamphetamine Medication had worn off. 
During winter break, because I was awake for much less time every day, this was not significantly affecting me except late at night. By the time my Dextroamphetamine SR had worn off, I would take my Dextroamphetamine IR booster, and it was only when that wore off that I would become depressed. 
But when school started, I started waking up at 7am again, and was awake for longer because of school. Because of this, I would have periods where I was severely depressed, including during the later hours of school, and later at night. 

I reasoned that this depressive episode occurred due to serotonergic suppression of dopamine. This happens due to the 5-HT$\mathsf{_{2C}}$ receptor, which when activated by Serotonin inhibits dopamine release, especially in the reward centers, including the ventral tegmental area and the striatum, which has the highest concentrations of this receptor. My genes indicate higher levels of this receptor. (See genes at bottom)

I had previously concluded that I likely had low baseline dopamine levels, especially in my reward centers. I came to this conclusion because:
- Stronger working memory and analytical skills indicated that my Prefrontal cortex was likely less affected
- Significant issues with motivation, and also issues with motor movements, indicated that the issue lay in my basal ganglia
- Dextroamphetamine was particularly effective compared to other ADHD meds. This is because Dextroamphetamine is very dopaminergic. Compared to Methylphenidates, like Concerta (which didn't work as well for me), are more reliant on baseline levels, so since mine was low I likely saw lower efficacy. Atomoxetine didn't work at all, because it only affected norepinephrine. 
This was later confirmed by genetic testing. This indicated that while I had higher dopaminergic activity in my prefrontal cortex, my overall dopamine levels, and the function of my dopamine receptors, was low. (See genes at bottom of document)

Given that I already had low baseline dopamine, especially in my reward centers, Serotonergic suppression of dopamine through 5-HT$\mathsf{_{2C}}$ receptors, which I had a higher density of, would be easily able to induce depression. 

While I am on Dextroamphetamine, the significant increase in dopamine offset the suppression by Fluoxetine, which is why I wasn't affected during its effects. However, without it, I became depressed. I also noticed that the depressive effects started ~1 hour after the cognitive effects from Dextroamphetamine ended, indicating that the amount of Dextroamphetamine needed to stop depression was less than what I needed for cognitive benefits. 
So Lisdexamfetamine was the solution. Since it is a prodrug of dextroamphetamine, it is essentially no different to Dextroamphetamine than Dextroamphetamine SR is to Dextroamphetamine IR. So taking some lisdexamfetamine in the early afternoon would result in antidepressant effects that would stop my depression in the later hours of school, and also later at night. 20mg of Lisdexamfetamine contains <6 mg of base dextroamphetamine, compared to my current Dextroamphetamine Sulfate, for which the 15 mg ER and 10 mg IR contain 11 mg and 7.3 mg of base dextroamphetamine respectively. 
So I consulted Dr. Birmingham, who specializes in Psychiatric Neuroscience, so he had the knowledge required to judge this request, and he made the prescription, which worked very successfully. 


###### Reasoning for Vortioxetine
SSRIs are known to take significant time to take effect. 
One of the main reasons for this is the presynaptic 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ serotonin autoreceptor, which when activated by the increased Serotonin from the SSRIs inhibits further Serotonin release as a regulating mechanism. It is only after this receptor desensitizes that therapeutic effects can be seen. 
However, my genes indicate that this receptor is over-expressed for me. This meant that for me, SSRIs would take even longer to show effect.

However, it was shown that agonizing this receptor significantly speeds up the desensitization of the presynaptic receptor. (Crucially, only the presynaptic receptor, which is the autoreceptor. This desensitization was not observed in the postsynaptic receptor, which is not an autoreceptor. This is also why antagonism would not work well, as the benefit from blocking the presynaptic receptor would be lost from blocking the postsynaptic receptor.)

That is one of the reasons why it is so beneficial that, in addition to inhibiting SERT like a regular SSRI, Vortioxetine has these multimodal effects, including at the 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptor. By agonizing this receptor, Vortioxetine speeds up the desensitization.  

Furthermore traditional SSRIs will be less effective for me, as my genes indicate less SERT expression, which mean that the Serotonin deficiency that we are assuming is causing my anxiety is not solely responsible to SERT, and that SSRIs will not be as effective as there will be less SERT for them to inhibit. Vortioxetine has shown antidepressant effects at lower SERT occupancy than traditional SSRIs, leading to the scientific consensus that its multimodal receptor activities are indeed partially responsible for its benefits. This is believed to be primarily through antagonism of 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptor. These receptors typically excite GABAergic interneurons which inhibit pyramidal neurons. Antagonism disinhibits these pyramidal neurons, allowing them to better stimulate Serotonergic neurons to release more Serotonin.

Vortioxetine also has other benefits. Through its complex multimodal activities, it has been shown to have a positive effect on neurotransmitters like norepinephrine and dopamine, and boosts cognitive function and neuroplasticity. In addition, by antagonizing the 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptor, it reduces the side effect profile that most SSRIs are associated with. 

This was prescribed to me alongside the Lisdexamfetamine a week into January. 
While it is harder to gauge the benefit accurately due to constant significant environmental changes, it does seem to have had a positive effect on my anxiety.

###### Genes
**Serotonin Autoreceptor:** *HTR1A*
The most important variation in this is the *rs6295 polymorphism*, which is a very well-studied polymorphism, where a G instead of a C means higher activity. Also the *rs10042486 polymorphism* which is less significant, but studies have shown that a T instead of a C has an increase in receptor activity. 
I have two G-alleles for the first polymorphism, and two T-alleles for the second, which are the worst possible results, as they mean that I will take significantly longer than normal to adjust to SSRIs, as it will take a long time to desensitize these receptors to the point where Serotonin levels may increase significantly. 

**Serotonin Transporter:** *SLC6A4*
This gene has the S/S ('short') alleles. This means that there is overall less SERT activity. Traditional SSRIs function primarily by inhibiting SERT (which is responsible for the reuptake of Serotonin). Given that I have low SERT activity, the Serotonin deficiency that we are assuming causes my anxiety is likely neither primarily caused by nor easily fixed through the inhibition of SERT. Since Vortioxetine increases Serotonin through other mechanisms in addition to SERT inhibition, it is likely that it will have better efficacy than traditional SSRIs. 

**Prefrontal Cortex Dopamine**: *COMT*
My A/A *rs4680* polymorphism results in 3-4 times less COMT activity. This results in higher dopamine in the Prefrontal cortex specifically, where COMT is a primary mechanism for dopamine 
clearance. While this typically allows for strong cognitive performance, this performance is highly vulnerable to stress, as stress can push neurotransmitter levels in the PFC past an optimal level.

**Basal Neurotransmitter Levels**:
*MAOA*
For the MAOA gene there are three polymorphism of interest. *rs1137070*, *rs6323*, & *rs1465107*. The T, G & A alleles respectively for these polymorphisms are associated with higher activity of the MAO-A enzyme, which is responsible for the breakdown of Catecholamines, especially norepinephrine & serotonin, and also dopamine. I have the T/T, G/G & A/A genotypes for these polymorphisms respectively, resulting in very high MAO-A activity, and therefore much lower levels of these key neurotransmitters. 
*Result: much lower Serotonin, Norepinephrine & Dopamine*

*MAOB*
The MAOB gene encodes the MAO-B enzyme, which is especially responsible for dopamine breakdown specifically. My A/A for the *rs1799836* polymorphism is associated with higher activity, further reducing dopamine levels. 
*Result: lower Dopamine*

*DBH*
This gene encodes the dopamine beta-hydroxylase enzyme, which synthesizes dopamine into norepinephrine. My C/C for the *rs1611115* polymorphism is associated with higher activity, so higher conversion of dopamine into norepinephrine, which results in higher norepinephrine, but at the cost of lower dopamine.  
*Result: higher norepinephrine but lower dopamine*

**Dopamine Receptor:** *DRD2*
This gene encodes the D$\mathsf{_2}$ Dopamine Receptor. For the three polymorphisms of interest, *rs1800497*, *rs1799732* & *rs1076560*, my genotypes of A/G, G/del, A/C suggest a decrease in receptor density and signaling. This worsens the effect of my naturally low dopamine, as the little dopamine I have is less able to activate these receptors well.
