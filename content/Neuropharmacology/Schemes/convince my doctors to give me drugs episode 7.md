
> [!genes|float-right-small] ### My relevant Genes
> **Serotonin Receptors:** 
> *[[HTR1A]]*
> The most important variation in this is the *rs6295 polymorphism*, which is a very well-studied polymorphism, where a G instead of a C means higher activity. Also the *rs10042486 polymorphism* which is less significant, but studies have shown that a T instead of a C has an increase in receptor activity. 
> I have two G-alleles for the first polymorphism, and two T-alleles for the second, which are the worst possible results, as they mean that I will take significantly longer than normal to adjust to SSRIs, as it will take a long time to desensitize these receptors to the point where Serotonin levels may increase significantly.
> 
> *[[HTR2C]]*
> My T/T for the *rs3813929* and G/G for the *rs1414334* indicate higher 5-HT$\mathsf{_{2C}}$ expression. 
> This would lead to higher Serotonergic suppression of dopamine
> 
> **Serotonin Transporter:** *[[SLC6A4]]*
> This gene has the S/S ('short') alleles. This means that there is overall less [[SERT]] activity. Traditional SSRIs function primarily by inhibiting SERT (which is responsible for the [[reuptake]] of Serotonin). Given that I have low SERT activity, the Serotonin deficiency that we are assuming causes my anxiety is likely neither primarily caused by nor easily fixed through the inhibition of SERT. Since Vortioxetine increases Serotonin through other mechanisms in addition to SERT inhibition, it is likely that it will have better efficacy than traditional SSRIs. 
> 
> **Prefrontal Cortex Dopamine**: *[[COMT gene|COMT]]*
> My A/A *rs4680* polymorphism results in 3-4 times less [[COMT]] activity. This results in higher dopamine in the Prefrontal cortex specifically, where COMT is a primary mechanism for dopamine 
> clearance. While this typically allows for strong cognitive performance, this performance is highly vulnerable to stress, as stress can push neurotransmitter levels in the PFC past an optimal level.
> 
> **Basal Neurotransmitter Levels**:
> *[[MAOA]]*
> For the MAOA gene there are three polymorphism of interest. *rs1137070*, *rs6323*, & *rs1465107*. The T, G & A alleles respectively for these polymorphisms are associated with higher activity of the [[MAO-A]] enzyme, which is responsible for the breakdown of [[Catecholamine|Catecholamines]], especially norepinephrine & serotonin, and also dopamine. I have the T/T, G/G & A/A genotypes for these polymorphisms respectively, resulting in very high MAO-A activity, and therefore much lower levels of these key neurotransmitters. 
> *Result: much lower Serotonin, Norepinephrine & Dopamine*
> 
> *[[MAOB]]*
> The MAOB gene encodes the [[MAO-B]] enzyme, which is especially responsible for dopamine breakdown specifically. My A/A for the *rs1799836* polymorphism is associated with higher activity, further reducing dopamine levels. 
> *Result: lower Dopamine*
> 
> *[[DBH]]*
> This gene encodes the [[dopamine beta-hydroxylase]] enzyme, which synthesizes dopamine into norepinephrine. My C/C for the *rs1611115* polymorphism is associated with higher activity, so higher conversion of dopamine into norepinephrine, which results in higher norepinephrine, but at the cost of lower dopamine.  
> *Result: higher norepinephrine but lower dopamine*
> 
> **Dopamine Receptor:** *[[DRD2]]*
> This gene encodes the [[D2 receptor| D$\mathsf{_2}$ Dopamine Receptor]]. For the three polymorphisms of interest, *rs1800497*, *rs1799732* & *rs1076560*, my genotypes of A/G, G/del, A/C suggest a decrease in receptor density and signaling. This worsens the effect of my naturally low dopamine, as the little dopamine I have is less able to activate these receptors well.
