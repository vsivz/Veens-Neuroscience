
Currently my motor skill impairments have been causing significant distress and dysfunction in my life, because it's severely hurting my ability to do well in musical theatre, so I have a solution.

The exact polymorphisms for the genes mentioned and their effects can all be found at the bottom of the document. 
### Natural dopamine levels
I am fairly certain that I have naturally low [[dopamine]] levels. 
This is why the very dopaminergic [[Dextroamphetamine]] medications have been very effective in treating my [[ADHD]] symptoms. 

This is also suggested genetically. My genes indicate higher [[MAO-A]] activity, leading to increased dopamine breakdown, and higher [[dopamine beta-hydroxylase|DBH]] activity, leading to higher dopamine synthesis into [[norepinephrine]].
###### Location
My dopamine levels are particularly low in areas like my [[basal ganglia]] rather than in my [[Prefrontal cortex]]
This is why my [[working memory]] and other analytical functions have been shown to be very strong, which is not usually seen in ADHD, while I still struggle with issues like motivation. 
This is shown genetically. My genes (see below) indicate lower [[COMT]] activity, which would lead to less dopamine breakdown in the PFC specifically, where [[DAT]] is not very expressed, so COMT is the primary mechanism for dopamine breakdown, and lower COMT activity offsets my other dopamine deficiencies. 

###### Motivation
Motivation has always been a significant struggle for me. 
This caused the procrastination issues that were my biggest problem up until last year, where I managed to fix it. 
But I still feel significantly decreased motivation, particularly when it comes to starting work, even though its easier to continue once it has been initiated. 
Furthermore, this lack of motivation extends to enjoyable activities too. Recently, I got an infection which made me very sick, and I was feeling very anhedonic. I had little 'wanting' to do anything, but when I forced myself to just watch TV, I found it to be enjoyable. 
This could arise from dysfunction with my [[D2 receptor|D2 receptors]], which are critical in the "wanting" of tasks, as opposed to "liking" tasks which also involve a variety of other mechanisms. 

###### Motor Control
I have always had significant difficulty with motor skills throughout my life. 
I was tested for Dyspraxia, which was ruled out, but my motor skills were impaired enough that it was something that a doctor recommended assessing
Both my gross and fine motor skills are significantly impaired. 
When tested in the fifth grade, my fine motor skills were in the bottom 5th percentile for my age. 

Currently, this has led to caused significant distress and dysfunction in my regular life because of how it affects me in my school's drama program. We are doing musical theatre, which involves both dancing and singing, and I have significant difficulty with both, and perform significantly worse than everyone else. 
With my singing, I have difficulty with pitch, so my singing instructor asked me to do a pitch recognition test, but I scored 10/10, indicating that my pitch difficulty does not arise from an issue with recognition. This suggests that the issue is with the fine motor skills required to produce notes accurately. 

Furthermore, I have even more difficulty with dancing. 
While my working memory is usually strong, I struggle to hold even simple sequences in my working memory, and struggle with quickly accessing them. 
I also have significant struggle with rhythm & tempo, and matching my steps to a beat. 
My movements significantly lack fluidity and smoothness. 

I have significant reason to believe that this is, at least in part, due to a dopamine deficiency. 
Normally, drama class starts around 2 PM, which is around the time when my morning dextroamphetamine medication wears off. 
However, recently there was a time where we had a late performance, so I happened to take my afternoon dextroamphetamine IR booster an hour before, and I noticed that I had significant improvement from my usual performance.

This is also suggested genetically, due to lower density of D2 receptors, which are particularly important in motor functions, especially in ensuring smoothness and fluidity of movement and rhythm and tempo. 

### Pharmaceutical Solution
I believe that this could be targeted pharmaceutically. 
[[dopamine agonists]] seem like they could be a good solution. 
[[Pramipexole]], which has affinity for [[D3 Receptor|D3]] and D2 receptors especially, would likely be the best for alleviating my motor issues. 
It would be better than [[Ropinirole]], which has more side effects, and is metabolised by [[CYP1A2]] which would be suppressed by [[caffeine]], which I consume frequently, leading to interactions. 
While this is predominantly prescribed for [[Parkinson's Disease]] and RLS, I suspect my motor difficulties arise from similar mechanisms, and can have similar solutions, to Parkinson's. 

Other solutions could be more [[Dextroamphetamine]] IR, which has been shown to have efficacy. However, there are some issues. Given that the issue is largely dopaminergic, the noradrenergic effects of Dextroamphetamine may be less helpful, as it may contribute to an increase in anxiety. Dopamine agonists would be preferred, as their impact would just be dopaminergic. 

Given that my motor skill impairments only cause significant dysfunction during drama which is when motor skills are important, an instant-release formulation would be preferable so that the main effects would only be seen during drama hours, and would result in minimal interactions with my other medications in other parts of the day. 

Given that these issues do not arise from Parkinson's, but instead a mechanism that may be similarly targeted, the dosage needed would likely be smaller, and would involve once-daily dosing rather than the usual TID. 

Pramipexole may also have other benefits, as it has been shown to contribute possible [[antidepressants|antidepressant]] effects. This may lead to improvements in the underlying mood disorder that the EEG indicates I have. 

### Genetic Bases
G/G *rs6323* in MAO-A means high MAO-A activity, lowering dopamine and especially serotonin. 
C/C *rs1611115* for DBH indicates higher dopamine synthesis into norepinephrine, lowering dopamine further. 
G/G *rs10770141* for TH suggests decreased production of the [[tyrosine hydroxylase]] enzyme, leading to decreased synthesis of *L-DOPA*, which is the rate-limiting step in the synthesis of catecholamines, including dopamine. 

My A/A *rs4680* polymorphism results in 3-4 times less COMT activity. This results in higher dopamine specifically in the Prefrontal Cortex, where COMT is a primary mechanism for dopamine clearance. While this typically allows for strong cognitive performance, this performance is highly vulnerable to stress, as stress can push neurotransmitter levels in the PFC past an optimal level.

A/G *rs1800497* and G/- *rs1799732* for DRD2 suggests a moderate reduction in D2 receptor density, reducing dopamine effectiveness.
