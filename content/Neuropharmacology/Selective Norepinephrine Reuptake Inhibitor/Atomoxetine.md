*Atomoxetine* is a [[Selective Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor]], used primarily to treat [[ADHD]]

###### Usage
Atomoxetine is used for:
- [[ADHD]]
- Cognitive Disengagement Syndrome (CDS)
- Traumatic Brain Injury

For ADHD, Meta-analyses & Systemic Reviews have found that atomoxetine has comparable efficacy and equal tolerability to [[Methylphenidate]] in children and adolescents, and equal efficacy and tolerability in adults
While its efficacy may be less than [[Lisdexamfetamine]], there is some evidence that it may be used in combination with [[stimulants]]


For CDS, multiple randomized controlled clinical trials have found that atomoxetine is an effective treatment, compared to methylphenidate, which has been found ineffective. 

###### Administration
Atomoxetine is available most commonly under the brand Strattera and generically

It is available as oral capsules containing 10, 18, 25, 40, 60, 80 & 100 mg of Atomoxetine HCl

The initial therapeutic effects of atomoxetine usually take 1-4 weeks to become apparent.
A further 2-4 weeks may be required for the full therapeutic effects to be seen.

The typical dosing varies based on age and weight

For children & adolescents up to 70 kg:
- The initial daily dose is 0.5 mg/kg
- The target total daily dose is 1.2 mg/kg
- The maximum total daily dose is 1.4 mg/kg

For children & adolescents over 70 kg and adults:
- The initial daily dose is 40 mg
- The target total daily dose is 80 mg
- The maximum total daily dose is 100 mg

###### Mechanism
Atomoxetine increases [[norepinephrine]] levels by inhibiting its transporter, [[NET]]

Throughout the brain, this increases norepinephrine levels, and it also increases [[dopamine]] levels in the [[Prefrontal cortex]], where [[DAT]] expression is very minimal, so dopamine reuptake is also mediated via NET.

Unlike with stimulants, Atomoxetine's effects arise from long-term neuroadaptive changes from the new [[Receptor Biology|receptor]] activity, but it does share 70-80% of the brain regions it affects with stimulants. 

Atomoxetine may have some activity at [[SERT]], possibly contributing to some increase in [[serotonin]], but this has to be further studied, although it could explain some of its side effects. 

Atomoxetine has also been found to [[antagonist|antagonize]] the [[NMDA receptor]] in rats, suggesting a [[glutamate|glutamatergic]] mechanism

###### Pharmacokinetics & Interactions
First-pass metabolism by the liver is dependent on [[CYP2D6]], resulting in an absolute bioavailability of 63% for extensive metabolizers and 94% for poor metabolizers. 

Concurrent treatment with a CYP2D6 inhibitor, like [[Fluoxetine]], has been shown to increase plasma atomoxetine by 100% or more. 

Maximum plasma concentration is reached in 1-2 hours. 
If taken with food, maximum plasma concentration decreases by 10-40%, and the $\ce{T_{max}}$ is delayed by 3 hours

It's half-life varies widely between individuals, with an average range of 4.5-19 hours

Strattera should be administered with caution to patients being treated with [[Salbutamol]] (or other [[beta-2 receptors|beta-2]] [[agonist|agonists]]) because the action on the cardiovascular system can be potentiated resulting in increases in heart rate and blood pressure. 

Atomoxetine has minor inhibition of [[CYP3A4]], but not enough to make dose adjustments in drugs metabolized by that enzyme
