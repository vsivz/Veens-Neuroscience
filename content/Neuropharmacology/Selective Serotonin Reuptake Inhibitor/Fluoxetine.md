Fluoxetine is a [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor]], and was the first SSRI to be approved by the FDA

###### Uses
Fluoxetine has been approved by the FDA for:
- [[major depressive disorder]] (8 and older)
- [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]] (7 and older)
- [[Panic Disorder]] (with and without [[agoraphobia]])
- [[bulimia nervosa]] 
- depressive episodes associated with [[bipolar I disorder]] (as an adjunct with [[olanzapine]])
- treatment-resistant depression when used in combination with olanzapine

The [[APA]] guidelines recommend Fluoxetine as a first-line pharmacotherapy (along with [[Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy|CBT]]) for patients with bulimia

It is also used off-label to treat:
- [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety disorders]]
- PMDD
- [[binge-eating disorder]]
- The [[APA]] endorses it for [[posttraumatic stress disorder|PTSD]]

###### Administration + Related
It is primarily sold under the brand name Prozac. (Also sometimes Sarafem)

Peak plasma concentrations are reached in 6-8 hours.

It has a long half life of 4-6 days, which makes it ideal to those prone to forgetting doses. 
Initially the half life is 1-3 days, but since Fluoxetine, and its active metabolite norfluoxetine, inhibit their own metabolism this increases to 4-6 days after long-term use. 
Steady concentration in the blood is achieved only after 4 weeks. 
Brain concentration of fluoxetine and its metabolites keeps increasing through the first 5 weeks.

The long half lives of fluoxetine and norfluoxetine help reduce the withdrawal effects of fluoxetine discontinuation.

On average, the initial [[antidepressants|antidepressant]] effects emerge after 2-4 weeks. 

It is available in the following forms:
- 10, 20, 40 mg oral capsules
- 90 mg delayed-release oral capsules
- 10, 20, 60 mg oral tablets
- 20 mg/5 mL oral solution

###### Mechanism
It inhibits the reuptaken of serotonin into presynaptic [[neurons]] by blocking [[SERT]], leading to increased [[serotonin]] levels in the [[synapse|synaptic cleft]]
It also is a small [[antagonist]] of [[5-HT2C]] receptors. But this is pretty small. Compared to SERT with with a binding affinity 1 $\mathsf{(K_{i} \, in\,nM})$ it has a binding affinity for these receptors of 112. 
This means at low doses, Fluoxetine will basically only have its effects on SERT. But at higher concentrations, when SERT becomes saturated, it may be better able to antagonize 5-HT2C.

Prolonged exposure to fluoxetine changes the expression of [[genes]] involved in [[myelin sheath|myelination]]
###### Pharmacokinetics
It strongly inhibits the [[CYP2D6]] and [[CYP2C19]] enzymes, leading to possible interactions. 
It moderately inhibits [[CYP2B6]] and [[CYP2C9]] 

It is metabolized via [[CYP2D6]], which it also inhibits. 

It is metabolized via CYP2D6, producing its active metabolite [[norfluoxetine]]
Norfluoxetine inhibits SERT much more weakly than Fluoxetine does. However, it has a very similar affinity to 5-HT2C receptors. 


###### Side effects
Side effects include:
- gastrointestinal problems
- nausea
- sleep problems
- sexual side effects
A rare side effect of Fluoxetine in particular is increased suicidal ideation in those younger than 25

Among the SSRIs, Fluoxetine has been shown to cause the most short-term weight loss and activation (e.g. [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]] and agitation)
