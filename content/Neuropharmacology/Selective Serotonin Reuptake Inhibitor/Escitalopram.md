Escitalopram is an [[antidepressants|antidepressant]] of the [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRI]] class

###### Usage
The FDA have approved it for:
- [[major depressive disorder]] in [[Adolescence|adolescents]] and adults
- [[Generalized Anxiety Disorder]] in adults
In Europe, it is approved for:
- [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]]
- [[obsessive-compulsive disorder]]
- [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety disorders]]
	- GAD
	- [[social anxiety disorder]]
	- [[Panic Disorder]]

Escitalopram is among the most effective and well-tolerated antidepressants for the short-term treatment of MDD in adults. 
It is also the safest one to give to children and adolescents. 

It has been shown to be modestly superior to [[Citalopram]] in efficacy and tolerability
###### Administration + Related
Its sold under the brand name Lexapro, and sometimes Cipralex

It is available in:
- 5, 10, 20 mg oral tablets
- 5 mg/5 mL oral solution

For adults with Generalized Anxiety Disorder and/or depression and children with depression:
- Initial dose: 10 mg/day; increase if necessary after at least 1 week (3 weeks in children 12+) of treatment to 20 mg/day
- Maintenance dose: 10-20 mg/day
- Maximum dose: 20 mg/day

Usual geriatric dose for depression: 10 mg/day

###### Mechanism
It is the S-(+) enantiomer of Citalopram

Being an SSRI, it increases [[serotonin]] levels by blocking [[SERT]]

Of all SSRIs, Escitalopram has the highest selectivity for SERT, and especially highest relative selectivity for SERT compared to [[NET]], compared to others which can interact with other proteins. r
Because of this, it has the least side effects out of the six main SSRIs

In addition to SERT, it can also bind to the [[allosteric sites|allosteric site]] of SERT, decreasing the dissociation rate from the main orthosteric site. 

###### Pharmacokinetics
It has a half-life of 27-33 hours 

Peak plasma concentration is typically attained in approximately 5 hours
steady-state plasma concentrations are achieved within 1-2 weeks
The absorption of Escitalopram is not affected by food

Escitalopram is hepatically metabolized by [[CYP3A4]], [[CYP2D6]] and [[CYP2C19]], yielding $S$-desmethyl-citalopram (S-DCT) and $S$-di-desmethyl-citalopram (S-DDCT)
S-DCT, the [[principal metabolite]], is present at ~1/3 the level of escitalopram; but S-DCT is a weak inhibitor of Serotonin [[reuptake]], and does not contribute significantly to therapeutic effects.
S-DDCT, the [[didemethyl metabolite]], is typically present at or below [[quantifiable concentration|quantifiable concentrations]] 

Escitalopram weakly inhibits [[CYP2D6]]
Escitalopram is  a substrate of [[P-glycoprotein]], so P-gp inhibitors can improve its BBB penetrability and enhance its effects. 

Escitalopram has low [[protein binding]] (56%) and is not likely to cause interactions with highly protein-bound drugs

Generally, within the 10-30 mg/day range, Escitalopram and S-DCT's pharmacokinetics are linear and dose-dependent
###### Side Effects
Generally, Escitalopram has the least side effects compared to other SSRIs.

Very common side effects include:
- Headache (24%)
- Nausea (18%)
- Ejaculation Disorder (9-14%)
- Somnolence (4-13%)
- Insomnia (7-12%)

Other common effects include:
- anxiety
- diarrhea
- dizziness
- dry mouth
- fatigue

Escitalopram (and Citalopram) are associated with a mild dose-dependent [[QT prolongation|QT interval prolongation]]. 
At 30 mg/day Escitalopram induced a QTc increase of 10.7 ms, significantly less than a therapeutically equivalent 60 mg/day dose of Citalopram, which induced a QTc increase of 18.5 ms. (A QTc increase less than 60 ms is not likely to confer significant risk).

Like other SSRIs, escitalopram has also been reported to cause hyponatremia (low sodium levels)
