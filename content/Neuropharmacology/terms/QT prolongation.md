QT prolongation refers to an extended QT interval on an electrocardiogram. 

The QT interval represents the time it takes for the [[heart]]'s electrical system to depolarize (contract) and repolarize (relax)

It is measured from the beginning of the Q wave to the end of the T wave.

*Corrected QT Interval (QTc)*: Because the QT interval depends on heart rate, it's often corrected to standardize measurement

One with a naturally low heart rate is generally at higher risk of adverse effects when taking a medication that increases QTc.
This is because a slower heart rate naturally results in a longer QT interval.
