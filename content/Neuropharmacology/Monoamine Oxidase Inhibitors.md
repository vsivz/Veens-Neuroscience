MAOIs are a class of drugs that function primarily by inhibiting the activity of the enzyme [[Monoamine Oxidase]] 

MAOIs inhibit the activity of one or both isoforms of MAO.

Irreversible inhibitors bind permanently to MAO, requiring new enzyme synthesis for activity restoration
Reversible inhibitors bind temporarily to MAO, and can be displaced by high concentrations of substrates

This inhibition prevents monoamines from being degraded after [[reuptake]], leading to increased concentrations of these [[neurotransmitter]]s

Some MAOIs are selective
MAO-A inhibitors inhibit [[MAO-A]], increasing primarily [[serotonin]] and [[norepinephrine]]
MAO-B inhibitors inhibit [[MAO-B]], increasing primarily dopamine 

###### Dietary Restrictions
MAOIs require avoiding foods high in tyramine, which is normally metabolized by MAO.
High levels of tyramine can cause a hypertensive crisis. 
Foods to avoid include:
- Aged cheeses
- Cured meats (ex. Salami, Pepperoni)
- Fermented Foods (ex. Sauerkraut, Soy Sauce)
- Certain [[alcohol|Alcoholic]] Beverages, (ex. Red wine, draft beer)n

###### Side Effects
Common side effects include
- Orthostatic hypotension
- Weight gain
- Insomnia or hypersomnia
- Sexual dysfunction
- Headache

Severe side effects include:
- Hypertensive crisis, triggered by ingestion of tyramine-rich foods
- [[Serotonin Syndrome]], when combined with Serotonergic agents
