
##### Overview & Neurobiology
Magnesium L-Threonate is a compound of [[magnesium]] and L-threonic acid, a sugar acid derived from the metabolic breakdown of [[Vitamin C|Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C)]] 

Unlike most magnesium salts, this compound is engineered for [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] effects, unlike most magnesium salts. 

Most magnesium salts are poorly transported into the brain. 
However, Magnesium L-Threonate is significantly better at permeating the [[blood-brain barrier]].
Because of this, MgT allows for much higher concentrations of Magnesium in the brain than most other supplements. 
MgT has moderate systemic absorption, but disproportionately high CNS availability compared to other salts. 

##### Research Insights
- Studies have shown that MgT supplementation is linked with improvements in [[3.7 Learning|learning]], [[memory]] and even increases in [[synapse|synaptic density]] in the [[hippocampus]]
- Enhanced magnesium availability has been linked with increased [[dendrites|dendritic spine]] density, suggesting that MgT might promote synaptic remodeling, which is critical for [[neuroplasticity]]
- Because of its role in maintaining synaptic integrity and preventing excitotoxicity, there is an interest in exploring MgT as a therapeutic option for conditions characterized by continuous cognitive decline, like [[Alzheimer's disease]]
