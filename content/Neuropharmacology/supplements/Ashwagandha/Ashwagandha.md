
**Ashwagandha** (Withania Somnifera) is an adaptogenic herb traditionally used in Ayurvedic medicine. 
It is widely recognized for its potential to reduce [[stress]], [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]] and improve [[sleep]] quality. 

#### Active Compounds
- [[Withanolides]]: A group of [[steroid|steroidal]] lactones, primarily responsible for Ashwagandha's therapeutic effects. 
- [[Alkalloids]]: Including [[Somniferine]] and [[Withanine]], which may impact [[Neural Transmission]]
- [[Flavonoids]]: Known for [[antioxidant]] and anti-[[inflammation|inflammatory]] properties

#### Mechanisms
Ashwagandha acts through multiple pathways in the [[1.2 Overview of the Nervous System|Nervous System]]

###### Regulation of HPA Axis
Ashwagandha's stress-reducing capabilities largely arise due to modulation of the [[HPA axis]]. 

Ashwagandha decreases circulating [[cortisol]] levels by attenuating excessive [[hypothalamus|hypothalamic]] secretion of *[[corticotropin-releasing hormone]] (CRH)*, leading to reduced secretion of *[[adrenocorticotropic hormone]] (ACTH)* from the [[pituitary gland]] and thus lower cortisol release. 

It enhances [[resilience]] to stress by stabilizing physiological responses, reducing the harmful effects of [[chronic stress]] exposure. 

###### GABAergic Modulation
Ashwagandha potentiates the activity of the [[GABA]] system. 

Withanolides are thought to bind directly to, or indirectly enhance the sensitivity of, [[GABA receptors]], promoting inhibitory signaling, reducing neuronal excitability, and promoting [[anxiolytics|anxiolytic]] and [[sedatives|sedative]] effects.
