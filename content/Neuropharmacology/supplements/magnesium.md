## Overview
Magnesium is an essential mineral that plays several critical roles in the [[1.4b Brain Structure|brain]] and [[1.2 Overview of the Nervous System|Nervous System]]

***
## Fundamental functions
###### Enzymatic Cofactor
Magnesium acts as a [[cofactor]] for over 300 enzymatic reactions. 
In [[neurons]], these enzymes are involved in energy metabolism, [[DNA]] & [[RNA]] synthesis, and protein synthesis - all vital processes for cell maintenance and function.  

###### Ionic Balance and Cellular Stability
It helps maintain the proper balance of ions across [[cell membranes]], contributing to the stabilization of electrical potentials
This is crucial for generating and propagating [[action potential|action potentials]]

***
## Modulation of Excitatory [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmission]]
one of the most well-documented roles of magnesium is *Voltage-Dependent Blockade* of [[NMDA receptor|NMDA receptors]]
These are [[glutamate]] receptors, so are key to [[excitatory neurotransmitter|excitatory neurotransmission]]

###### Blockage of NMDA receptors
- When a neuron is at its *resting potential*, Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ Ions occupy a within the NMDA receptor's ion channel. This "plug" prevents ions like Ca$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ and Na$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ from passing through the receptor, even if the receptor's binding sites for glutamate and [[Glycine]] are activated. 
  
- This block by Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ is voltage-dependent: When the neuron is at resting state, the positive charge of Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ is electrostatically attracted into the channel, effectively blocking it. This *prevents the receptor from being overly active in the absence of a strong signal*

- When a neuron becomes *depolarized* - often due to the activation of other excitatory receptors or incoming synaptic activity - the internal membrane potential becomes less negative. This change in voltage repels the Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ ions from the channel. 

###### Voltage-Dependent Opening
- Because the NMDA receptor *requires the binding of both it's ligands* (glutamate and a co-[[agonist]] like glycine) *and a depolarized membrane* to relieve the Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ block, it functions as a "coincidence detector". 

- This means that the [[Receptor Biology|receptor]] *only allows ion flow when presynaptic glutamate release coincides with postsynaptic depolarization*. 

- Once the Mg$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ blockade is lifted, Ca$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ (and Na$\mathsf{^{2+}}$) ions flow into the neuron. The influx of Ca$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ is critical to the activation of intracellular signaling pathways that lead to [[long-term potentiation]] - a cellular model of learning and memory. 

***
## Neuroprotection
- By *blocking the channel at resting potentials*, magnesium prevents continuous Ca$\mathsf{^{2+}}$ entry, which could lead to calcium overload and excitotoxicity (a process that can damage or kill neurons.) This protective role is essential for maintaining the health of neural circuits. 

- Proper Magnesium levels ensure that the process of Long Term Potentiation occurs efficiently, strengthening synaptic connections when needed. 
  
- There is emerging evidence that Magnesium may also modulate neuroinflammatory responses. 
  by impacting [[cytokines|cytokine]] production and other inflammatory pathways, magnesium helps maintain a healthier neural environment

- Altered magnesium levels have been linked to disorders such as [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] and [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]].

## Role in Sleep
Magnesium supplementation can help [[sleep]]

###### Modulation of NMDA Receptors
By modulating NMDA receptors, Magnesium helps prevent a hyperexcitable state of neurons, which can be a major barrier to initiating sleep. 

###### Enhancement of GABA
Magnesium is thought to interact at [[modulatory sites]] on the [[GABA-A  Receptor]]. 
Interaction can enhance the receptor's responsiveness to [[GABA]].
By blocking excess NMDA signaling, magnesium indirectly supports GABAergic function by preventing excessive neuronal activation that can override inhibitory signals. 

###### Melatonin and Circadian Rhythms
Although direct evidence is still emerging, since magnesium is known to serve as a cofactor in numerous enzymatic reactions, it it possible that magnesium could support the activity of enzymes that are responsible for the synthesis of [[melatonin]] from [[serotonin]].

Altered magnesium levels have been linked in changes in [[clock gene]] expression. 
This suggests that magnesium might help synchronize the molecular clocks within cells, ensuring that the timing of melatonin synthesis alights properly with environmental light-dark cycles.
