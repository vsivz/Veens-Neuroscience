Anticholinergics are a class of drugs that block the action of [[acetylcholine]] in the [[Central Nervous System]] and [[Peripheral Nervous System]] by inhibiting [[muscarinic receptors]] 

These drugs interfere with [[parasympathetic nervous system]] activity, which normally controls functions such as salivation, digestion, urination and heart rate regulations

###### Mechanism
These drugs primarily [[antagonist|antagonize]] muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (mAChRs):
- M$\mathsf{_1}$ Receptors - CNS, gastric glands
- M$\mathsf{_2}$ Receptors - heart
- M$\mathsf{_3}$ Receptors - smooth muscle, glands
- M$\mathsf{_4}$ & M$\mathsf{_5}$ Receptors - CNS

By blocking these receptors, they reduce cholinergic [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmission]], leading to their effects.
