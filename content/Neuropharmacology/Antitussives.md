#### Overview
Antitussives, also known as Cough Suppressants, are medications designed to reduce or suppress the cough reflex. 
They are primarily used for non-productive (dry) coughs where the cough itself causes discomfort or interferes with sleep, rather than for productive coughs that clear mucus from the airways. 

#### Mechanism
Antitussives work by targeting the cough reflex which involve both [[Central Nervous System|central]] ([[brainstem]]) and [[Peripheral Nervous System|peripheral]] (airways) components.

###### Central Action
Most common antitussives, like [[dextromethorphan]] and [[codeine]], act on the brain's cough center in the [[medulla oblongata|medulla]]. 
They inhibit the neural pathways that trigger coughing. 

###### Peripheral Action
Some agents may reduce irritation in the throat and airways or act as local anesthetics. 
However, central suppression is much more widely used.
