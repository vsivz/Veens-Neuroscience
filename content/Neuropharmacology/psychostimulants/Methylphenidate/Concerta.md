Concerta is a [[stimulants|stimulant]] medication used primarily to treat [[ADHD]]

The base drug itself is just [[Methylphenidate]] HCl, and Concerta just has a different release mechanism, so it has the Methylphenidate's 
 [[Methylphenidate#Usage|Usage]], [[Methylphenidate#Mechanism|Mechanism]], [[Methylphenidate#Pharmacokinetics and Interactions|Pharmacokinetics and interactions]] and [[Methylphenidate#Side Effects|Side Effects]] 

###### Administration
Concerta comes in 18, 27, 36 & 54 mg tablets. 

The usual starting dose is 18 mg once daily. 
The maximum dose for children & adolescents is 54 mg/day, and the maximum dose for adults is 72 mg/day

Nonmedicinal ingredients: butylated hydroxytoluene, carnauba wax, cellulose acetate, hypromellose, lactose, phosphoric acid, poloxamer, polyethylene glycol, polyethylene oxides, povidone, propylene glycol, sodium chloride, stearic acid, succinic acid, synthetic iron oxides, titanium dioxide, and triacetin.

According to the Product Monograph, the recommended dose conversion from other MPH HCl formulations to concerta is:
- 5 mg MPH HCl b.i.d/t.i.d or 20 mg MPH HCl SR: 18 mg Concerta
- 10 mg MPH HCl b.i.d/t.i.d or 40 mg MPH HCl SR: 36 mg Concerta
- 15 mg MPH HCl b.i.d/t.i.d or 60 mg MPH HCl SR: 54 mg Concerta
- 20 mg MPH HCl b.i.d/t.i.d: 72 mg Concerta
###### Release Mechanism

Uses an *Osmotic-Controlled Release Oral Delivery System (OROS)*, with three layers:
- an osmotically active trilayer core surrounded by:
- a semipermeable membrane with:
- an immediate-release drug overcoat

In an aqueous environment, like the [[gastrointestinal tract]], the drug overcoat, which contains 22% of the dose, dissolves within 1 hour, providing an initial dose. 
Water permeates through the membrane into the tablet core. As the osmotically active polymer excipients expand, methylphenidate, methylphenidate is released through an orfice. 
The membrane controls the rate at which the water enters the tablet core, which in turn controls drug delivery.
The drug release rate from the system increases with time, over a period of 6-7 hours.
The biologically inert components of the table remain intact during gastrointestinal transit, and are eliminated in the stool as a tablet shell along with insoluble core components. 

Following oral administration, plasma MPH concentrations reach an initial maximum at about 1 hour, followed by gradual ascending concentrations over the next 5 to 9 hours.
On average, across all doses of Concerta, it takes between 6-10 hours to reach peak plasma concentrations.

The relative bioavailability of Concerta and methylphenidate IR three times a day in adults is comparable
