Methylphenidate is a popular [[stimulants|stimulant]] with many different brand names and release mechanisms.

###### Usage
Methylphenidate is most commonly used to treat:
- [[ADHD]]
- [[narcolepsy]]

It may also be prescribed off-label in treatment-resistant cases of [[5.4b Bipolar Disorders|bipolar disorder]] and [[major depressive disorder]]
There is some weak evidence favoring methylphenidate's efficacy for [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]], including providing additional benefit as an adjunct therapy to [[antidepressants]]

Methylphenidate has been reported to be effective for treatment of [[social anxiety disorder]] when ADHD is comorbid
###### Administration
In Canada, there are 5 methylphenidate medications approved:
- [[Biphentin]]
- [[Concerta]]
- [[Foquest]]
- [[Quillivant XR]]
- [[Ritalin SR]]

Derivatives of methylphenidate are also sold as their own medication:
- [[Focalin]] ([[dexmethylphenidate]])
- [[Azstarys]] (dexmethylphenidate & [[serdexmethylphenidate]])

###### Isomerism
Methylphenidate is a [[racemic mixture]] of two of its [[stereoisomer|stereoisomers]], the threo [[disastereoisomers]]. The other two are not used as they have negligible [[psychostimulant]] effects. 
The two [[enantiomer|enantiomers]] used are:
- [[dexmethylphenidate]] - the significantly more active enantiomer. Also sold as its own formulation
- levomethylphenidate - the significantly less active enantiomer, contributes minimal therapeutic effects

###### Mechanism 
Methylphenidates increase levels of [[dopamine]], and to a slightly lesser extent [[norepinephrine]], by inhibiting [[DAT]] and [[NET]].

Because they just block the transporters, their efficacy is much more dependent on basal levels of these [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmitters]].

By increasing the amount of these neurotransmitters in the [[synapse]], Methylphenidates increase [[neurons|neuronal]] firing rate. This is as opposed to [[Amphetamine|Amphetamines]], which decrease the firing rate, but compensate in other ways. 

On average, methylphenidate elicits a 3-4 times increase in dopamine and norepinephrine in the [[striatum]] & [[Prefrontal cortex]]

###### Pharmacokinetics and Interactions
Methylphenidate taken orally has a bioavailability of 11-52%, depending on the release mechanism
Dexmethylphenidate is much more bioavailable than levomethylphenidate

The half-life of methylphenidate is 2-3 hours
The peak plasma time is achieved between 1-3 hours

Methylphenidate has a low plasma [[protein binding]] of 10-33%

Immediate-release methylphenidate has greater oral bioavailability and speed of absorption when taken with a meal

Methylphenidate is metabolized into ritalinic acid (which has little to no pharmacologic activity) by CES1A1 enzymes in the liver.
Dexmethylphenidate is selectively metabolized at a significantly slower rate than levomethylphenidate

Coingestion with [[alcohol]] increases the blood plasma levels of dexmethylphenidate, according to some sources, by up to 40%

Studies have shown that Methylphenidate may inhibit the metabolism of vitamin K anticoagulants, certain anticonvulsants, [[tricyclic antidepressants]] & [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]]

Concomitant use of halogenated anesthetics and Methylphenidate may increase the risk of sudden blood pressure and heart rate increase during surgery.
###### Side Effects
Common side effects include:
- loss of appetite
- weight loss
- [[insomnia]]
- [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]], nervousness & irritability
- depression
- increased sweating
- headache
- restlessness
- increased heart rate and blood pressure
- dizziness
- dry mouth

Methylphenidate has been correlated with a temporary slowing of growth rate in children and adolescents, with an average decrease of 2 cm in height, and 2.7 kg in weight following 3 years of continuous use. 

There is some clinical evidence that methylphenidate may lower the convulsive threshold in patients with prior history of seizures.
