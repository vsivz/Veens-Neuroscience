 Amphetamine is a powerful [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] [[stimulants|stimulant]] that is used recreationally, and is also prescribed as [[ADHD meds]]
*Generally more potent/effective* than Methylphenidate-based meds
In Canada, there are currently three approved: 
- [[Mixed Amphetamine Salts]]
- [[Lisdexamfetamine]]
- [[Dextroamphetamine]]
#### Mechanism
> [!blank|float-right-medium]
> ![[Pasted image 20250126164950.png#invert]]

>[!pharma] [[Catecholamine]] modulation
> Amphetamines increase the amounts of dopamine and norepinephrine, and to a much lesser extent, Serotonin
>The reinforcing and motivational salience-promoting effects of amphetamines are due mostly to enhanced dopaminergic activity in the [[mesolimbic pathway]].
>The euphoric and locomotor-stimulating effects of amphetamine are dependent upon the magnitude and speed by which it increases [[synapse|synaptic]] dopamine and norepinephrine concentrations in the [[striatum]]

Amphetamine strongly induces [[cocaine- and amphetamine-regulated transcript]] [[genes|gene]] expression. This induces observable increases in neuronal development and survival in vitro. 

Amphetamines can also inhibit [[Monoamine Oxidase|monoamine oxidases]] at high doses. 

>[!pharma] entering the neuron
> Amphetamine can enter the presynaptic [[neurons|neuron]] either through transporters, DAT, NET and [[SERT]] or by diffusing across the neuronal membrane directly.
> When the transporter uptakes it, Amphetamine competitively blocks that DAT from being able to [[reuptake]] its [[neurotransmitter]]. 

>[!pharma] TAAR1 agonization
> Upon entering the neuron, Amphetamine agonizes the TAAR1 receptor, a [[G-protein-coupled receptors|GPCR]]. This activation increases [[cAMP]], and triggers two important signaling pathways involving *Protein Kinase A (PKA)* and *Protein Kinase C (PKC)* 
> These two signaling pathways lead to the phosphorylation of [[DAT]], [[NET]] and to a lesser extent [[SERT]]. 
> - Phosphorylation by either PKA and PKC can result in internalization of that transporter - non-competitive reuptake inhibition
> - PKC-meditated phosphorylation induces the reversal of monoamine transport through their transporter

[[Selective Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|NRIs]] like [[Atomoxetine]] prevent [[norepinephrine]] release induced by amphetamines, and have been found to reduce the stimulant and euphoric effects of amphetamines. 

>[!pharma] VMAT2 interaction
>Amphetamine enantiomers are also substrates for a kind of neuronal synaptic vesicle uptake transporter called *VMAT2*
>When amphetamines is taken up by VMAT2, the vesicle effluxes (releases) dopamine, norepinephrine and serotonin. 
>It also inhibits other transporters, including 
>- VMAT1
>- SLC1A1, a [[glutamate]] transporter
>- SLC22A3, an extracellular [[monoamine]] transporter

> > #### Isomerism
> > Amphetamine has two [[enantiomer|enantiomers]]:
> > - Dextroamphetamine, the right-handed (+) enantiomer, is much more cognitively stimulating, is sold as its own drug
> > - Levoamphetamine, the left-handed (-) enantiomer, has less cognitive effects than Dextroamphetamine, and more peripheral effects
> > 
> > 
> > 
> > Levoamphetamine is much more selective for [[NET]] than [[DAT]], leading to more [[norepinephrine]] release than [[dopamine]]. Dextroamphetamine has similar affinity for each. 
> > 
> > In addition, Dextroamphetamine is a much more potent [[agonist]] of [[trace amine-associated receptor 1|TAAR1]], a receptor that mediates a significant part of Amphetamine's effects, than Levoamphetamine, so it has more potent effects in general.
> > 
> > Dextroamphetamine produces roughly 3-4 times more CNS stimulation, and has less cardiovascular and [[Peripheral Nervous System|PNS]] effects, compared to Levoamphetamine.
>
> > #### Pharmacokinetics
> > The oral bioavailability of amphetamine varies with gastrointestinal pH, but it is typically around 90%
> > 
> > The half lives of Dextroamphetamine and Levoamphetamine are 9-11 hours and 11-14 hours respectively.
> > The immediate release and extended release versions of both enantiomers reach peak plasma concentrations at 3 and 7 hours post-dose respectively
> > 
> > [[CYP2D6]], [[dopamine beta-hydroxylase]], FMO3, XM-ligase, GLYAT are all enzymes that metabolize amphetamines and its metabolites. 
>
> > #### Side Effects
> > Most common side effects of medical amphetamine use include: (not including abuse of amphetamine or its derivatives like [[methamphetamine]])
> > - fast heartbeat
> > - decreased appetite
> > - tremors
> > - headache
> > - [[insomnia]]
> > - dizziness
> > - upset stomach
> > - weight loss
> > - dry mouth
> > - [[5.4a Anxiety Disorders|anxiety]]
