Dextroamphetamine is a potent [[stimulants|stimulant]] of the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], and an [[ADHD meds|ADHD med]]
It is the right-handed (+) [[enantiomer]] of [[Amphetamine]], so it has nearly the same [[Amphetamine#Mechanism|Mechanism]], [[Amphetamine#Pharmacokinetics|Pharmacokinetics]] & [[Amphetamine#Side Effects|Side Effects]]

###### Uses
It is primarily used to treat [[ADHD]] and [[narcolepsy]]

It is sometimes prescribed off-label for [[5.4b Depressive Disorders|depression]] and obesity

Studies have shown the safety and efficacy of long-term continuous amphetamine use for ADHD.

###### Administration + Related
It is most commonly sold under brand names Dexedrine and ProCentra. Also, Dextrostat, Xelstrym, Zenzedi & Liquadd

It's immediate release tablets last ~4 hours
It's extended release spanules last 6-8 hours, using a 50/50 release mode


Dextroamphetamine comes in a variety of forms:
- 5, 10, 15 mg oral capsules, extended release
- 5 mg/5 mL oral solution
- 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 30 mg oral tablets
- 4.5, 9, 13.5, 18 mg/9 hours transdermal film

For Narcolepsy:
- The typical starting dose for adults and [[Adolescence|adolescents]] is 10 mg/day. Increased by up to 10 mg per week if needed. 
- The typical starting dose for children (6-11) is 5 mg/day. Increased by up to 5 mg per week if needed. 

For ADHD:
- The typical starting dose for adults and children (6+) is 5 mg taken once or twice per day. If taken twice, take it upon waking and then 4-6 hours later. Increased by up to 5 mg per week if needed.
- For children (3-5), the typical starting dose is 2.5 mg/day. Increased by no more than 2.5 mg per week.
###### Chemistry
The drug that is being consumed is Dextroamphetamine Sulfate
30 mg of Dextroamphetamine Sulfate contains 22 mg of base Dextroamphetamine

*Dextroamphetamine* is the *right-handed (+) [[enantiomer]]* of [[Amphetamine|Amphetamine]], which has more potent effects on the Central Nervous System
The absence of levoamphetamine in Dextroamphetamine makes it more *targeted towards cognitive effects*, compared to physical energy and alertness.
