Vortioxetine is an novel [[antidepressants|antidepressant]] of the [[Serotonin Modulator and Stimulator]] class. 
It's multimodal action helps it overcome some problems associated with traditional [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]] and [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|SNRIs]]

### Usage
Vortioxetine is indicated for treatment of [[major depressive disorder]] in adults. 
It is not approved for pediatric patients. 

### Administration + Related
It is most commonly sold under the brand name *Trintellix*, and also *Brintellix*

It is available in 5, 10 & 20 mg immediate-release film-coated tablets

The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/day. 
Dosage may then be increased to 20 mg/day, as tolerated (typically after ~10 days)

### Pharmacodynamics
Vortioxetine, like SSRIs, inhibits [[SERT]], which increases [[serotonin]] by reducing its reuptake. 
However, Vortioxetine differs from traditional SSRIs & SNRIs in that in addition to its activity at SERT, it also has activity at several [[serotonin receptors]], especially [[5-HT3|5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$]]


>[!chem] Neurotransmission
>^neurotransmission

**Serotonin Increase**
The receptor that Vortioxetine interacts with the most is the 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptor, for which it is an antagonist. 
This antagonism increases [[pyramidal neuron]] activity by removing 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptor-mediated excitation of [[GABA]] [[interneurons]]. 
So, it leads to a significant increase in Serotonin levels.  ^4b41ff

Vortioxetine is also a [[partial agonist]] at [[5-HT1B|5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$]] receptors, which are both autoreceptors and [[heteroreceptors]]. This decreases their activity under high activation, but not under low activation. 

Weak antagonism of the [[5-HT1D|5-HT$\mathsf{_{1d}}$]] autoreceptors may also contribute to Vortioxetine's antidepressant activity.  

*Activity at the 5-HT1A Autoreceptor*
Traditional SSRIs require significant chronic exposure before their full therapeutic effect can be seen, partially due to the time it takes to desensitize the [[5-HT1A|5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$]] [[serotonin autoreceptors]], which, when activated by Serotonin, will reduce Serotonin levels as a negative feedback regulation mechanism. ^0f72b6

Benefit would not be achieved with an [[antagonist]] as the benefit of blocking the presynaptic 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptors would be cancelled out by the simultaneous blockade of postsynaptic 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptors, which increase Serotonin levels. 

So Vortioxetine [[agonist|agonizes]] these receptors. While an agonist will acutely suppress serotonergic [[neurons|neuron]] firing in the [[Dorsal Ralphe Nucleus]], repeated dosing will lead to its recovery through the desensitization of somatodendritic, but not post-synaptic 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptors. 

5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonism also appears to contribute to this, as co-administration of vortioxetine and a 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ agonist attenuated Vortioxetine's acute suppression of DRN firing, and delayed the recovery of neuronal firing. 

**Norepinephrine**
Vortioxetine was shown to increase extracellular [[norepinephrine]] levels significantly in the [[Medial Prefrontal Cortex (outer)]] and [[hippocampus|vHIP]].  ^e49199

Unlike an SNRI, acute dosing of Vortioxetine did not increase NE in the [[Nucleus Accumbens]] (NAc), and only affected the [[locus coeruleus]] (LC) firing at very high i.v. doses, unlike the reduction in firing rates produced by acute SSRI treatment. 

The absence of an effect of Vortioxetine on LC firing may be due to 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonism. Typically, activation of 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors has been shown to increase NE levels in the LC, which would reduce LC neuronal firing via stimulation of [[alpha-2 receptors|alpha$\mathsf{_{2}}$ adrenergic receptors]]
Hence, Vortioxetine's action on 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors appears to be involved in its enhancing effects on NE. 
However, other receptor activities may be involved, since the minimal effective dose of vortioxetine promoting NE release is higher than one that fully occupies 5-HT3 receptors and SERT. 

Some studies have shown that 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptor agonists increase extracellular NE levels. 
Since the minimal effective doses of vortioxetine correspond to those with significant $(\approx40\%)$ 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptor occupancy, it appears that 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ agonism contributes to the increased extracellular NE levels in the mPFC and vHIP. 

The increase may require a combination of 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonism and 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ agonism, as the SERT inhibitor and 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ agonist [[vilazodone]] failed to increase NE in these regions. 

**Dopamine**
Vortioxetine increased extracellular [[dopamine]] levels in the vHIP after an acute dose, but this was not sustained after chronic administration. 
However, Vortioxetine increased extracellular DA in the mPFC both acutely and chronically. 
But Vortioxetine failed to increase extracellular DA in the NAc acutely.  ^11f51b

These regional differences may be explained using the different origins of DA in these regions. 
In the NAc, extracellular DA just comes from dopaminergic neurons within the NAc. 
But in the mPFC, it also comes from noradrenergic neurons in the mPFC, and is also increased by stimulation of the LC. 

Stimulation of post-synaptic 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$  receptors has been shown to increase [[Prefrontal cortex|PFC]] DA levels, and the effects of vortioxetine on extracellular DA levels only arises at doses that produce significant occupancy of 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ receptors. 
However, since vilazodone failed to produce these increases, there is likely other additional mechanisms that are involved in this dopamine increase. 

**Acetylcholine & Histamine**
Vortioxetine increases extracellular [[acetylcholine]] (ACh) and [[histamine]] (HA) levels in the mPFC, which may contribute to the positive effects of Vortioxetine on cognitive function.  ^145d7d

Vortioxetine's effects on these [[neurotransmitter|neurotransmitters]] is likely mediated via its activities at Serotonin Receptors, as Vortioxetine has low affinity for cholinergic and histaminergic receptors. 

Several of the receptors targeted by vortioxetine, including 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ ,5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$ & 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors, are involved in the release of ACh either directly via their presence on cholinergic neurons


>[!synapse] Synaptic Transmission & Neuroplasticity
>^neuroplasticity
- Studies have shown that [[vortioxetine]] enhances [[synapse|synaptic]] transmission. 
Although not the SSRI it was compared to ([[Escitalopram]]), Vortioxetine potentiated [[theta waves|theta]] burst-induced [[long-term potentiation]] and counteracted Serotonin-induced spontaneous inhibitory post-synaptic currents (slPSCs) in studies of hippocampal pyramidal neurons. 
In both studies, 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonism play a key role. 
Since the majority of 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors are located on GABAergic interneurons, vortioxetine might attenuate the inhibitory control of pyramidal neurons by these interneurons, and thereby increase [[glutamate|glutamatergic]] neurotransmission, LTP & [[neuroplasticity]].

While an acute dose of vortioxetine reduced LTP in non-stressed rats, like other Serotonergic drug, Vortioxetine reduced the LTP reduction induced by an acute [[stress|stressor]]. 

Since 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors are [[Ionotropic Receptors|ionic]], so they are able to strongly excite GABAergic interneurons. 
Thus, the blockade of 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ receptors may strongly disinhibit pyramidal neuron activity. 

A drug induced increase in hippocampal [[neurogenesis]] in the [[dentate gyrus]] in adults has been associated with antidepressant effects. 
In rats, Vortioxetine quickly produced a significant increase in cell proliferation in the dentate gyrus, while other SSRIs required significantly longer to produce the same effect.
Vortioxetine increased [[dendrites|dendritic]] length and the number of dendrite intersections, suggesting that vortioxetine accelerates the maturation of immature neurons.


>[!cognition] Cognitive Function
>^cognition
- Studies have indicated that 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$, 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$, 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$, and [[5-HT7|5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$]] receptors can regulate cognitive functions. 
Vortioxetine's enhancement of cholinergic and histaminergic neurotransmission may contribute to its positive effect on cognitive function, given the important role of these neurotransmitter systems on cognition. 

Furthermore, vortioxetine's receptor activities have the potential to modulate glutamate neurotransmission, affecting cognitive function either directly, or indirectly via GABA interneurons. 

Vortioxetine (but not Escitalopram or [[Duloxetine]]) reversed memory impairments in rats depleted of Serotonin using a [[tryptophan hydroxylase]] inhibitor.
A selective 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ agonist and a 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonist also reversed memory impairments in the Serotonin-depleted rats, indicating that these receptors play a role in the effect of vortioxetine on memory function. 

The enhanced activity of pyramidal neurons in the mPFC evoked by vortioxetine may contribute to the maintenance of persistent neuronal activity required for [[working memory]]


A [[qEEG]] analysis in rats showed that Vortioxetine dose-dependently increased arousal, as did [[flesinoxan]] (a 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ agonist), [[Duloxetine]] & [[ondansetron]] (a selective 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ antagonist), but not [[Escitalopram]] or a 5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$ antagonist.

Furthermore, Vortioxetine, unlike Escitalopram and Duloxetine, increased [[theta waves|theta]], [[alpha waves|alpha]] & [[gamma waves|gamma]] power bands of the [[EEG]]
The combination of Vortioxetine and flesinoxan produced an even more pronounced effect on the power spectrum than vortioxetine alone. 
Furthermore, ondansetron and a 5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$ antagonist increased theta power significantly. 

These data suggest that vortioxetine increase frontal cortical activity through several of its receptor activities. 

>[!target] Target Pharmacology
>^targetPharmacology
- At clinical doses, all of Vortioxetine's targets are typically occupied, primarily 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ and SERT at lower doses and 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$, 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$, & 5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$ at higher doses. 

| Target               | Function        | Affinity $\ce{K_i (\text{nM})}$ |
| :------------------- | :-------------- | :------------------------------ |
| SERT                 | Inhibitor       | 1.6 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$            |
| 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$    | Antagonist      | 3.7 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$            |
| 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ | Agonist         | 15 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$             |
| 5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$    | Antagonist      | 19 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$             |
| 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$ | Partial Agonist | 33 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$             |
| 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1d}}$ | Antagonist      | 54 $\mathsf{K_{i}}$             |

The Potency Rank is 5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$ > SERT > 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1B}}$ > 5-HT$\mathsf{_{1A}}$ $\approx$ 5-HT$\mathsf{_7}$

A study of the [[midbrain ralphe nuclei]], the region of highest SERT concentration found that at steady-state conditions, the mean SERT occupancy was:
- $\approx50$% at 5 mg
- $64\%$ at 10 mg
- $>80$% at 20 mg

Vortioxetine may exert antidepressant effects at SERT occupancies as low as 50%
In contrast, [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor|SSRIs]] & [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitor|SNRIs]] typically require SERT occupancy of at least 80%
This is likely due to benefit from Vortioxetine's action at other receptors. 

### Pharmacokinetics
Vortioxetine has a:
- Bioavailability of 75%
- Mean T$\mathsf{_{max}}$ of 7-8 h
- Mean elimination half-life (T$\mathsf{_{1\text{/}2}}$) of 57 h upon oral administration

Steady-state concentrations will be reached in approximately 12 days (assuming it takes approximately 5 half-lives to reach Steady-State)

Vortioxetine shows linear and time-independent pharmacokinetics at doses of 2.5, 5, 20, 40, 60 & 75 mg.

>[!attention] Metabolism
>^metabolism
- Vortioxetine is extensively metabolized. 
The major metabolite is considered pharmacologically inactive. 
Other metabolites are present in low concentrations in plasma or are not able to penetrate the [[blood-brain barrier]] and are therefore not considered pharmacologically relevant. 

After correction for weight, there were no [[Male-Female Differences]] in plasma levels of Vortioxetine and its major metabolite. 

The P450 enzymes responsible for the metabolism of vortioxetine include:
- [[CYP2D6]]
- [[CYP3A4]]
- [[CYP3A5]]
- [[CYP2C9]]
- [[CYP2C19]]
- [[CYP2A6]]
- [[CYP2C8]]
- [[CYP2B6]]
The most important of these is CYP2D6
Because of this, caution should be taken when co-administered with CYP2D6 inhibitors. [[Bupriopon]], a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor, was found to more than double total and peak Vortioxetine levels. 

Vortioxetine shows no significant inhibition or induction of P450 enzymes. 

Vortioxetine was highly [[protein binding|protein bound]], so it has interactions with other highly protein bound drugs. 

### Side Effects
Likely due to their antagonism of [[5-HT3|5-HT$\mathsf{_3}$]] receptors, their side effect profile tends to be milder than many other serotonergic drugs. 

The most common side effects include:
- nausea
- vomiting
- constipation
- sexual dysfunction

Compared to 14-20% for placebo, vortioxetine doses ranging from 5-20mg had sexual dysfunction rates of 16-34%
Treatment-emergent sexual dysfunction caused by a prior SSRI was better improved by vortioxetine than by switching to [[Escitalopram]]
